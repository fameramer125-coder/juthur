The Theory — Preface to Juthur Reads

مسوّدة v3 · ١٨ أغسطس ٢٠٢٦

Drafting note. This preface is proposed for placement before Part 1 of Juthur Reads (جُذُورٌ تَقْرَأُ; Amer, 2024), ahead of the theoretical foundations. It carries §1, §2, §3, §4, §5, §8 and §9 of al-Mushafaha — The Governing Doctrine of Juthur. It states a doctrine of delivery; it does not amend the six principles, and says so explicitly so that no reader takes it for a revision of the theory.

Before the theory: how it is carried

This book describes how a child comes to read. It does not, by itself, say how that description is to reach the child — and a method that leaves its own delivery unspecified will be delivered by whatever means lies nearest. In the year this preface is written, what lies nearest is a screen.

So before the six principles, one sentence about the hand that carries them.

This is not a programme against screens. It is a programme to return an absent voice. The distinction decides everything that follows. A method defined by what it refuses belongs to the anxiety that produced it and ages with it. A method defined by what it restores outlasts the occasion. The screen is not the illness; it is what arrives when a voice stops — and a screen removed from a silent house has treated nothing. It has opened a gap, and the gap will be filled again within the month.

I came to this from a book about absence rather than about technology. The pandemic did not create the silence it exposed; it removed the scaffolding that had been hiding it, and handed the child a device to fill it. Let one thing be plain, since it is the point at which this argument is most often misheard: the absence is an absence of voice, not of employment. It falls to fathers as often as to mothers, and it is found in houses where every adult is present and no one speaks.

al-Mushafaha (الْمُشَافَهَةُ) is the transmission of language mouth to ear, in the presence of another person. It is the oldest pedagogy in this tradition and the most severely tested: an unbroken chain, verified person by person, that has held a text word for word across fourteen centuries with no screen, no slide, and no lecture hall. It proceeds in three movements — Talqin (التَّلْقِين), the adult says it first; Tardid (التَّرْدِيد), the child returns it until it is held; Inshad (الإِنْشَاد), melody and rhythm carry what plain speech cannot. Receive, return, sing.

It is not an argument against books. It could not be, and this is a book. Print carries; voice transmits. A copy of the Qur'an makes no one a reciter, and that has never been a case against writing it down. What the doctrine refuses is narrower and harder: a medium standing in for a person. One question decides every instance — does this carry the voice to the child, or does it excuse the adult from being present?

A necessary caution to the reader who knows this book's structure. al-Mushafaha is not a seventh principle. Tazamun, Tadarruj, Takhsis, Nafidha, Shumul and Takamul are six, and they remain six. The doctrine sits above the theory rather than inside it: the theory accounts for how reading is acquired, and the doctrine governs only how it is transmitted. Neither amends the other. If it opens the book, that is an order of reading, not an order of rank — the reader who reaches the principles without knowing how they are to be delivered has already been handed the wrong instrument.

From this follows the programme's plainest commitment, and the one that draws the most resistance: Juthur is delivered by voice, produced and heard, with no moving image in the primary years and below. No video, no projected slides. Audio is made, recorded, listened to, and kept.

The case does not rest on alarm. A screen need not harm a child to be a loss; it is enough that it occupies time drawn from talk, listening, movement, play and print — an arithmetical claim, not a clinical one, and not answerable by pointing to a well-made programme. Health guidance already advises against sedentary screen exposure in the earliest years. The most digitised school systems have begun retreating on their own evidence. And a school that takes phones at the door and teaches by slide deck has told the child that a screen harms when he chooses it and helps when we do; children read that contradiction long before they read the policy.

Two things this argument will not claim, because it does not need them and cannot support them. It does not claim that television and tablets cause later dependence on phones — habituation and displacement are defensible, a causal chain is not, and the larger claim would take the smaller one down with it. And it does not delay reading. What precedes print here is the phonological and bodily work that makes print hold; the earlier years are not emptier of literacy but denser in its foundation.

One exception stands without qualification. Assistive technology prescribed for an individual child is exempt. A deaf child needs sight; a non-speaking child may need a device to speak with; a child kept home by illness reaches the room only by a link. There is an old caution that a good is sometimes surrendered because it is the road to a harm. It is sound, and here it runs the other way: to withhold a certain good from one child against a possible harm to others is precisely the trade it warns against. The clause grants no one a screen for teaching, and it protects the doctrine as much as it protects the child.

What remains is the practice. Say it first. Have it said back. Sing what will not otherwise stay. Then open the book — and read on, because the six principles begin overleaf, and they are the substance of what is to be carried.

الْمُشَافَهَةُ

جُذُور لَيْسَ مَشْرُوعًا ضِدَّ الشَّاشَةِ؛ جُذُور مَشْرُوعٌ لِإِعَادَةِ الصَّوْتِ الْغَائِبِ. فَالشَّاشَةُ لَيْسَتِ الدَّاءَ، بَلْ هِيَ الْبَدِيلُ الَّذِي يَحْضُرُ حِينَ يَسْكُتُ صَوْتٌ.

وَالْمُشَافَهَةُ نَقْلُ اللُّغَةِ مِنْ فَمٍ إِلَى أُذُنٍ بِحُضُورِ إِنْسَان: تَلْقِينٌ، ثُمَّ تَرْدِيدٌ، ثُمَّ إِنْشَادٌ. الْكِتَابُ يَحْفَظُ، وَالشَّيْخُ يُقْرِئُ؛ فَلَيْسَتِ الْمُشَافَهَةُ ضِدَّ الْوَرَقِ، وَإِنَّمَا ضِدَّ أَنْ يَحُلَّ الْوَسِيطُ مَحَلَّ الْإِنْسَان.

يُقَدَّمُ هَذَا الْعَمَلُ صَوْتًا — إِنْتَاجًا وَسَمَاعًا — بِلَا صُورَةٍ مُتَحَرِّكَةٍ فِي الْمَرْحَلَةِ الِابْتِدَائِيَّةِ وَمَا قَبْلَهَا. وَتُسْتَثْنَى التِّقْنِيَةُ الْمُسَاعِدَةُ الْمَوْصُوفَةُ لِطِفْلٍ بِعَيْنِهِ، بِلَا قَيْدٍ.

al-Mushafaha

Juthur is not a programme against screens. Juthur is a programme to return an absent voice. The screen is not the disease; it is the substitute that arrives when a voice stops.

al-Mushafaha is the transmission of language mouth to ear, in the presence of another person: Talqin, then Tardid, then Inshad. Print carries and voice transmits — the doctrine is not opposed to books, only to a medium standing in for a person.

This work is delivered by voice, produced and heard, with no moving image in the primary years and below. Assistive technology prescribed for an individual child is exempt without qualification.
