THE KITCHEN WORKSHOP ورشة المطبخ

Miqyas Facilitator Kit · 90 minutes · any school, any facilitator, anywhere.
Run a real rater-calibration workshop with zero prior knowledge of ACTFL or AERO in the room, the metaphors carry the theory, the protocol carries the rigor. Part of the Miqyas project pack; travels with the Miqyas toolkit file, the workshop deck, the blind rating sheets with their sample booklets, the tally calculator, and the trainer and trainee guides.

0 · Who can run this?

Anyone who has attended this workshop once and shadowed a facilitator once (see §7). You do not need to be a certified examiner, a department chair, or the tool's author. You need: the pack's files, a projector, printed rating sheets, and the discipline to stay silent during blind rounds.

1 · The three sentences that ARE the workshop (memorize these; say them first at the lesson, minute 30, never before Round 1)

AERO = the recipe book What we teach each grade, the dishes on the menu. It never says how well anything was cooked.
ACTFL = the tasting scale How well the dish actually turned out, from first taste (Novice) to master chef (Superior).
Miqyas = the shared kitchen Where the cooks taste the same dishes and compare what they tasted. Today we measure ourselves, not students.

And the three rooms of the kitchen, say them whenever a "mode" appears:

Interpersonal = cooking together Two at one stove, adjusting as they go · conversation.
Interpretive = tasting the absent chef's dish You can't ask what's in it · reading, listening, viewing.
Presentational = serving guests Prepared, plated, one-to-many, a speech or essay; polish first, because no one sends it back mid-meal.

2 · Materials checklist (before the day)

  • Send participants the trainee guide and the one-page information sheet for teachers two days early · never the samples or the deck (blind means blind). Ask them not to open the toolkit's Tab 7 or Tab 8 before the day: the workshop samples are there, and for the Arabic set the author's proposed level, for the French and Spanish sets the border each was written for, are folded away one click from view.
  • Bring a second person for the tally. Sorting the sheets and typing the counts while leading a discussion is not a one-person job.
  • Print from miqyas_workshop_rating_sheets.html, per participant: the Round 1 sheet, the Round 2 sheet (they differ on purpose, its facilitator page says why), and the sample booklet of their own language. No names on any sheet: each rater invents a code and writes it on both. Both rounds ask for the major level only.
  • Have each language's samples pre-checked once by a specialist of that language ("could a real learner have produced this?"). The samples that ship with this kit are their author's constructions; the French and Spanish sets have not yet had that check. Do it before you use them, or write your own.
  • Open the Miqyas hub on the projector; rehearse the tally calculator with fake data once, alone, then clear it. It keeps one grid per language and remembers what you type, so a closed tab loses nothing.
  • Separate the seats. A whisper between raters spoils the room's data.
  • Print a few copies of The Kitchen Companion (the Teacher Companion file in this pack), the pocket guide that answers every button, for teachers to take home.

3 · The 90 minutes, station by station

MinutesStationWhat the facilitator does · and the trap to avoid
0–5Open the kitchenRead the independence notice aloud. (The three sentences of §1 wait for the lesson at 30–45: they name levels.) Say: "Today we look at our own ratings, not at students. No names are collected, and nothing from today goes into anyone's appraisal. In a group this small a colleague may still recognise your sheet; you should know that before you start." Trap: do not explain levels now, and do not say what you hope Round 2 will show; either one contaminates the rounds.
5–20Blind tasting · Round 1Distribute sheets. Total silence. Collect. Major level only. If someone asks "what does Intermediate mean?", answer: "Rate with your current understanding, that is exactly what we are measuring." Trap: answering definition questions mid-round.
20–30The revealYour second person enters the counts into the tally calculator while you speak, sample by sample. Stop at the widest spread and invite, without asking anyone to own a rating: "Would someone explain a reason for Intermediate? And a reason for Advanced?" Trap: correcting anyone, or asking who gave which rating. The reveal is for astonishment, not correction.
30–45The tasting lessonNow teach, using the hub: the three sentences of §1, the ladder of major levels (Tab 4), sustained function vs. one shiny sentence (Tab 2), and the FACT lens. Anchor every concept to a disagreement the room just had, but never name a workshop sample's level and never describe one of the six samples as an example of anything. Trap: opening Low/Mid/High sublevels · that is Workshop 2.
↳ 42–45Scene 4.5 · The Template Continuum
the last three minutes of the tasting lesson		Say: "Memorization is not the enemy, it's the first step. The question is: did the learner BUILD on the template, or just repeat it?" Put the hub's Tab 11 → Generative Capacity Check on the projector (the theater in Tab 2 has a button that opens it): Intact Reproduction · Generative Adaptation. Then ask the room about their own classes, not about the six workshop samples (those were not written from frames, so there is nothing to compare them with): "Think of the last task you gave with frames. Did the frames come back as given, or changed?" Let two or three people answer. Count nothing. Trap: letting sound like a verdict on a student, it describes one sample, and it is where every learner starts.
45–55Language traps & the ApexShow the Arabic hypotheses in Tab 3, as hypotheses. French and Spanish are empty there on purpose: invite those teachers to write one trap each on a card from their own experience. Never illustrate a trap with one of today's six samples. Then open Tab 11 · the Methods Triangle for two minutes: AERO and ACTFL are the base every school has; the top corner, the Cognitive-Linguistic Apex, is thinking-in-language, a student below a method's floor still thinks the method, and the scaffolded task lets that thinking be seen. Trap: the facilitator pretending expertise in all three languages, host their expertise instead.
55–70Blind tasting · Round 2Same samples, fresh sheets, same silence, major level only. Trap: rushing, or telling the room that a changed rating is what you are hoping for. Keeping a rating is as sound as changing it.
70–85Feed the dial · liveEnter Round 2 · every U in its own column: missing data, never a low rating. Show what changed and what did not, sample by sample; Krippendorff's α sits beside it as a secondary figure, and the calculator's own caveat about small rooms is read aloud. Export the tally JSON for each language. Then, on the hub's header, feed the parked dial its first real numbers in front of everyone, one language's file at a time (the dial holds one measurement, the last one fed; say which language it is showing), the needle's first true movement is the workshop's photograph. If the distance did not fall, say exactly this: "That is a finding, not a failure, now we know which border resists us." If it did fall, say this: "Closer is not yet the same as right. That is checked later, against reference judgments collected outside this room." Trap: dressing up a weak number, or a strong one. The tool's whole brand is that it never does.
85–90CommitmentsTwo things only, because five minutes hold no more. Each teacher leaves with two dated samples to collect per student before Workshop 2, and the date of Workshop 2 is fixed before anyone stands up. Everything else goes out afterwards in writing (see §3b).

3b · What to send round after the workshop (not in the 90 minutes)

Between workshops, the collected samples feed each student's chart in the toolkit, where the passage flags and the momentum triangle (rising · steady · falling) turn them into a differentiated plan per skill. A held crossing can print a student-facing passage milestone page, only after the teacher ticks the attestation, stamped FORMATIVE MILESTONE ONLY on paper; it is not a certificate and says so. A noisy rating is superseded: ghosted, never erased. The five-minute weekly ritual is one Night a week from the toolkit's Nights Logbook: read the night aloud in the staffroom, everyone judges alone on paper, then open the reveal together. Every night in that logbook is stamped Tier C · اجتهاد, one author's reasoned proposal, not an answer key; it climbs to B · اعتضاد when an independent specialist confirms it and to A · اعتماد only when two raters agree blind. "Nothing here outranks your own judgment until it has earned it."

4 · The three oaths of the kitchen (send them with the written note of §3b; read them aloud at Workshop 2)

1 · No invented numbers. The dial moves only on measured data. In this kitchen a blank is more honorable than a guess.
2 · "Unratable" is a legitimate dish. Not enough evidence means more probes, never automatic demotion, and since toolkit v2.7 it never leaves without its reason: the hub asks why, and the reasons, counted, tell the department what to fix.
3 · We rate the language, we honor the thinking. A student below a method's floor fails the language, not the thought, scaffold the language so the thinking can be seen (hub, Tab 11).

Swap freelyNever change
The sample sets (build your languages' own, specialist-checked) · the local targets (each department sets its own, labeled as its own decision) · the languages themselves · the scale labels if your system runs CEFR/IB (the protocol is scale-independent)Blind rating in silence · two rounds with calibration between · specialist validation before samples are used · no invented numbers, ever · "unratable" as a legitimate answer · no names collected, collective numbers only, and the limits of that anonymity said aloud at the start

6 · Questions you will be asked (and the answers that work)

"Does this certify us as examiners?"– No. Official OPI certification is exclusive to ACTFL's own workshops. This is internal calibration: it makes our judgments agree; it confers no credential.

"Why can't we see the correct answers?"– Because no answer key is used today. The samples carry only their author's proposal, which is not shown. Reference judgments are collected separately, from raters who never see this room's numbers, so that this room agreeing cannot be mistaken for this room being right.

"My student speaks better than this sample but makes more mistakes, how do I rate?"– Welcome to the exact center of the kitchen: sustained function first, then whether errors block understanding. Hold that question for Workshop 2, which is also where sublevels (Low · Mid · High) are opened, on real samples.

7 · Train the next facilitator (how the value spreads)

  1. Attend the workshop once as a rater.
  2. Shadow once: run the tally screen while the facilitator speaks; watch for the traps column of §3.
  3. Own the kit: run §3 yourself with the facilitator silent in the back row. From then on, the kit is yours to run and to adapt under the permission at the foot of this page, and so is training the next person, anywhere in the world.
