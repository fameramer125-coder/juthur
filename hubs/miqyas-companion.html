THE KITCHEN COMPANION رفيق المطبخ

Miqyas, the teacher's pocket guide · every button, what it does, why it exists · print it, fold it, keep it.
Rule of the house: you never need an assistant. If a button isn't on this card, it isn't in the tool.

The page at a glance · five zones, top to bottom

Zone 1 · The dial: dark header: the agreement needle and its four controls.
Zone 2 · The kitchen's labels: framework chips (ACTFL·AERO·CEFR·IB) and the language bar (AR·FR·ES).
Zone 3 · The eleven rooms: the tab bar; each tab is one room of the kitchen. Since v2.5 a small Tabs button beside the header cycles the eleven room names through English → Arabic → French → Spanish, for navigation only; the deep prose stays English until a native-speaker teacher has reviewed the translations.
Zone 4 · Inside the rooms: the working buttons (rate, record, track, bridge).
Zone 5 · The rules of the kitchen قواعد الطبخ: what is saved, where, and what never leaves your device.

1Zone 1 · the dial (the kitchen's thermometer)

Dark header, top of the page. The needle rests off-scale until it is fed a real number.
خُذ اللقمةَ الأولى · THE FIRST TASTE
Press it: the whole tool switches to First-Day Mode, a kitchen-metaphor panel opens (recipe book / tasting scale / shared kitchen), and every tab grows a one-line "For you as a teacher" strip. Press again to switch it off.
Why: so a teacher on day one understands the system before meeting a single technical word, one first taste, two minutes. Changes language only, never data or ratings. Your choice is remembered on this computer. Since v2.5 the panel holds two collapsed sections, Lire en français and Leer en español: full translations of the same five questions and glossary, tagged اجتهاد (Tier C) pending a native-speaker teacher's review.
النبض · THE PULSE (motion dial)
Press it: the interface's motion cycles through three doses · full pulse (cards float, the astrolabe sweeps, flags bob, headings breathe), whisper (the calm professional shimmer), still (no motion at all). Your choice is remembered on this computer.
Why: a living instrument should feel alive, but the reader owns the dose. Print always renders still, and no data or computation ever changes with the motion.
FEED FROM WORKSHOP FILE (JSON)
Press it: a file picker opens. Choose the file exported by the workshop tally calculator. The needle wakes, glides to the Round 2 agreement, and leaves a ghost mark at Round 1 so everyone sees the distance travelled. (JSON is just the file format the tool saves in, like .docx for data. You never type it or read it; you only pick the file and the tool does the rest.)
Why: the dial is the number this instrument exists to produce, how much the tasters agree. It accepts only measured numbers, so feeding it is always an event, never a decoration.
FEED THE DIAL Round 1 % · Round 2 %
Type the two percentages, press it: same as loading the file, by hand, needle to Round 2, ghost at Round 1, and the plate prints the numbers plainly.
Why: for the day you have the workshop numbers on paper and no file. Both routes save the result on this computer so the needle greets you in place tomorrow.
RESET TO PARKED
Press it: the saved measurement is erased and the needle returns to rest, off-scale, labeled "unmeasured."
Why: to start a new season clean, or to undo a wrong entry. Parking is honest zero, not a score of zero.
DEMO SWEEP (FICTIONAL · DISPLAY ONLY)
Press it: a 12-second animation with invented numbers, stamped DEMO · fictional on a hatched plate. It parks itself when done and saves nothing.
Why: so leadership can see what the dial will do on workshop day, without the tool ever pretending a measurement happened. The stamp is the honesty.

2Zone 2 · the kitchen's labels (chips & languages)

The light strip under the independence notice, and the colored bar above the tabs.
ACTFL · AERO · CEFR · IB
Press any chip: a plate opens stating that framework's exact status here. Green = works with this edition as-is (ACTFL is the tasting scale; AERO has its own room, Tabs 10–11). Amber = the protocol transfers but that build doesn't exist yet (CEFR, IB).
Why: no chip on this page is decoration. Each one states plainly how that framework relates to this tool, in words you can repeat to an inspector exactly.
AR · FR · ES
Press a language: the sample bank, comparisons, and traps switch to that language. The little lamp beside each tells its validation status, Arabic author-rated (double-rating pending); French and Spanish drafted, unrated.
Why: one kitchen, three cuisines. The lamp keeps the tool from ever implying a validation that hasn't happened.

3Zone 3 · the eleven rooms (what each tab is for)

The numbered tab bar. One sentence each, the room explains itself once you're inside.
1 FACT Analysis: paste one student sample; count what's observable first, then judge. · 2 Rater Calibration: the workshop room: sustained performance vs. one shiny sentence; since v2.5 it also holds the Workshop Theater: the 90 minutes staged as eleven simple scenes and two interludes, stepped with a button or the arrow keys, so the whole workshop is readable inside the tool while the projector deck stays in the project folder. The dial button in the header reads “Feed from workshop file (JSON)”: it opens the computer file chooser on purpose, you feed it the tally export after a real workshop; before the first workshop there is nothing to load. · 3 Boundary Map: each language's famous traps (where raters slip). · 4 Level Guide: the tasting ladder, plus clickable seams between the rungs: what changes at each border. · 5 Placement Profile: find a student's floor and ceiling. · 6 Sublevel Engine: turn floor+ceiling+comfort into Low/Mid/High. · 7 Parallel Comparison: the same task cooked in Arabic, French, and Spanish, side by side. · 8 Anchor Bank: the reference taste-samples, each labeled with its provenance. · 9 Living Scale: track one real student across the year (chart, flags, recorder, guided rating, momentum triangle). · 10 AERO Crosswalk: the bridge between the recipe book and the tasting scale. · 11 Methods Triangle: thinking methods (describe/compare/analyze…) and the language floor each one stands on.

4Zone 4 · the working buttons, room by room

Room 4 · Level Guide
CLICK A BORDER SEAM (⟡ between the rungs)
Click the dashed seam between two levels: a border card opens, what changes when a student crosses here, what you'll start hearing, the kitchen image, and the evidence that confirms the crossing. Click again to close. Beneath the borders sits the Sentence Ladder: the same rungs read as the biography of the sentence, kernel, strung, woven, society, with the guard that structure is evidence, never the verdict; since v2.5 its tradition note speaks all three grammars, Ibn Hishām for Arabic, the phrase simple/composée/complexe for French, the oración simple/compuesta for Spanish, with each language’s late fingerprint (maṣdar · avant de partir · al llegar).
Why: instruction lives between levels, not at them. The rungs describe stations; the seams describe the journey, and when a real student makes that journey, the chart in Room 9 plants the flag.
Room 1 · FACT Analysis
DESCRIPTIVE COUNT (NO RATING)
Press it: the tool counts what is countable in the pasted sample, length, sentence shapes, connectors, and refuses to attach a level.
Why: description before judgment is the first discipline of tasting. Machines count; humans rate.
GENERATE REPORT
Press it: your step-by-step answers become one structured FACT report (Function · Accuracy · Context · Text-type) you can copy into your notes.
Why: so your rating carries its reasoning with it, the report is your evidence trail, written by you.
Rooms 5 & 6 · Placement & Sublevel
RUN PLACEMENT MATCH SUBLEVEL DEFINITION
Choose from the dropdowns, press: Room 5 turns "which tasks succeed / which collapse" into a floor-and-ceiling profile. Room 6 turns floor + probe + comfort into Low / Mid / High, or answers "unratable · gather more evidence," which is a real answer, not an error.
Why: this encodes the examiner's logic (level = the rung you sustain, not the rung you touch once). The engine proposes; you decide.
Room 7 · Parallel Comparison
CHOOSE TASK ▾
Pick a task: the same prompt appears as performed in all three languages at the same level, side by side.
Why: to see with your own eyes that the ladder is about function, not about which language, and where languages genuinely differ (script, gender, spacing), the notes say so.
Room 8 · Anchor Bank
THE NIGHTS LOGBOOK (open a night, judge, then reveal)
The anchor sets above them show each specimen's text only; the author's proposed level, border and markers are folded under "Rate it yourself first", so nobody meets a proposal before forming a judgment. Below the anchor sets: complete training scenarios, a student context, a performance, a decision to make, and a folded reveal naming the trap and the reasoned call. Twenty-seven nights, nine per language, mapping the whole expected-situations space (the no-surprises doctrine): performance shapes, rater lenses, evidence integrity, learner profiles, and the genuine crossing. Discipline: judge first, then open the reveal.
Why: a sample is read; a night is lived. Traps you have fallen into once in private, you catch forever in class, and every real workshop dispersion becomes a new night about a rating pattern, never about a student. One night a week in the staffroom is calibration that never stops.
EXPORT BANK JSON
Press it: the whole sample bank downloads as one file, for backup, or to hand your department's bank to another school.
Why: anchors are the kitchen's taste-memory. Every sample carries its provenance label ("author-proposed, pending blind double-rating"), and the label travels with the export.
Room 9 · Living Scale · the busiest room
ADD POINT student · date · mode · level · AERO context
Fill the small form, press it: one dated dot lands on the student's chart, speaking as a solid line, writing as a dashed line. One student per chart. And when a new point stands across a border from the last one, the chart plants a flag on its own: ◆ hollow until a later rating holds it, ▽ gray when a rating came in lower, and the passage log beneath the chart writes the line: مِنْ هُنَا كَانَ العُبُور.
Why: a level is a photograph; the chart is the film, and the flags are the milestones, so you can point at the trail and say "yes, they passed through here." Flags mark recorded ratings, never certified proficiency, and the tool never shows percent-progress between borders.
GUIDED RATING · HUMAN JUDGMENT, SCAFFOLDED
Press it: a four-step wizard walks you to a level, sustained function → probe behavior → comfort → confirm. Confirm fills the level box for you and attaches your reasoning to the point. Cancel leaves everything untouched.
Why: so a teacher who has never rated alone is never alone. The wizard asks; only you answer; nothing is decided without your confirmation.
● REC DOWNLOAD RECORDING
Press REC: the browser asks permission, then records audio from the microphone; press again to stop. Download saves the file to this computer.
Why: rate from evidence, not memory. The recording lives and dies on your device, it is never uploaded anywhere. Follow school policy on student recordings.
SUPERSEDE / RESTORE (in the data table)
Open the data table under the chart, press Supersede on a rating: the rating is annulled after re-assessment, it stays in the record, turns ghost on the chart, and no line, crossing, or alert is computed from it. Press Restore and it returns whole. Fully reversible, always visible.
Why: annulment is a rating skill in its own right, whoever holds the tools of annulment holds the tools of certification. So both acts are logged, ghosted rather than erased, and undoable: the kitchen corrects a tasting; it never pretends the tasting didn't happen.
TASK VERSION ▾ + GENERATIVE CAPACITY CHECK (in the Add-a-sample form), new in v2.7
The Task version box must be answered for every sample: scaffolded or independent. A point will not save while it is blank. Choose "Scaffolded · template provided": a brass panel opens with two choices · Intact Reproduction (the template came back exactly as given) or Generative Adaptation (the learner changed, combined, or built on it). Pick one, then Add point; the symbol shows in the data table and travels in the export as generative_classification.
Why: a memorized frame and a created sentence can sound identical. One honest tick keeps them apart in the record, and describes one sample, never a student.
UNRATABLE → DIAGNOSTIC REASON (in the guided rating and in the Rating list), new in v2.7
Choose "Unratable" at the level step, or pick "U, Unratable" in the Rating list and press Add point: a window opens and asks why, task above the language floor · task elicited only the supplied frames · not enough evidence · evidence integrity in doubt · other (you write it). It has no ✕ and ignores Escape: pick a reason and Confirm, or Cancel to take back the Unratable choice. No dot is added to the chart, an unratable sample has no rung, and the reason is listed beside the chart and saved in the export as unratable_reason, with the task version and thinking method chosen at that moment.
Why: "I can't rate this yet" is a legitimate answer, and the reason is the useful part. Ten unratables for "task above the floor" are a message about the tasks, not the students.
PRINT ITS MILESTONE PAGE (in the passage log)
Press it beside a held crossing: a one-page passage milestone page opens in the print dialog, the student's name, the border crossed, the dates, the ladder with the new rung marked, and signature lines. مِنْ هُنَا كَانَ العُبُور across the top. Since v2.7 a Teacher Attestation comes first: tick the box confirming this is an internal classroom milestone, not an official ACTFL® certification and not valid for placement decisions, the Print button stays grey until you do. On paper, a faint red stamp crosses the page: FORMATIVE MILESTONE ONLY.
Why: the first organ that serves the student's hands, not the rater's. It appears only for crossings held by later evidence, never provisional ones, and its own fine print says what it is not: an internal classroom milestone, not an official ACTFL® rating or credential.
THE MOMENTUM TRIANGLE (no button · it reads itself)
Below the chart, always on: each skill sits on one of three vertices · Rising ▲ · Steady ▬ · Falling ▽: computed from the last two active ratings. Steady is tiered by months on the rung and by altitude, in three bands: lowlands NL–IL 4/8/12 · midlands IM–IH 6/10/15 (the catalog's own "two years at IM") · highlands AL+ 7/12/18, and the tiers time the alarm, not the care. With five or more ratings whose last five legs strictly alternate, a ◌? token orbits the triangle's center: not a fourth state, and always a hypothesis: adjacent (one rung, e.g. IM–IH) = possible border band, verify first (task-demand check · blind re-rate · one probe), and only then push the ceiling with scaffolds; wide (2+ rungs) = check the scale and the day before either the rater or the student is blamed.
Why: two students at the same rung are not the same student. The trichotomy holds per moment (up, down, or same, no fourth); oscillation is a path pattern above it, so it orbits between the vertices instead of sitting on one. The level names where they stand; the momentum names what to teach tomorrow. Differentiation, made operational.
LOAD DEMO DATA (FICTIONAL) EXPORT JSON CLEAR CHART
Demo draws an invented student, clearly labeled, so you can practice reading the chart. Export saves the real chart as a file. Clear empties the chart, and warns you first if unsaved work would be lost.
Why: practice safely, back up honestly, and never lose a term's tracking to one misclick.
Room 10 · The Bridge
CLICK ANY GRADE PLATE SET OUR TARGET ▾
Click a plate (grade × mode): its detail card opens, what AERO asks at that grade (with the page number), the range on the ladder where that task is performable, and where it feels comfortable. Set our target pins a ◇ diamond on the ladder for your department.
Why: the bridge connects what-we-teach to how-well-it's-going by function: it is expressly not a grade-equals-score table (that equation is prohibited, and the tool refuses it). The ◇ is labeled a department decision, because it is one.
Room 11 · Methods Triangle
CLICK A METHOD CHIP
Click describe / historical / compare / analyze / research: its card opens, where AERO demands it (verbatim, with page), the language floor it stands on, stated per mode: a floor is a vector, not one number, and a three-rung task ladder, each rung with its line in the student's voice ("I can …", author-written in the Can-Do tradition): scaffolded below the floor · independent at it · stretch above it.
Why: methods are cooking techniques, assigned before their floor without scaffolds, they fail the dish, not the cook. The card hands you the scaffolded version so the thinking is graded, not disguised language, and the house rules beneath the triangle bind the floors to tasks, never to students: every scaffold is a dated bridge that fades, and any student may attempt the independent version.
SCAFFOLDED TASK | INDEPENDENT TASK (switch at the top of each method card), new in v2.7
Scaffolded (the default) opens two panels under the task ladder: the Template Bank: the frames you hand the learner, in Arabic, French and Spanish, each one an editable box; + Add Template adds yours, × removes one, Export Templates (JSON) saves your department's bank, Restore starter frames brings back the originals · and the Generative Capacity Check (pick one answer, then Save this check). Independent closes both: with no template handed out there is nothing to reproduce. The switch remembers your choice per method.
Why: the starter frames are one author's proposal (Tier C), your department's own frames belong there. And the check is the instrument's answer to its hardest border: did the learner repeat the frame, or build on it?

5Zone 5 · the rules of the kitchen (data & safety)

Everything stays in this kitchen. The tool is one file. It works without internet (the only thing it fetches when online is its web fonts; offline it uses the computer's own). What you save lives in this browser on this computer: the dial numbers, the first-day preference, your ◇ targets, your template bank, the task switch, and two nameless logs (Generative Checks and Unratable reasons, kept as dates and categories, never a student's name). Nothing is sent, synced, or uploaded, ever. Chart points are the exception: they live only while the page stays open, so export the chart before you close the tab. Recordings save only where you put them. Clearing the browser's site data erases saved settings, and closing the tab erases the chart, so Export JSON is your backup habit. And the one rule above all: لا يتحرّك المؤشّر قبل أن يوجد رقمٌ حقيقيّ: no number in this tool is ever invented.

The Kitchen Dictionary · every strange word, each with its picture مسرد المطبخ

Twenty-eight words. Meet one in the tool, find it here, and its kitchen picture will hold the meaning for you.

Tier A · B · C (the honesty grades)One cook’s word · a second taster agreed the dish is real · two blind judges agreed on the score.Follow one sample, a short student letter, through its life. Monday: the author writes it and proposes “IM.” Nobody else has seen it. That is Tier C: اجتهاد (ijtihād, one mind's reasoned effort), realistic and reasoned, but never an answer key. Wednesday: an independent specialist of the language reads it: “these errors are real, our students make exactly these.” Now it is Tier B: اعتضاد (i'tiḍād, propped up by a second independent witness). Double-rating day: two raters rate it separately on the double-rating form, neither seeing the other’s sheet, both say IM. Now it is Tier A: اعتماد (i'timād, formally adopted, the word used for accreditation), and only now may the sample serve as an anchor for the department's own use. (A reference judgment for research is a further step: it comes from certified raters.) All three words share one Arabic verbal pattern (افتعال), so the climb has one sound: اجتهاد ← اعتضاد ← اعتماد. In one line: C = one person said it · B = an independent specialist confirmed it · A = two people agreed without seeing each other. C is not a defect; it is a birth certificate, every workshop lifts material from C toward A. Why declare it at all? A tool that forbids you to trust your impression of a student cannot ask you to trust its author’s impression of himself, the tool’s own self-rating is Tier C too. Since v2.5 the grades are drawn as the Trust Triangle in Room 8 (beside the Nights Logbook), third of the family, after the Methods Triangle and the momentum triangle: wide base C, narrow apex A, and every workshop pushes material upward.
The ladder (levels)How cooked is the dish · from first taste to master chef.What a student can do in unrehearsed language. Not a grade, not an exam score.
Sublevel (Low/Mid/High)Just plated · resting · ready to serve.Where you stand inside a rung: arrived, solid, or knocking on the next.
SustainedOne good bite doesn't make a banquet.A level is what holds across the whole performance, never one shiny sentence.
Floor & ceilingThe dish they can always cook · the dish that still burns.The level sustained everywhere vs. the level that collapses under probing.
AnchorThe taste-sample kept on the shelf.A performance the team agreed shows a level, kept as the reference.
Blind ratingTasting with the label covered.Judging without seeing anyone else's answer, so agreement is real.
CalibrationCooks tasting the same spoon until "needs salt" means the same thing.Practicing on shared samples so that ratings rest on the same criteria. Agreeing more is not yet being right: that is checked against reference judgments from outside the room.
κ (kappa)Agreement with the lucky guesses skimmed off the top.How much two fixed raters agree beyond chance. The dial shows it only when a file of their rating pairs is loaded; after a workshop the dial's number is exact agreement, in whole percent.
The three rooms (modes)Cooking together · tasting the absent chef's dish · serving guests.Interpersonal, Interpretive, Presentational · every task lives in one.
Unratable"I need another taste before I judge this."A legitimate professional answer. More evidence, never automatic demotion.
Diagnostic Unratable (عدم القابلية للتقييم التشخيصي)The dish sent back with a note, "undercooked", "not enough on the plate", "someone else's cooking", never just sent back.Since v2.7 an Unratable always carries its reason; the reasons, counted, tell the kitchen what to fix.
Generative Check (فحص القدرة التوليدية)Did the cook follow the recipe card to the letter, or change one ingredient on purpose?The teacher's one-tick record, for a scaffolded task, of whether the template was reproduced intact or adapted. Following the card is the first step, not a fault.
Template Bank (بنك القوالب)The recipe cards pinned above the beginner's station.The sentence frames a scaffolded task provides, per method and language, editable, exportable, yours.
Tier C Status Bar (شريط حالة الاجتهاد)The sign on the taste-library door: "every jar here is one cook's word, so far."The yellow bar at the top of Room 8: how many nights have been validated (today 0), and the reminder that none of them is an answer key. It turns orange, then green, only as workshops lift material to B and A.
The BridgeThe walkway between the recipe book and the tasting scale.AERO tasks hung on the ladder by function, a range, never a grade-for-score swap.
The Cognitive-Linguistic Apex (رأس المثلث)The recipe book and the tasting scale are the counter every kitchen has; the missing corner is the cook's own thinking, done in the new language.The top vertex of the Methods Triangle (Room 11): thinking-in-language, when and how a way of thinking becomes honest in the target language. AERO says what, ACTFL says how well, the apex says whether the task fits the rung.
Scaffolded Template Continuum (مُتَّصَل القوالب)First the cook follows the card, then swaps an ingredient, then writes a card of their own.A working hypothesis, not a finding: at Novice the frame comes back intact; at Intermediate it is manipulated; at Advanced and Superior it is taken apart and rebuilt. The Generative Check records the first crossing on that road.
Method floorDon't demand flambé from a first-week cook, hand them the technique with support.The proficiency a thinking method stands on; below it, scaffold the language so the thinking shows.
Passage flag (مِنْ هُنَا كَانَ العُبُور)The night the new dish first survives a full service, the kitchen marks the date on the wall.A marker the growth chart plants when two ratings stand on opposite sides of a border: hollow until a later rating holds it, gray when recorded lower. Attested by evidence, never by hope.
The three states (مثلث الحال)Tonight's service against the last: better, the same, or off, a dish is never anything else.Rising, steady, or falling, the only three states a student can hold at a level, each with its own teaching move. The level says where; the momentum says what next.
FACTThe mise en place: four trays laid out before anyone tastes, Function · Accuracy · Context · Text type.How any performance, in any language, is broken down before a level is named. Description first, judgment second.
The seams (المعابر)The pass between two stations · where a cook moves up the line.The clickable borders between levels (Tab 4): what changes, what you start hearing, and what evidence confirms a crossing.
Supersede (الإلغاء)A dish sent back after re-tasting: struck from tonight's menu, still in the night's ledger.Annulling a rating after re-assessment, ghosted on the chart, excluded from every computation, never erased, always reversible.
Author-proposedA recipe awaiting the second chef's signature before it enters the house cookbook.A rating, range, or floor proposed by one rater and honestly labeled as such, pending blind double-rating.
Passage milestone pageThe first good review, pinned up in the kitchen.A printable page for a crossing that was held by later evidence, an internal classroom milestone, never an official ACTFL rating.
DemoThe cardboard dish in the display window: looks real, feeds no one, always labeled.Fictional numbers that show how the tool moves. Stamped on every tooltip, saved nowhere, parked when done.
AFL vs. heritageOne cook grew up in this kitchen; the other walked in last week, same recipe, very different day one.Learning the language from zero vs. growing up with it at home: same task, different rungs expected, and that is fair, not unfair.
