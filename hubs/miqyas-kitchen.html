🇫🇷 Lire en français — la version complète 🟡 Tier C — اجتهاد — Pending native review

Traduction : proposition de l'auteur (assistée par IA), en attente de relecture par un enseignant de français natif — même règle de confiance que le reste de l'outil : اجتهاد jusqu'à confirmation.

AERO = le livre de recettes. Ce que nous enseignons à chaque niveau — tâches et sujets. Il dit QUOI cuisiner, jamais à quel point c'est réussi.
ACTFL = l'échelle de dégustation. À quel point le plat est réellement réussi — du premier goût (Novice) au grand chef (Superior). Elle mesure À QUEL POINT C'EST RÉUSSI, jamais ce qui est au menu.
Miqyas = la cuisine partagée. Où nous, les cuisiniers, goûtons les mêmes plats ensemble et nous entraînons jusqu'à ce que nos jugements s'accordent. Elle nous mesure NOUS, les évaluateurs — avant de mesurer les élèves.

Une seule scène traverse tout cet instrument : une cuisine d'évaluateurs qui apprennent à s'accorder, observant le sentier de montagne de chaque élève — ses frontières, ses cols, ses drapeaux — à travers le laiton d'un observatoire ; et l'instrument lui-même est vivant : il respire, il pulse, ses phrases naissent et grandissent.

Les trois « modes » que vous rencontrerez sans cesse — la même cuisine, trois pièces différentes :
Interpersonnel = cuisiner ensemble. Deux personnes devant le même fourneau, goûtant et ajustant en direct — « plus de sel ? » « moins de piment ! » Un dialogue : chacun peut demander, corriger, recommencer sur-le-champ.
Interprétatif = goûter un plat laissé par le chef. Le cuisinier est parti — impossible de lui demander ce qu'il y a dedans. Lire un texte, regarder un film, écouter un exposé : on déchiffre les saveurs seul. D'où son statut de compétence à part entière.
Présentationnel = servir son plat à des invités. Préparé en cuisine, dressé, apporté à plusieurs personnes à la fois. Les invités ne peuvent pas le renvoyer en cours de repas — on le peaufine donc avant de servir. La répétition y est permise, ce qui explique pourquoi il en dit moins sur l'aisance spontanée.

Le premier jour, ne regardez qu'une seule chose : le barreau de l'élève sur l'échelle. Tout le reste — drapeaux, orbite, bandes, échelle de la phrase — n'est qu'un commentaire sur ce barreau, et peut attendre.

1 · Qu'est-ce qu'ACTFL, en une phrase ?
Une échelle de ce qu'un élève SAIT FAIRE avec la langue — parler, comprendre, écrire — en situation réelle et non répétée. Ce n'est ni une note ni un score d'examen : un élève peut avoir un A à ses devoirs et se trouver malgré tout sur un barreau bas de l'échelle. Les deux peuvent être vrais à la fois, et ni l'un ni l'autre n'est scandaleux.

2 · Qu'est-ce qu'AERO, en une phrase ?
La liste de ce que nous enseignons et pratiquons à chaque niveau — la carte des tâches adoptée par notre école. AERO dit QUOI ; ACTFL dit À QUEL POINT C'EST RÉUSSI. Les onglets 10 et 11 montrent comment les deux s'articulent — mais vous n'en avez pas besoin votre première semaine.

3 · Que me demande-t-on exactement ? (trois verbes, rien de plus)
Enseigner les repères de votre niveau — ils sont dans votre plan de cours. Écouter honnêtement — quand vous évaluez ce qu'un élève a réellement dit ou écrit, jugez ce que vous avez observé, non ce que vous espériez. Calibrer — venir à l'atelier et comparer vos évaluations avec celles de vos collègues. C'est tout le travail. Rien ici n'est un test sur vous.

4 · Comment savoir si mon évaluation est juste ?
Seul, vous ne pouvez pas — personne ne le peut, et c'est normal. C'est précisément pour cela que cette cuisine existe : nous évaluons les mêmes échantillons séparément, puis nous comparons. Quand votre réponse diffère de celle d'un collègue, cette différence n'est pas une erreur — c'est exactement l'information que l'atelier est conçu pour recueillir.

5 · Et si je n'ai aucune idée du niveau d'un élève ?
Dites : « Je ne peux pas encore évaluer cela. » Dans cet outil, c'est une réponse professionnelle légitime (le moteur a même un nom pour cela : non évaluable) — jamais un échec. Demandez un autre échantillon, un autre sujet, un autre jour. Deviner vers le bas « par sécurité » est la seule chose que nous ne faisons jamais.

+ · Mots que vous rencontrerez
Sous-niveau — faible/moyen/élevé à l'intérieur de chacun des trois premiers barreaux (Novice · Intermédiaire · Avancé).
Ancre (échantillon de référence) — une performance exemple dont nous avons convenu qu'elle montre un niveau, conservée comme échantillon de dégustation de référence.
Évaluation à l'aveugle — évaluer sans voir la réponse de quiconque au préalable, pour une comparaison honnête.
Calibrage (étalonnage) — s'entraîner ensemble jusqu'à ce que nos évaluations s'accordent mieux qu'avant.
κ (kappa) — le nombre d'accord corrigé du hasard, affiché sur le cadran quand des paires d'évaluations complètes sont chargées ; le chiffre principal reste l'accord exact.
Palier de confiance (A · B · C) — jusqu'où faire confiance à un échantillon, une nuit ou une évaluation proposée : اجتهاد (C, proposition raisonnée d'une seule personne) → اعتضاد (B, confirmée par un spécialiste indépendant) → اعتماد (A, deux évaluateurs à l'aveugle sont d'accord). Si c'est marqué C, c'est un point de départ, jamais une réponse toute faite.
Locuteur patrimonial — apprendre la langue à partir de zéro contre grandir avec elle à la maison : même tâche en classe, barreaux différents sur l'échelle — et c'est attendu, non injuste.
Non évaluable — pas assez de preuves pour juger encore. Une réponse toujours légitime.
Marqueur de passage — le repère que le graphique de croissance plante quand deux évaluations se trouvent de part et d'autre d'une frontière. Vide tant qu'une évaluation ultérieure ne le confirme pas.

🇪🇸 Leer en español — la versión completa 🟡 Tier C — اجتهاد — Pending native review

Traducción: propuesta del autor (con asistencia de IA), pendiente de revisión por un docente de español nativo — misma regla de confianza que el resto de la herramienta: اجتهاد hasta que se confirme.

AERO = el recetario. Lo que enseñamos en cada grado — las tareas y los temas. Dice QUÉ cocinar, nunca qué tan bien salió.
ACTFL = la escala de cata. Qué tan bien está realmente cocinado el plato — desde el primer bocado (Principiante) hasta el gran chef (Superior). Mide QUÉ TAN BIEN, nunca qué hay en el menú.
Miqyas = la cocina compartida. Donde los cocineros probamos los mismos platos juntos y practicamos hasta que nuestra cata coincide. Nos mide A NOSOTROS, los evaluadores — antes de medir a los estudiantes.

Una sola escena recorre todo este instrumento: una cocina de catadores aprendiendo a coincidir, observando el sendero de montaña de cada estudiante — sus fronteras, sus pasos, sus banderas — a través del latón de un observatorio; y el instrumento mismo está vivo: respira, late, sus oraciones nacen y crecen.

Los tres "modos" que seguirás encontrando — la misma cocina, tres salas distintas:
Interpersonal = cocinar juntos. Dos personas ante la misma estufa, probando y ajustando sobre la marcha — "¿más sal?" "¡menos picante!" Una conversación: cada quien puede preguntar, corregir, reintentar en tiempo real.
Interpretativo = probar un plato que dejó el chef. El cocinero ya se fue — no puedes preguntarle qué lleva. Leer un texto, ver una película, escuchar una charla: descifras los sabores tú solo. Por eso es una destreza aparte.
Presentacional = servir tu plato a los invitados. Preparado en la cocina, emplatado, llevado a muchas personas a la vez. Los invitados no pueden devolverlo a media comida — así que lo pules antes de servir. Aquí se permite el ensayo, y por eso mismo dice menos sobre la destreza espontánea.

El primer día, mira solo una cosa: el peldaño del estudiante en la escalera. Todo lo demás — banderas, órbita, bandas, escalera de la oración — es comentario sobre ese peldaño, y puede esperar.

1 · ¿Qué es ACTFL, en una frase?
Una escalera de lo que un estudiante PUEDE HACER con el idioma — hablar, comprender, escribir — en situaciones reales y no ensayadas. No es una nota ni una calificación de examen: un estudiante puede sacar A en la tarea y aun así estar en un peldaño bajo de la escalera. Ambos hechos pueden ser ciertos a la vez, y ninguno es escandaloso.

2 · ¿Qué es AERO, en una frase?
La lista de lo que enseñamos y practicamos en cada grado — el mapa de tareas adoptado por nuestra escuela. AERO dice QUÉ; ACTFL dice QUÉ TAN BIEN. Las pestañas 10 y 11 muestran cómo encajan ambos — pero no las necesitas tu primera semana.

3 · ¿Qué se espera exactamente de mí? (tres verbos, nada más)
Enseñar los estándares de tu grado — están en tu mapa curricular. Escuchar con honestidad — al evaluar lo que un estudiante realmente dijo o escribió, juzga lo que observaste, no lo que esperabas. Calibrar — venir al taller y comparar tus evaluaciones con las de tus colegas. Eso es todo el trabajo. Nada aquí te evalúa a ti.

4 · ¿Cómo sé que mi evaluación es correcta?
Solo, no puedes — nadie puede, y eso es normal. Por eso existe esta cocina: evaluamos las mismas muestras por separado y luego comparamos. Cuando tu respuesta difiere de la de un colega, esa diferencia no es un error — es exactamente la información que el taller está diseñado para recoger.

5 · ¿Y si no tengo idea de qué nivel tiene un estudiante?
Di: "todavía no puedo calificar esto." En esta herramienta es una respuesta profesional legítima (el motor incluso tiene un nombre para ello: no calificable) — nunca un fracaso. Pide otra muestra, otro tema, otro día. Adivinar hacia abajo "por seguridad" es lo único que nunca hacemos.

+ · Palabras que encontrarás
Subnivel — bajo/medio/alto dentro de cada uno de los tres primeros peldaños (Principiante · Intermedio · Avanzado).
Ancla (muestra de referencia) — una actuación ejemplo que acordamos que muestra un nivel, guardada como muestra de cata de referencia.
Evaluación ciega — evaluar sin ver antes la respuesta de nadie más, para una comparación honesta.
Calibración — practicar juntos hasta que nuestras evaluaciones coincidan más que antes.
κ (kappa) — el número de acuerdo corregido por azar, que aparece en el dial cuando se cargan pares completos de evaluaciones; la cifra principal sigue siendo el acuerdo exacto.
Nivel de confianza (A · B · C) — hasta qué punto se puede confiar en una muestra, una noche o una calificación sugerida: اجتهاد (C, propuesta razonada de una sola persona) → اعتضاد (B, confirmada por un especialista independiente) → اعتماد (A, dos evaluadores ciegos coinciden). Si dice C, es un punto de partida, nunca una respuesta definitiva.
Hablante de herencia — aprender el idioma desde cero frente a crecer con él en casa: misma tarea en clase, distintos peldaños en la escalera — y eso es esperado, no injusto.
No calificable — no hay evidencia suficiente para juzgar todavía. Una respuesta siempre legítima.
Marca de pasaje — la señal que planta la gráfica de crecimiento cuando dos evaluaciones quedan a lados opuestos de una frontera. Vacía hasta que una evaluación posterior la confirme.

AERO = the recipe book What dishes we teach each grade — the tasks and topics. It says WHAT to cook, never how well it turned out.
ACTFL = the tasting scale How well the dish is actually cooked — from first taste (Novice) to master chef (Superior). It measures HOW WELL, never what's on the menu.
Miqyas = the shared kitchen Where we cooks taste the same dishes together and practice until our tasting agrees. It measures US, the raters — before we measure students.

One scene runs through this whole instrument — مشهدٌ واحد يجري في الأداة كلها: a kitchen of tasters learning to agree, watching each learner's mountain trail — its borders, passes, and flags — through the brass of an observatory; and the instrument itself is alive: it breathes, it pulses, its sentences are born and grow. Four voices, one story: the kitchen speaks of the judges, the trail of the student, the brass of the measurement, the breath of the tool.

And the three "modes" you'll keep meeting? Same kitchen — three different rooms:

Interpersonal = cooking together Two people at one stove, tasting and adjusting as they go — "more salt?" "less heat!" A conversation: each side can ask, fix, retry in real time.
Interpretive = tasting a dish the chef left behind The cook is gone — you can't ask what's in it. Reading a text, watching a film, hearing a lecture: you work out the flavors alone. That's why it's its own skill.
Presentational = serving your dish to guests Prepared in the back, plated, brought out to many people at once. A speech, an essay, a report: the guests can't send it back mid-meal — so you polish it before serving. Rehearsal is allowed here, which is exactly why it tells us less about spontaneous skill.

First day, look at one thing only: the student's rung on the ladder. Everything else in this instrument — the flags, the orbit, the bands, the sentence ladder — is commentary on that rung, and it can all wait until the rung feels familiar.

1 · What is ACTFL, in one sentence?

A ladder of what a student can do with the language — speak, understand, write — in real, unrehearsed situations. It is not a grade and not an exam score: a student can earn an A on homework and still stand on a low rung of the ladder. Both facts can be true at once, and neither is a scandal.

2 · What is AERO, in one sentence?

The list of what we teach and practice at each grade — our school's adopted map of tasks. AERO says what; ACTFL says how well. Tabs 10 and 11 show how the two fit together (the bridge, and the thinking methods) — but you don't need them in your first week.

3 · What exactly is required of me? (three verbs, that's all)

Teach your grade's benchmarks — they're in your curriculum map. Listen honestly — when you rate what a student actually said or wrote, judge what you observed, not what you hoped (Tabs 1 and 6 walk you through it step by step). Calibrate — come to the workshop and compare your ratings with your colleagues'. That is the entire job. Nothing here is a test of you.

4 · How do I know my rating is right?

Alone, you can't — nobody can, and that's normal. That is the whole reason this kitchen exists: we rate the same samples separately, then compare. When your answer differs from a colleague's, that difference isn't a mistake — it's exactly the information the workshop is built to collect.

5 · What if I have no idea what level a student is?

Say "I can't rate this yet." In this tool that is a legitimate professional answer (the engine even has a name for it: unratable) — never a failure. Ask for another sample, another topic, another day. Guessing downward "to be safe" is the one thing we never do.

+ · Words you'll meet (tap when you meet them)

Sublevel — Low/Mid/High inside each of the first three rungs (Novice · Intermediate · Advanced): just arrived / solidly there / knocking on the next rung.
Anchor — an example performance we agreed shows a level, kept as a reference "taste sample."
Blind rating — rating without seeing anyone else's answer first, so the comparison is honest.
Calibration — practicing together until our ratings agree more than they did before.
κ (kappa) — the chance-corrected agreement number for two fixed raters, shown on the dial only when a file of their rating pairs is loaded. The workshop tally does not produce one: after a workshop the dial shows exact agreement.
Tier (A · B · C) — how far a sample, night, or suggested rating can be trusted: اجتهاد (C, one person's reasoned proposal) → اعتضاد (B, an independent specialist confirmed it) → اعتماد (A, two blind raters agreed). Drawn as the Trust Triangle in Room 8. If it says Tier C, it is a starting point, never an answer key.
Interpersonal / Interpretive / Presentational — the three kitchen rooms above: cooking together / tasting the absent chef's dish / serving guests. Every AERO benchmark and every sample you rate lives in one of the three.
Unratable — not enough evidence to judge yet. A legitimate answer, always.
AFL vs. heritage — learning Arabic from zero vs. growing up with it at home: same task in class, different rungs on the ladder — and that's expected, not unfair.
Passage flag — the marker the growth chart plants when two ratings stand on opposite sides of a border: مِنْ هُنَا كَانَ العُبُور. Hollow until a later rating holds it — attested by evidence, never by hope.

1Text Analysis — the FACT lens

the mise en place — count and arrange before you taste
For you as a teacher: paste what a student actually said or wrote, then tick what you observed with your own eyes and ears. The tool only counts words — you are the judge, and this tab trains your eye for the four things worth judging.

FACT breaks any performance, in any language, into four dimensions that jointly determine a proficiency level: Function, Accuracy, Context, Text type. Language-neutral by design — this is the layer that stays identical whether you're calibrating Arabic, French, or Spanish raters.

F — Function

What can the learner DO with language? Isolated words, created sentences, connected paragraphs, extended argued discourse?

A — Accuracy

Do errors block comprehension? Is there a pattern of errors in basic structures?

C — Context / Content

Familiar personal topics only — or general, abstract, specialized domains?

T — Text type

Word lists → discrete sentences → paragraphs → connected multi-paragraph texts → rhetorically structured discourse.

Calibration protocol — four steps

1
Listen to / read the whole sample before forming any judgment.
2
Identify the highest sustained level — not the best single sentence produced.
3
Verify a stable floor across the entire sample before promoting.
4
Compare your judgment against reference anchors and log your deviation.

Woven sentences (complex)One clause living inside another — because, which, when. Heard in single memorized chunks far earlier; the rung is when weaving becomes chosen, varied, and sustained under pressure.IH, reaching for AL
The sentence societySentences holding jobs — scene-setter, event-chain, complication, wrap-up: the oral paragraph. And a quieter change inside them: weaves fold into dense phrases — أريد أن أذهب becomes أريد الذهاب — the clause packs itself into the noun. Late growth lives in the phrase.Advanced

Three traditions, one weave. Every grammar the department teaches mapped a ladder of this kind long ago — each describes simple, compound, and complex sentences in its own terms. Arabic: Ibn Hishām catalogued الجُمَل التي لها محلٌّ من الإعراب — sentences seated inside a larger sentence in a single word's slot — and the famous pair الكُبرى والصُّغرى names the sentence whose very predicate is itself a sentence: زيدٌ قامَ أبوه; the tradition's name for the kernel's heart is العُمدة. French: the school grammar's phrase simple / composée / complexe, with propositions woven by coordination and subordination around the proposition principale — a map the Port-Royal grammarians were drawing in the 1660s. Spanish: the RAE's oración simple / compuesta — coordinadas strung, subordinadas woven — around the oración principal and its núcleo. Three vocabularies for a comparable climb. What this does not show: that learners of Arabic, French and Spanish pass through these rungs in the same order or at the same pace. The three grammars are illustrations of the late stage, not evidence of one acquisition sequence; that remains an open question.

And the late fingerprint speaks all three languages: Arabic packs the clause into the noun — أريد أن أذهب → أريد الذهاب (the maṣdar, iḍāfa chains, participles doing clause-work); French folds it into infinitives and participles — avant qu'il parte → avant de partir, une lettre qu'il a écrite → une lettre écrite; Spanish reaches for al + infinitivo and the gerund — cuando llegué → al llegar, mientras caminaba → caminando. In all three, late growth lives in the phrase, not the clause count.

Register and scope: dialectal weaving is target-language weaving — a heritage speaker who weaves richly in dialect while producing fuṣḥā kernels sits on a register bridge, not the kernel rung — and the same bridge carries the Spanish heritage speaker who weaves at home but writes school-register kernels, and the French learner fluent in spoken on va… weaving while formal nous-register still produces single clauses. Read the ladder across both registers before placing anyone. And this is the sentence's biography in the new language — performance, not mind: first-language children weave by age three; a Novice rung never means a word-level thinker.

The guard: structure is evidence, never the verdict. We rate sustained function; nobody earns a rung by clause-counting — a perfectly woven sentence that fails its message sits below a plain kernel that succeeds. Weaving keeps mattering, but it stops discriminating: it climbs to early Advanced, then plateaus — the late fingerprint is the dense phrase (the maṣdar for the أنْ-clause, iḍāfa chains, participles doing clause-work), not the clause count. And the mirror guard: the ladder describes, it never rations — do not hold "because" hostage until IH, and never sand a learner's early weaves down to kernels: a speaker reaching above their rung is the ladder working. Above the plate: when sentences stop holding jobs and start holding a thesis — argument sustained, hypothesis entertained — the ladder has left Advanced for Superior. And the principle beneath it all: words are generated inside sentences, not the other way around — understand the sentence, and communication has happened even where a word or two stayed dark.

What transfers across languages, and what doesn't

ACTFL's guidelines are language-neutral in their functions — but the surface markers of a function do NOT transfer by literal translation, because acquisition difficulty differs radically. Example: French opposes periphrastic passé composé to synthetic imparfait; Arabic opposes the bare perfective (māḍī) to the periphrastic past-imperfective (kāna + imperfect) — related aspectual work, done by different machinery, acquired on different paths. What does NOT transfer (Arabic-specific) — declared policies

1. Diglossia: integrated view — the colloquial variety is a documented code choice, not an error; a rating is lowered only when the learner fails to adapt to a context demanding MSA. (Declared position, not an ACTFL rule.)
2. Case endings (iʿrāb) in speech: dropping them does not block any level — pausal, iʿrāb-light delivery is the educated-native norm. (Declared position.)
3. Vowel diacritics in writing: outside the level judgment; orthographic case letters (e.g., tanwīn alif) are spelling, judged separately from diacritics.

THE TRUST TRIANGLE · مُثَلَّثُ الثِّقَة

The third triangle of the family — beside the Methods Triangle (how we think) and the momentum triangle (where the student is moving), this one answers: how far may we trust the material?

C — the wide base: author-written, one person's word — every sample and night is born here; realistic and reasoned, never an answer key. B — the middle band: an independent specialist confirmed the dish is real (the native-speaker eye). A — the apex: two blind raters agreed without seeing each other — only now may a sample serve as an anchor.

The shape is the law: the higher you climb, the narrower the room and the higher the price of entry — and the whole triangle moves upward one workshop at a time. In one line: C = one said it · B = a second checked it · A = two agreed, blind.

Arabic names for the department: اجتهاد (C — one mind's reasoned effort) · اعتضاد (B — propped up by a second, independent witness) · اعتماد (A — officially adopted, the word already on every certificate). Same root pattern (ifti'āl) in all three — the climb has one sound.

The Momentum Triangle — مثلث الحال rising · steady · falling — there is no fourth

Two students at the same rung are not the same student. The level names where they stand; the momentum names what to teach tomorrow — and a student at a level is always in exactly one of three states: improving, holding, or slipping. There is no fourth — that is not a slogan but the trichotomy law of any ordered scale. This panel reads each skill's state from the same crossing engine as the flags above (active ratings only; superseded ghosts excluded) and answers with a differentiated move. This is differentiation made operational.

Declared rules (gate-reviewed): state = comparison of the last two active ratings per skill, or the length of the current unchanged run — steady tiers graduated by altitude: lowlands NL–IL 4/8/12 · midlands IM–IH 6/10/15 · highlands AL+ 7/12/18 months (the ladder narrows as it climbs — ACTFL's famous inverted pyramid — and the school's catalog itself expects ~two years at Intermediate Mid, which is why IM sits in the middle tier, not the fast one). The tiers time the alarm, not the care: a quiet stretch still gets the same regular look; the timer only decides when quiet becomes a case. An oscillation pattern needs at least five ratings: across the last five, ties dropped, three or more strictly alternating legs — below that, the shape is indistinguishable from chance and no verdict fires. It is drawn as a ◌ orbiting the center — not a fourth state, and always a hypothesis to verify (see its card), never a finding. A fall is treated as noise until re-assessed. All patterns here come from a single rater's judgments: a colleague's blind rating of one sample is the cheapest truth-check. The instructional moves are author-crafted practice guidance, not ACTFL doctrine. Train these calls before you need them: the Nights Logbook (Tab 8) rehearses the same judgments with the trap named and the reveal folded.

11Methods Triangle — how thinking, task, and language meet

the cooking techniques — and when a cook is ready for each
For you as a teacher: five ways of thinking-in-writing (describe, narrate history, compare, analyze, research), each with the rung where it becomes honest in the target language — and a scaffolded version for every rung below it. In the kitchen: these are cooking techniques, and a technique attempted too early ruins the dish, not the cook.

A research method is a thinking discipline carried by language functions. Below a certain rung, a student can still think the method — they do it fluently in their first language — but cannot yet perform it in the target language. AERO and ACTFL form the base every school already has; the top vertex is the one usually missing — the Cognitive-Linguistic Apex, thinking-in-language: when and how a way of thinking becomes honest in the target language. This layer implements the Muthallath Model (المُثَلَّث). The triangle joins the three corners this instrument now holds: AERO (the curriculum already demands each method at a named grade), ACTFL (each method's functions have a floor and a comfort rung), and the method itself. Its practical output: matched tasks per rung, each spoken in the student's voice (I can …), so "weak analysis" is never written on what is actually "not enough language yet."

Choose a method

Provenance: the floors and task ladders below are an author-proposed function-floor analysis — the same epistemic status as Layer 10's bridge, equally open to revision by its author — and they are stated for presentational writing: a floor is a vector by mode, never a scalar. HOUSE RULES OF THE FLOORS أعراف الأرضيات
① Floors are prices of tasks, never properties of students — a department adopting this layer forswears, in writing: hardening the scaffolded rung into a placement (every scaffold is a dated bridge with a planned fade), and withholding the independent or stretched version from any student who elects to attempt it.
② Until independent validation runs, floors license no consequential decision about any individual student: they are defaults a teacher may override in either direction.
③ A placement within one sublevel of a floor sits in the border zone — assign the scaffolded version by default.
④ The scaffold class includes the learner's full linguistic repertoire: planning the method's logic in the strongest language, then delivering in the target language, removes construct-irrelevant load at the source.
⑤ For Arabic, a floor must name its register: dialectal performance of a method's full discipline is target-language thinking — never silently penalized as its absence.
