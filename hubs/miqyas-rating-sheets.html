MIQYAS · RATING SHEETS · FACILITATOR NOTE

Keep this page. It is not handed to raters.
v2.7pairs with kit v1.5

Print run

Per rater: one Round 1 sheet, one Round 2 sheet, and one sample booklet in the language they teach (the booklets follow this page: Arabic, French, Spanish). Single-sided A4. The booklet is reused in Round 2; the Round 2 sheet is handed out only after Round 1 has been collected. Sample numbers 1–6 on the sheets are samples 1–6 in the booklet. Give every rater the one-page information sheet for teachers before anything else, and keep a second person beside you for the tally.

Why the two sheets are not identical

Round 1 measures judgment as it walks in. The kit's own rule for that round is that any teaching contaminates it. So the Round 1 sheet says nothing about sustained function, FACT, method floors or memorized frames, carries no worked example in its first row, names no border, and says nothing about what the workshop hopes to see in Round 2. Each of those would teach the very idea the workshop is about to measure the effect of teaching.

Both sheets ask for the major level only (Novice, Intermediate, Advanced, Superior, Distinguished). Sublevels are Workshop 2, on real samples, and a single short sample does not carry enough evidence for one. The Round 2 sheet adds what the lesson has by then taught: the sustained-function reminder, the FACT prompt, and the five Unratable reason codes that match the toolkit's Tab 9 dialog.

The rating column and the confidence column are identical on both sheets. Those two are what the Round 1 / Round 2 comparison is computed from, so the comparison is not disturbed by the additions.

Why neither sheet asks about memorized frames

The Generative Check compares a sample with the frames the learner was given. The six workshop samples were not written from supplied frames, so a rater has nothing to compare them with, and a column asking for that judgment would manufacture data. The check is introduced in Scene 4.5 on the projector and tested separately, on samples written from frames with the frames shown.

Unratable

In Round 1 a rater who circles U writes the reason in their own words. After the workshop, code those free-text reasons into A–E yourself, so that the use of Unratable can be compared across rounds without having taught the categories first. Enter U in the tally calculator's U column: it is missing data, never a low rating, and the calculator leaves it out of every agreement figure, Krippendorff's α included.

Rater codes

The code exists so one rater's Round 1 sheet can be paired with their Round 2 sheet. It is invented by the rater and no name is ever written down. Say the limit plainly at the start: with two to four raters in a language group, a code keeps a sheet unnamed but cannot make its writer unrecognisable to colleagues. Report collective numbers only. At the reveal, never ask who gave a particular rating; ask whether anyone is willing to explain a rating, without saying which one was theirs.

What is pre-printed and why

Samples are numbered 1–6, never labelled with a level, and the booklet order is deliberately not the order of difficulty. The mode is printed for the rater (samples 1, 3, 5 and 6 are speech transcripts, 2 and 4 are writing) because the mode is a fact about the sample, not a judgment to collect.

Key, for the facilitator only. Booklet sample 1 is the toolkit bank's NH-01 (French F1, Spanish S1)  ·  2 is S-01 (F6, S6)  ·  3 is IM-01 (F3, S3)  ·  4 is AL-01 (F5, S5)  ·  5 is IL-01 (F2, S2)  ·  6 is IH-01 (F4, S4). For the Arabic set the bank holds the author's proposed level; for the French and Spanish sets it holds only the border each sample was written for, and no level. Both are folded away, one click from view, in the public toolkit file (Tabs 7 and 8), so ask raters not to open those tabs before the workshop, and do not project them until Round 2 has been collected.
