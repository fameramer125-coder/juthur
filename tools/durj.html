الدُّرْج — مواصفةُ الأداة

ad-Durj — The Drawer · Instrument Specification

النسخة ٥ · ١٨ أغسطس ٢٠٢٦

1. The instrument

ad-Durj (الدُّرْج; the Drawer) is one drawer in each classroom holding what a household keeps. It has two wings, and it is not the instrument without both:

الأَثَر · Athar — what the child brings. The trace of one rare personal object: a first shoe, a boarding pass, a first photograph, a first anything. The object visits; its trace stays.

الْحِكَايَا · Hikaya — what the family tells. A true story from the household, written by the family, read in class, and circulated among the children.

Athar moves an object from home into language. Hikaya moves a household into the classroom. The drawer holds both, and the class opens it aloud: لِنَفْتَحِ الدُّرْج — let us open the Drawer. It is closed again at the end of every session: أَغْلِقُوا الدُّرْج.

The drawer is deliberately an ordinary object. Nothing kept in a drawer is treasure; a drawer holds what a household did not throw away. That is the whole premise.

Always written with tashkeel — الدُّرْج — in every appearance. Unvocalised, "الدرج" reads as دَرَج, stairs. In a project built on precise reading this is not a small point, and the visual identity carries a wooden drawer so the meaning lands before the word is read.

2. Position within Juthur

ad-Durj is an instrument, not a volume. It belongs beside Mirsah, Bawsala, Jumbaz al-Huruf and the Anfas Ritual, which are named for objects and practices — not in the botanical sequence of the Asfar (seed · sprout · branch · leaves · fruit · harvest).

Against the other classroom instruments:

Instrument Works at Supplies the learner with
الْحُرُوفُ الضَّخْمَةُ — Big Letters Below the word A form to say it in
الْكُرَةُ الْعَجِيبَة — The Wondrous Ball The word and the fact A prompt to answer
الدُّرْج — The Drawer Above the sentence Something to say

ad-Durj is the only one that supplies content. Sequenced across a year: form → retrieval → content.

3. Grounding in TRT

ad-Durj is the classroom realisation of the Mirsah (الْمِرْسَاةُ; the Anchor) in Tactile Reading Theory (TRT; نَظَرِيَّةُ الْقِرَاءَةِ الْمَلْمُوسَةِ) — Amer (2024): language fastens most durably to what the body has already handled. The drawer supplies a class set of anchors the teacher did not choose and could not have invented.

It serves four of the six principles directly — Takhsis (التَّخْصِيص; Specification): every child works from different material, so differentiation is structural, not administered · Nafidha (النَّافِذَة; Window): the drawer is a window between household and classroom, and §6 makes it open both ways · Takamul (التَّكَامُل; Integration): one item serves vocabulary, narrative and composition across a term · Tadarruj (التَّدَرُّج; Gradation): §7 and §8.

Every session opens with the Anfas Ritual (طَقْسُ الْأَنْفَاسِ; Amer, 2024) before the drawer is opened. The drawer is not opened into noise.

Delivery follows al-Mushafaha and its three mechanisms — Talqin (التَّلْقِين), Tardid (التَّرْدِيد), Inshad (الإِنْشَاد). Tales are received by ear before they are read on paper; refrains and repeated lines are chanted; the household's voice reaches the child as voice.

Teacher consistency is checked against the TRT Fidelity Scale (مِقْيَاسُ إِخْلَاصِ التَّطْبِيقِ; Amer, 2024). Without it two teachers will run two different instruments — see §11.

Terminology note. ad-Durj is new and is not in the 2024 glossary. It must not be cited as Amer (2024). Cite as Amer (2026), or as an unpublished TRT extension, until it is formally entered.

4. The governing rule: the trace, not the treasure

The drawer never keeps an irreplaceable object.

  1. The object comes for one session.
  2. Within that session it is recorded — photographed, traced on card, weighed, described.
  3. It goes home the same day, before dismissal.
  4. What stays is the بِطَاقَةُ الأَثَر · Athar Card: the trace, the child's words, the date.

This resolves loss, damage, family objection, and the indignity of asking a child to surrender what they love. It also yields a better teaching artefact: a card can be sorted, compared, written on and re-read; a first shoe cannot.

5. Equity — three doors of equal standing

Not every child has a boarding pass. Not every family keeps first photographs. Not every child can ask at home. An instrument that quietly sorts children by what their household preserved is an instrument that harms.

Door The child brings Used when
Object The item itself, for one session It exists and may leave the house
Trace A drawing, tracing, or photograph It exists but cannot travel
Account Words only — an object present, lost, or gone It does not exist or cannot be asked about

The Account door is introduced first, before the Object door, so it reads as a full option and not a consolation. A remembered object that no longer exists must be rebuilt in language — which is more useful, not less.

6. Hikaya — the family wing

6.1 The return leg

The traffic runs both ways, or the instrument fails.

Families that only write, only attend, and only supply will read the arrangement as extraction — and one family saying so aloud in a parents' group ends the project. So:

  • Home → school: the household writes a true story worth retelling.
  • School → home: the child carries home a tale the class made, reads it aloud to the household, and brings back the household's written reply.

The return leg is not decoration. It is the half that makes the school part of the house, rather than the house a supplier to the school.

6.2 Attendance is never the unit

The object problem could be solved invisibly. Attendance cannot. Every child in the room knows exactly whose family came and whose did not — and in Dubai that means working mothers, travelling fathers, non-Arabic-speaking households, children raised by domestic staff, separated families. A schedule of names with empty slots beside particular children does more damage than anything else in this document.

Therefore the tale is decoupled from the teller. The family writes; the reading may be done by the child, by another child, by the teacher, or by a relative on an audio recording. Attendance in person is a bonus and is never announced as an expectation.

Sessions are scheduled by tale, not by child — "this term's three tales" — so that no empty slot ever carries a name.

6.3 What a tale is

True, and worth retelling. Not "amusing." Requiring funny stories excludes households whose best stories are not funny and pressures the rest into performance. Humour arrives or it does not.

Length: 150–400 words. One page. Written in whatever Arabic the family has — see §6.5.

6.4 Capacity — say the real number aloud

Twenty-five children times one family visit is twenty-five sessions. That does not survive contact with a syllabus.

Three tales per session, one session per term, nine tales per class per year. The tale is therefore a class anthology, not an individual entitlement. Say this to children and families at the outset. A promise of "everyone's family" that the calendar cannot keep is worse than no promise.

Across a school career a child's household contributes several tales; in any single year, most do not. Frame the unit as the class's collection, never as a turn owed.

6.5 The dialect bridge — this is the lesson, not the problem

Families will write in ʿāmmiyya, or in English. Do not correct this at intake and do not ask for fuṣḥā.

The class converts the tale together, from ʿāmmiyya to fuṣḥā, as the core exercise. It is the most useful diglossia work available at any level, it costs nothing, it treats the family's language as material rather than error, and it gives the child a reason to care about a register they otherwise experience as school-only.

English-written tales follow the same path with a translation stage first.

6.6 Tale exchange

A child tells another household's tale.

This forces perspective-taking, lifts from the shy child the burden of exposing their own family, ends the problem of "my family has nothing worth telling," and reproduces how oral tradition actually moved. It also makes the tale a shared object of the class rather than private property.

Own-family telling remains available to any child who wants it.

6.7 Editorial reading

Family-written text entering an American school classroom unread is a risk: political content, other children named, family disputes, detail unsuitable for the age.

The teacher reads every tale before circulation and may ask for a change or decline it. This is framed to families in writing as editorial, in the way any anthology is edited — not as vetting.

6.8 The draw — when offers exceed places

Nine tale-slots a year against a class of twenty or more means offers will exceed places. The places are allocated by public draw (قُرْعَة).

The draw is not an administrative convenience. It changes what the child concludes from a name that is not called: not my family did not care, but the draw did not fall. That substitution is the entire reason for it, and four conditions protect it:

  1. Only families who offered enter the draw. No one who did not offer is named, counted, listed, or asked again. The draw protects those who put themselves forward; silence protects everyone else.
  2. It is drawn in front of the children, by the children. A draw conducted in a staff room and announced afterwards is not a draw; it is a decision wearing the costume of chance, and children detect the difference.
  3. A name drawn leaves the bag until the bag is empty. Terms will not be equal; years will.
  4. The written tale enters the anthology whether or not the name is drawn. The nine are reading slots, not anthology places: attendance and publication are separate doors — §10 — and nothing a household wrote is discarded because a draw went elsewhere.

Where fewer families offer than there are places, there is no draw and no comment.

7. Athar — how the object becomes the lesson

One item, revisited at rising demand:

Stage Task Language outcome
1 · Naming Name it, and five things about it that can be touched Concrete noun and adjective stock
2 · Sensing Describe it through four senses without naming it; the class guesses Sensory lexis; circumlocution
3 · Dating Place it on the class timeline; who held it before you Past tense; temporal connectives
4 · Telling How it came to you, in five sentences, in order Narrative sequence
5 · Comparing Pair with another child's card; what do the two share Comparative structures; abstraction
6 · Arguing What makes a thing worth keeping at all Justification; evidence; generalisation
7 · Composing Write the item's own account, in its voice Sustained composition; register shift

Stages 1–4 sit in the primary years; 5–7 are secondary.

Cross-subject. One card set serves Social Studies (migration, timelines), Moral Education (belonging, stewardship), and Islamic Studies (أَمَانَة — a thing held in trust: the ethics of what we preserve and why).

8. The card follows the child

An Athar Card does not die with the class.

Each card is archived in the child's own sleeve and travels with them through the school. In a later year the child receives their earlier card back and rewrites the same item at the new stage — the Grade 2 card returning in Grade 9.

Three things follow, and the third is the largest:

  1. The child meets their own earlier language and can see the distance travelled. Nothing else in the school does this.
  2. Tadarruj becomes literal rather than notional: the same object, ten years of rising demand.
  3. It produces a longitudinal corpus of child Arabic writing — the same children, the same stimuli, tracked across a decade. With family consent and ethical clearance, this is the most publishable asset in the Juthur programme, and no equivalent dataset exists for Arabic.

Consent for research use is separate from consent to participate, opt-in, revocable, and never a condition of taking part.

9. The secondary frame — a different instrument in the same drawer

The weakest assumption in v1 was that adolescents would cooperate with a memory exercise. A fifteen-year-old will not carry his first shoe past his classmates. In Grades 9–12 the sentimental frame does not merely underperform; it produces refusal, and refusal is contagious.

At secondary the vocabulary changes and the question hardens:

Primary Secondary
ذِكْرَى — a memory دَلِيل · مَعْرُوض — evidence, an exhibit
"What do you remember about it?" "What does a household keep, and why?"
The child's own object Any household's object, including one they did not choose
Telling Argument, ethics, inheritance, impermanence

Same drawer, same cards, same corpus. An intellectual question rather than an emotional one — which is also how the material opens onto value, gratitude and أَمَانَة in an Islamic Studies classroom without any of it being preached.

10. Output — the drawer must publish

v1 had nothing leaving the drawer. Juthur is a publishing programme; the instrument should behave like one.

Each class issues one small annual anthology — its tales for the year and a selection of Athar cards, in fuṣḥā after the §6.5 conversion, with the household credited as author.

And an annual audio archive. Every tale is recorded — read by the household, the child, or a classmate — and kept as the class's spoken record for the year. Under al-Mushafaha the recording is not an accessory to the printed anthology; it is the primary artefact, and the book is its written trace. The archive is what allows a tale to be heard again in a later year by children who were not in the room, which no printed page achieves.

The archive is audio only. No video — §8 of the doctrine, and the reason a relative who cannot attend sends a voice rather than a face.

This gives families a physical reason to take part, converts a year of work into an artefact the child owns, and is the strongest single evidence object to place in front of an inspection team. Production is a stapled or perfect-bound booklet; the cost sits inside §13.

11. Assessment and fidelity

Assessment is of the language, never of the object, the tale, or the household. State this to teachers in writing: the pull toward rewarding the interesting item is real, and it punishes precisely the children §5 exists to protect.

Dimension Evidence
Sensory precision Concrete, specific terms rather than general ones
Sequence Events ordered and connected
Elaboration Extends beyond the prompt unasked
Register Adjusts language to task and audience; ʿāmmiyya → fuṣḥā control
Return Revisits the card unprompted

The last dimension draws on the Lathatha Index (مُؤَشِّرُ اللَّذَاذَةِ; Amer, 2024). A card handled many times is the strongest single indicator the instrument is working.

Not scored: rarity, cost, condition, photogenic quality, family attendance, or how much the household supplied.

Fidelity. Each teacher is checked once a term against the TRT Fidelity Scale on five points: Anfas before opening · Account door introduced first · object returned same day · tale decoupled from attendance · assessment of language only.

12. Safeguarding

Risk Control
Irreplaceable item lost §4 — the object never stays overnight
A child has nothing to bring §5 — the Account door, introduced first
A family cannot attend §6.2 — attendance is never the unit
Unsuitable family content §6.7 — editorial reading before circulation
Family privacy exposed The child decides what is said; provenance is never required
Painful memory surfaces Any card or tale retired on request, without discussion
Comparison and status Cards indexed by month, not by child; no display wall, no ranking
Cultural or religious sensitivity Images of people optional throughout
Research use §8 — separate opt-in consent, revocable, never a condition

A card belongs to the child, not the class, and may be withdrawn permanently at any time without a reason. Say this at the outset — the guarantee is what makes honest cards possible.

Home letter, one page in Arabic, before launch: objects return the same day · a drawing or a description is equally welcome · tales may be written in any Arabic · no family is expected to attend.

13. Rollout

Phase Scope Success criterion
1 One drawer, one class, one term 90% of children produce a card; zero objects lost; three tales circulated
2 One grade level, all sections Cards used in three subjects; ʿāmmiyya→fuṣḥā conversion running; first anthology printed
3 Whole department Secondary frame running at Stages 5–7; cards archived and following children; Lathatha returns tracked

Cost. AED 150–250 per classroom in year one (drawer, 200 cards, sleeves), under AED 100 a year after, plus roughly AED 200 per class for the annual anthology. The cheapest instrument in the programme by a wide margin, and the only one needing no manufacturing partner, no electronics, and no supply chain.

14. What ad-Durj is not

  • Not show-and-tell. Show-and-tell ends at the telling; this begins there.
  • Not a display case or a keepsake store.
  • Not a time capsule — nothing is sealed, nothing waits.
  • Not an assessment of families.
  • Not a guaranteed turn for every household — §6.4.

الْمُشَافَهَةُ

جُذُور لَيْسَ مَشْرُوعًا ضِدَّ الشَّاشَةِ؛ جُذُور مَشْرُوعٌ لِإِعَادَةِ الصَّوْتِ الْغَائِبِ. فَالشَّاشَةُ لَيْسَتِ الدَّاءَ، بَلْ هِيَ الْبَدِيلُ الَّذِي يَحْضُرُ حِينَ يَسْكُتُ صَوْتٌ.

وَالْمُشَافَهَةُ نَقْلُ اللُّغَةِ مِنْ فَمٍ إِلَى أُذُنٍ بِحُضُورِ إِنْسَان: تَلْقِينٌ، ثُمَّ تَرْدِيدٌ، ثُمَّ إِنْشَادٌ. الْكِتَابُ يَحْفَظُ، وَالشَّيْخُ يُقْرِئُ؛ فَلَيْسَتِ الْمُشَافَهَةُ ضِدَّ الْوَرَقِ، وَإِنَّمَا ضِدَّ أَنْ يَحُلَّ الْوَسِيطُ مَحَلَّ الْإِنْسَان.

يُقَدَّمُ هَذَا الْعَمَلُ صَوْتًا — إِنْتَاجًا وَسَمَاعًا — بِلَا صُورَةٍ مُتَحَرِّكَةٍ فِي الْمَرْحَلَةِ الِابْتِدَائِيَّةِ وَمَا قَبْلَهَا. وَتُسْتَثْنَى التِّقْنِيَةُ الْمُسَاعِدَةُ الْمَوْصُوفَةُ لِطِفْلٍ بِعَيْنِهِ، بِلَا قَيْدٍ.

al-Mushafaha

Juthur is not a programme against screens. Juthur is a programme to return an absent voice. The screen is not the disease; it is the substitute that arrives when a voice stops.

al-Mushafaha is the transmission of language mouth to ear, in the presence of another person: Talqin, then Tardid, then Inshad. Print carries and voice transmits — the doctrine is not opposed to books, only to a medium standing in for a person.

This work is delivered by voice, produced and heard, with no moving image in the primary years and below. Assistive technology prescribed for an individual child is exempt without qualification.
