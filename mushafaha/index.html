الْمُشَافَهَة — ميثاقُ جُذور الحاكم

al-Mushafaha — the Governing Doctrine

النسخة ٥ · ١٨ أغسطس ٢٠٢٦

1. The thesis

Juthur is not a programme against screens. Juthur is a programme to return an absent voice.

The distinction is the whole document. A movement defined by what it refuses is hostage to the panic that produced it; when the panic moves on, the movement dates. A doctrine defined by what it restores outlives the occasion that prompted it — and the absence of screens follows from it as a consequence rather than standing as a slogan.

The screen is not the disease. The screen is the substitute that arrives when a voice stops. Remove the screen without restoring the voice and nothing has been treated; a vacuum has been created and it will be refilled within the month, by the same device, with the teacher's own hand.

al-Mushafaha (الْمُشَافَهَةُ) is the transmission of language mouth to ear, in the presence of another person. It is the oldest pedagogy in the Arabic tradition and the most rigorously tested: an unbroken chain of oral transmission, verified person by person, that has preserved a text verbatim for fourteen centuries without a screen, a slide, or a video.

2. Where this comes from

The argument originates in The Mother is Absent in the Time of the Pandemic (الْأُمُّ غَائِبَةٌ فِي زَمَنِ الْجَائِحَةِ) — Amer. The pandemic did not create the absence it exposed. It removed the scaffolding that had been concealing a household in which the adult voice had already gone quiet, and it handed the child a device to fill the silence.

A necessary guard, stated here and repeated wherever this argument is presented. The absence is an absence of voice, not of employment. It falls to fathers as often as to mothers, and it occurs in households where every adult is physically present and no one is speaking. Nothing in this doctrine is a claim about whether women should work. Any presentation of this material that omits this guard will be heard as a social argument about working mothers, and will deserve to be.

3. What al-Mushafaha is — and is not

Classically, the book preserves and the teacher transmits. A copy of the Qur'an makes no one a reciter; the ijaza is granted by a person to a person, and the written text is the record of the chain, not a replacement for it.

Therefore: print carries, voice transmits. al-Mushafaha is not opposed to books, and cannot be — Juthur is a publishing programme. It is opposed to a medium standing in for a person. A printed volume that a teacher reads aloud is Mushafaha. A recording of that teacher, listened to and answered, is Mushafaha. A video that plays while the adult withdraws is not.

The test is a single question: does the medium carry the voice to the child, or does it excuse the adult from being there?

4. Relation to Tactile Reading Theory — a delivery doctrine, not a seventh principle

al-Mushafaha is not a new principle of TRT and must never be presented as one.

Tactile Reading Theory (TRT; نَظَرِيَّةُ الْقِرَاءَةِ الْمَلْمُوسَةِ) — Amer (2024) — rests on six principles: Tazamun · Tadarruj · Takhsis · Nafidha · Shumul · Takamul. That set is published and closed. Adding a seventh after publication would destabilise the framework and invite the reasonable objection that the theory was revised to fit a later commitment.

al-Mushafaha sits above the theory as its condition of delivery. TRT describes how reading is acquired. al-Mushafaha governs how it is transmitted. The two do not compete and neither amends the other.

It is, however, continuous with TRT rather than foreign to it: hearing and breath are two of the four senses TRT works in, the Anfas Ritual (طَقْسُ الْأَنْفَاسِ; Amer, 2024) is already a breath practice, and the Lathatha Index (مُؤَشِّرُ اللَّذَاذَةِ; Amer, 2024) measures a pleasure that in Arabic is overwhelmingly aural — rhythm, assonance, rhyme.

Terminology note. al-Mushafaha as a named doctrine is new and is not in the 2024 glossary. Cite as Amer (2026) or as an unpublished extension. Do not attribute it to Amer (2024).

5. The three mechanisms

Mechanism Arabic Function In practice
Talqin التَّلْقِين Transmission The adult says it first; the child receives it from a person, not a page
Tardid التَّرْدِيد Fixing The child returns it, repeatedly and across days, until it is held
Inshad الإِنْشَاد Intensification Melody and rhythm carry what plain speech cannot hold

Receive · return · sing. The sequence is not decorative; each stage fails without the one before it.

6. Why Inshad and Tardid are load-bearing, not ornamental

They replace what the screen was doing. A screen supplies two things a classroom otherwise struggles to produce: repetition without boredom, and affect. Any policy that removes the screen without supplying both is subtraction, and teachers will reverse it — correctly — because they still have to teach. Melody and structured repetition supply both, at no cost and with no device.

They carry precisely what Arabic-weak learners lose first. Chanted and sung text encodes vowel length (madd), gemination (shadda), and quantity — the exact features tashkeel represents and the first to collapse in a heritage speaker educated in English. A child who sings a line with correct madd has internalised quantity without a rule having been taught. No explanation achieves this; rhythm achieves it as a by-product.

They are the tradition's own memory technology. Metre exists because language held in rhythm survives; the Qur'an is preserved by recitation, not by storage.

Three conditions, without which this does harm:

  1. The reference recording must be authoritative. Melody fixes error as durably as it fixes accuracy — a line learned sung wrongly is harder to correct than one never learned. Reference audio is produced by a qualified reciter and not by whoever volunteered.
  2. Inshad, not music. Unaccompanied voice, or percussion only. Families in an international school will differ on instrumentation and will not differ on chanting. This costs nothing and prevents an avoidable objection.
  3. Repetition is scheduled, not massed. Four returns across four days hold; twenty in one sitting do not.

7. Movement, and the allocation of time

7.1 Song and movement are one mechanism

Rhythm is bodily before it is auditory; no one holds a beat without a body. In Arabic the coupling is not incidental. Madd and shadda are quantities of time, and duration is grasped by movement long before it is grasped by the eye. A child who claps a long vowel has understood quantity; a child who has had madd explained to him has understood nothing yet. The rocking of the kuttab was a technique, not a mannerism.

Inshad and gross motor work are therefore recorded here as one mechanism, not two, and are timetabled together.

A distinction that must be kept. Gross motor work — whole-body movement, clapping, stepping, rocking — belongs with song, on the oral side. Fine motor work — pencil grip, letter formation, tracing — belongs with writing. Collapsing the two under one heading moves the physical half of the method onto the wrong side of the ledger and quietly reduces the oral share to singing alone.

7.2 The allocation

Oral–kinesthetic work (Talqin · Tardid · Inshad · gross motor) against print work (reading, writing, fine motor), as a share of instructional minutes:

Stage Oral–kinesthetic Print
Pre-K · KG 85% 15%
Grades 1–2 80% 20%
Grade 3 70% 30%
Grade 4 60% 40%
Grade 5 50% 50%
Grade 6 40% 60%
Grade 7 35% 65%
Grade 8 30% 70%
Grades 9–12 25% — floor 75%

The floor is structural, not a rounding. A gradient descending at a fixed rate reaches zero in the upper secondary years, and a doctrine that expires at Grade 10 is a technique for young children, not a governing principle. Recitation, oral argument, khutba delivery and dialogue keep the oral share load-bearing to the end of school. Below 25% the doctrine has been abandoned, whatever the documents say.

The denominator. Percentages are of instructional minutes averaged across a two-week cycle, not per lesson. Per-lesson ratios cannot survive an irregular timetable and will be satisfied on paper rather than in practice.

Status of these figures. They are a design commitment, not a research finding. No study yields 70% for Grade 3 rather than 65%, and this document does not pretend otherwise. They are declared, published, applied consistently, and reviewed annually against Lathatha returns and oral production data. Consistency and honest declaration are what make them defensible; a claim of empirical derivation would not survive the first question.

7.3 Differentiation — and its direction

"Allowance for individual differences" without a stated direction protects no one, because each teacher will improvise and the improvisation will follow instinct.

The direction is stated: a struggling reader, a child with dyslexia, or a child with language delay stays at the higher oral share, and stays there longer. They are not accelerated into print.

The prevailing instinct runs the other way — a weak reader is given more reading drill — and that instinct contradicts this programme's own theory. Under TRT-Inclusive (Amer, 2024) the response to a fragile decoder is to strengthen the phonological and bodily foundation the decoding must rest on, not to increase practice on the unsupported skill.

Adjustment is by stage, not by exemption: a Grade 4 child may sit at the Grade 3 allocation for a term. No child is moved below the floor of their stage in the other direction without a documented reason.

7.4 How this is stated to parents

Not as delayed reading. In an English-medium American school, "Grade 1 is 80% singing and movement" will be heard as my child is not being taught to read, and the objection is reasonable if the framing invites it.

The argument is a reading argument. Phonological awareness — hearing and manipulating the sounds of the language — precedes decoding and predicts it; this is among the most consistently supported findings in early literacy research and is embedded in mainstream literacy guidance. The 80% is not a reduction of literacy instruction. It is the condition under which the 20% holds. Print time is smaller, denser, and better prepared.

Do not argue from school starting age. The claim that later formal reading instruction produces equal or better readers — usually made by pointing to Finland — is actively contested among reading researchers, and leading authorities argue directly against delay. Deploying it invites a rebuttal that damages the phonological-awareness case standing beside it, which is solid. The programme does not delay reading; it prepares it.

8. The position on screens

Stated positively: Juthur is delivered by voice — produced and heard — with no moving image, in the primary years and below. No video, no slide decks, no projected presentation. Audio is produced, recorded, listened to, and archived.

A screen is defined here as a surface delivering moving image or scrollable content. This definition is not incidental — without it the doctrine forbids Juthur's own instruments. A static character on an LED array is not a screen. A teacher's administrative dashboard is an adult tool and outside the scope. The Wondrous Ball (الْكُرَةُ الْعَجِيبَة) is compliant; so is any device that displays one fixed symbol and does not scroll, animate, or feed.

Four supports, in order of strength:

Displacement. A screen need not be harmful in itself to be a loss; it is sufficient that it occupies time drawn from talk, listening, movement, play and print. This argument is arithmetical rather than clinical and cannot be answered by pointing to a well-made programme.

Health guidance. The World Health Organization (2019) recommends no sedentary screen time for children under two, and no more than one hour for ages two to four. An early-years setting using screens is operating outside international health guidance, not inside best practice.

Precedent. Sweden — among the most digitised school systems in the world — has reversed course, restoring printed textbooks and handwriting after declines in reading comprehension. The precedent matters because it is not a conservative system resisting technology; it is a system that took digitisation furthest and retreated on evidence.

Institutional coherence. A school that confiscates phones in the corridor and teaches by slide deck in the classroom has told the child that screens are harmful when he chooses them and beneficial when we do. Children read that contradiction before they read the policy.

What this paper does not claim. That television and tablets cause later smartphone dependency. Habituation and displacement are defensible; a causal chain is not established, and asserting it as settled invites a single correction that discredits everything defensible alongside it. The weaker claim is sufficient and it holds.

9. The one exception — non-negotiable

Assistive technology prescribed for an individual child is exempt without qualification.

A deaf child requires visual access. A non-speaking child may require an augmentative communication device. A child kept at home by illness reaches the classroom only by link. An absolute prohibition does not read as pedagogical courage in an inclusive setting; it reads as exclusion, and it is answerable as such.

The principle warns that a good is sometimes abandoned because it is the road to an evil. Applied here, without this clause, it runs in reverse: a certain good for a specific child would be surrendered against a possible harm to others. This clause protects the doctrine as much as it protects the child, and it opens nothing — it grants no one a screen for teaching.

Research The audio archive is data. Longitudinal oral corpora in Arabic barely exist
Teacher formation Every teacher records reference audio. Fidelity is checked on delivery, not on planning

11. What al-Mushafaha does not mean

  • Not opposition to print. §3.
  • Not opposition to technology for adults — administration, recording, archiving, analysis are unaffected.
  • Not a claim about mothers' employment. §2.
  • Not a prohibition that binds colleagues outside the programme. §12.
  • Not a moral panic. It survives the removal of every screen-related claim in §8 and rests on the tradition in §3.

12. How this is adopted — identity, not mandate

Do not seek a prohibition. A ban is a rule enforced against colleagues, requires authority over divisions one does not hold, and is re-litigated every term. It also invites its own defeat: one exception, one complaint, one accreditation query.

Declare a property instead:

Juthur is a screen-free method. Voice produced, voice heard.

This binds no one, requires no permission, cannot be voted down, and becomes a distinguishing mark rather than a dispute. Results accumulate; others ask why. The practice is identical and the politics are inverted — the difference between imposing a thing and being asked for it.

13. How this appears across the programme

A doctrine that lives in one document is a document. al-Mushafaha is the face of Juthur and must be visible on everything the programme produces, in whichever of three registers the surface allows.

13.1 Three registers

The line. For a spine, a footer, a site header, a slide of one sentence, an email signature:

جُذُور: مَنْهَجٌ بِلَا شَاشَةٍ. صَوْتًا يُنْتَج، وَصَوْتًا يُسْمَع. Juthur is a screen-free method. Voice produced, voice heard.

The block. For a verso page, an inside cover, an about page, the front matter of any specification, the first page of any teacher pack — the standard eight-line statement issued as The Mushafaha Front Matter, reproduced without alteration.

The charter. This document, in full, for administration, accreditation, inspection, funders, and any external presentation of the programme.

No Juthur artefact is issued without at least the line. This is a production rule, not an aspiration: an artefact that carries nothing has not been finished.

The source documents. The Mushafaha Front Matter is the source of the three registers, not a carrier of them, and is exempt: the registers are its content, and a second copy inside the source is the one place drift could not be caught by comparison. This charter is not exempt and carries the line beneath its title.

Presence is not compliance. A block that is present but altered — a word added to its heading, a paragraph dropped, the English half missing — is a breach, and a more dangerous one than absence, because it passes a careless check. Verification is by character-for-character comparison against The Mushafaha Front Matter, never by presence alone.

13.3 In the mother book

Juthur Reads (جُذُورٌ تَقْرَأُ; Amer, 2024) is the programme's source text, and al-Mushafaha belongs in it as the most prominent single statement the reader meets — not appended, not in an annex, not folded into a later chapter.

Its placement is a preface preceding Part 1, before the theoretical foundations. The reason is precise and should be stated in the book itself: the doctrine does not precede the theory logically — TRT is the account of how reading is acquired, and al-Mushafaha governs only how it is transmitted — but it precedes it in the reading order, because a reader who reaches the six principles without knowing how they are to be delivered will deliver them by whatever means is nearest, which in 2026 is a screen.

The preface carries §1 (the thesis), §2 (the origin, with the guard on absence of voice rather than of employment), §3 (print carries, voice transmits), §4 (why this is not a seventh principle), §5 (the three mechanisms), §8 (the position on screens) and §9 (the assistive-technology exception). The remainder stays in this charter and is referenced.

§9 is not optional in the preface. An inclusion officer or accreditor who reads §13.3 must not be able to conclude that the assistive-technology exception is confined to the charter and absent from the book.

On the ordering of editions. Where the published edition cannot yet carry the preface, the block under §13.1 goes on the verso of the next printing, and the preface enters at the next revision. Nothing waits for a new edition.

وَالْمُشَافَهَةُ نَقْلُ اللُّغَةِ مِنْ فَمٍ إِلَى أُذُنٍ بِحُضُورِ إِنْسَان: تَلْقِينٌ، ثُمَّ تَرْدِيدٌ، ثُمَّ إِنْشَادٌ. الْكِتَابُ يَحْفَظُ، وَالشَّيْخُ يُقْرِئُ؛ فَلَيْسَتِ الْمُشَافَهَةُ ضِدَّ الْوَرَقِ، وَإِنَّمَا ضِدَّ أَنْ يَحُلَّ الْوَسِيطُ مَحَلَّ الْإِنْسَان.

يُقَدَّمُ هَذَا الْعَمَلُ صَوْتًا — إِنْتَاجًا وَسَمَاعًا — بِلَا صُورَةٍ مُتَحَرِّكَةٍ فِي الْمَرْحَلَةِ الِابْتِدَائِيَّةِ وَمَا قَبْلَهَا. وَتُسْتَثْنَى التِّقْنِيَةُ الْمُسَاعِدَةُ الْمَوْصُوفَةُ لِطِفْلٍ بِعَيْنِهِ، بِلَا قَيْدٍ.

al-Mushafaha

Juthur is not a programme against screens. Juthur is a programme to return an absent voice. The screen is not the disease; it is the substitute that arrives when a voice stops.

al-Mushafaha is the transmission of language mouth to ear, in the presence of another person: Talqin, then Tardid, then Inshad. Print carries and voice transmits — the doctrine is not opposed to books, only to a medium standing in for a person.

This work is delivered by voice, produced and heard, with no moving image in the primary years and below. Assistive technology prescribed for an individual child is exempt without qualification.
