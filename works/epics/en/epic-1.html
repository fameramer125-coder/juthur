Translator's Threshold

This fascicle opens the companion literary volume promised at the close of the Collected Codex (EN v1.0): the full, circle-by-circle English rendering of the Four Arcs. The source is the ratified Arabic edition of the first epic of Sindbad and the Lost Treasure, Tree Stage (12+) of the Juthur project, grounded in Concrete Reading Theory (CRT; نَظَرِيَّةُ الْقِرَاءَةِ الْمَلْمُوسَةِ; Amer, 2024).

Conventions held throughout: proprietary terms of Amer (2024) are never translated, only romanized; the project name remains Juthur ; ritual and pull lines are given bilingually so the Arabic voice is never out of the reader's ear; the Silence-of-the-Question stations preserve their durations, breath counts, and body verbs exactly. Each epic is a Safar, a voyage that begins the sea anew, not a chapter.

This is a professional English draft of the author's ratified text, faithful sentence by sentence, pending review by a human Arabic-to-English literary translator before external publication.

A Threshold · the door to four voyages

عَتَبَةٌ

Before you lie four arcs that sail an astonishing archipelago: three in which every island bears the name of a virtue while its reality conceals that virtue's opposite, and a fourth in which no island lies by its name, but a daylight burns that never goes out. One question weaves them all: is the name the thing named? Or is meaning never known by the name alone, but by the context we live beneath it?

These voyages belong to the third stage of the Juthur project, the "Tree" stage (twelve years and above), whose bodily motto is: «جَسَدِي يَسْتَشْعِرُ، بَصِيرَتِي تَتَذَوَّقُ»، My body senses; my insight tastes. Here the imagination widens to the great questions: fear, memory, freedom, presence, and what it means to search.

"The skeleton is hidden, the literature is visible, and the body reads before the mind." Every epic leans on one inner sense. This first one leans on Balance : a sea that lifts and lowers, a vertigo that begs for a direction, and a broken compass that will not settle on any north. And gold is the gathering thread that binds the senses, as the root binds its branches.

Its hero is Sindbad, a sailor who drowned seven times and learned not fear but the question, searching for a "lost treasure" he believed to be a thing that exists, and the root of his search is a question he did not understand, asked by his mother before she boarded the sea and never returned. He is accompanied by Juha , who tells the truth in the shape of a joke. And he is followed by a shadow he calls the Hungry One : not a monster to be seen, but a hunger that is never filled, devouring wakefulness and presence.

The Golden Rule of Juthur: «لَا تَصِفْ لَهُ مَا سَيُحِسُّ بِهِ؛ دَعْهُ يُحِسَّ، ثُمَّ صِفْ مَا حَدَثَ»، Do not describe to him what he is about to feel; let him feel, then describe what happened. The endings here are therefore left open, and the meanings are left without a lock.

Some scenes close with a pause called the Silence of the Question: a number of seconds, two breaths, and one question aimed at the body rather than the mind. It never tells you what you will feel; it asks where you feel it. The word at its foot, such as It opens or It clenches, names a movement, not a meaning. Wait the seconds, answer aloud only if you wish, and skip any pause that does not sit well. No pause is a test.

And these are voyages , not chapters of one continuous novel; each Safar begins the sea anew, because "the sea only remembers." If the compass returns, and the Hungry One returns, and the companions return, it is because what truly repeats is the search itself, not its events. And one thread keeps returning on Juha's tongue:

«إِذَا وَجَدْتَ مَا تَبْحَثُ عَنْهُ، سَتَتَمَنَّى أَنَّكَ مَا بَحَثْتَ.»

If you find what you are searching for, you will wish you had never searched.

Is it a warning? Or does it, when we arrive, turn into an answer? That is the journey of this book.

The Isle of Bustle · the first port, the first mask to invert

جَزِيرَةُ النَّشَاطِ

1 / 1 A Sea That Remembers بَحْرٌ يَتَذَكَّرُ

Plate · A sea that only remembers

A shoreless sea, waves that do not break but breathe, and a black shadow crossing the horizon.

بَحْرٌ بِلَا شَاطِئٍ، مَوْجٌ لَا يَنْكَسِرُ بَلْ يَتَنَفَّسُ، وَظِلٌّ أَسْوَدُ يَعْبُرُ الْأُفُقَ

A sea without a shore. Waves that do not break, but breathe: a deep inhale, a deep exhale. A shattered ship whose planks clatter against each other like shivering teeth, and on the water's surface the skeleton of a captain whose hand still grips the helm.

Sindbad stands on a broken mast that tilts under his feet with every wave, distributing his weight between his legs like one who learned how to stand from drowning, not from being saved. The water reaches his chest, cold, pulling him gently toward the bottom as though it were calling him by name. He does not resist; he lets his body sway with the water instead of stiffening against it. He looks at the horizon, then laughs, a laugh that comes from a place deeper than fear.

Sindbad: For the seventh time you drown me, O sea… perhaps you love me.

He was not searching for gold. When he was small, his mother asked him a single question he did not understand, then boarded the sea and never returned. Ever since, he sails, believing the lost treasure to be the answer which, if he found it, would prove his mother right to have waited.

From beneath the water Juha appears; he does not swim, he walks along the seabed, an apple in his hand.

Juha: Ever since I ate Noah's apple, the sea no longer wets me.

Sindbad: I do not eat apples underwater.

Juha: Then you will keep on drowning.

The remains of an ancient ship drift past them. Juha throws it a glance.

Juha: This was an old friend. Before he died he told me: the sea drowns no one. The sea only remembers.

A black shadow crosses the horizon. Not a bird and not a cloud, but a void that moves. It vanishes.

Only a glimpse, yet something in his stomach contracted, and a cold that did not come from the water ran through his arms. It was not fear of a monster that can be seen, but of something that knows his name and waits.

Sindbad: What is that?

Juha: Something hungry.

Sindbad draws his grandfather's compass from inside his shirt. The glass is cracked across, and the needle, which once pointed north without a doubt, swings, slows, and swings again, refusing every direction the sea has a name for. He does not put it away. It is the one thing the water has not taken from him.

1 / 2 The Island That Sleeps الْجَزِيرَةُ الَّتِي تَنَامُ

On the back of Juha's flying donkey they reach an island that is painfully green, its air heavy with jasmine and thyme. White sands into which the feet sink deeper than they should.

The waitress is asleep and the coffee cup is still hot. The fisherman is asleep and his nets were never cast. The children are asleep and their pencils tremble. Above every sleeper floats a dream-bubble, its lower half a cold grey, its upper half brightly colored. One bubble cracks, and black smoke seeps out of it.

Sindbad: Wake up!

The Sleeper: Leave me… my dreams are better…

Juha: Do not wake him. Only this girl can.

1 / 3 Zaynab زَيْنَبُ

Zaynab appears from behind a palm tree. Ten years old, holding a wooden mask that belongs to her father.

Zaynab: I was asleep… and I dreamt that two men would come, one of them laughing as he drowned.

Juha: She is not a child. She is the island's memory.

Sindbad: Then why don't you wake them?

Zaynab: Because when they wake… they will weep. And then I will know that I was the cause.

1 / 4 The Incomplete Awakening الصَّحْوَةُ النَّاقِصَةُ

Sindbad takes the mask and puts it on upside down, and the smile inverts into weeping. Zaynab takes it, turns it over, smiles. She places it on her father's face; he opens his eyes, sees the weeping mask, groans, closes them again.

Sindbad: Once more. Do not wake them onto what they have lost, wake them onto what is still in their hands.

So Sindbad grasped the first lesson of the journey: that the island's name, "Bustle," makes no bustle, and that names may be the first of lies.

فِي الْأُفُقِ ظِلٌّ أَسْوَدُ يَتَرَقَّبُ. صَوْتٌ خَفِيضٌ: «سَآكُلُ يَقَظَتَكَ قَبْلَ نَوْمِكَ.»

On the horizon a black shadow lies in wait. A low voice: "I will eat your wakefulness before your sleep."

The Isle of Mirrors · where one meets the face he flees

جَزِيرَةُ الْمَرَايَا

2 / 1 The Living Mirror الْمِرْآةُ الْحَيَّةُ

Plate · The living mirror

A black hand emerges from the side of the mirror, it has no color except absence.

يَدٌ سَوْدَاءُ تَخْرُجُ مِنْ جَانِبِ الْمِرْآةِ، لَا لَوْنَ لَهَا إِلَّا الْغِيَابُ

An island of glass. Every wall a mirror, and the water itself reflects. In one great mirror Sindbad sees a child weeping on a shore, and a father walking away. The child stretches out his hand, and a black hand emerges from the side of the mirror.

Voice of the Hungry One: Give me your face…

The Child in the Reflection: Why did you leave me?

Sindbad grips the mirror's edge. His tears fall into the water; it ripples, then stills, and now it reflects his own face: tired, sorrowful, but his eyes no longer afraid.

Sindbad: This is me.

And he felt something loosen behind his ribs, as though a fist that had stayed clenched for years was finally letting go.

The Silence of the Question

ثَمَانِ ثَوَانٍ · Eight seconds

نَفَسَانِ… · Two breaths… then notice

When you saw your own face in the mirror, place your palm on your chest. Did something open behind your ribs, or close? Describe its movement, not its meaning.

حِينَ رَأَيْتَ وَجْهَكَ فِي الْمِرْآةِ، ضَعْ كَفَّكَ عَلَى صَدْرِكَ. هَلِ انْفَرَجَ شَيْءٌ خَلْفَ ضُلُوعِكَ، أَمِ انْطَبَقَ؟ صِفْ حَرَكَتَهُ، لَا مَعْنَاهَا.

It opens يَنْفَرِجُ

2 / 2 Marjana and the Transparent Mirror مَرْجَانَةُ وَالْمِرْآةُ الشَّفَّافَةُ

Marjana appears between the glass walls, a lantern in one hand and the look of someone who has already read him. She gives him a small mirror of transparent glass that reflects nothing.

Marjana: You will not need it to see your face. You carry the mirror inside you.

2 / 3 Under the Beds تَحْتَ الْأَسِرَّةِ

In a corner, a family hides beneath a table, staring into a mirror that shows them "what is worst."

The Mother: Look. So you will know what the worst is.

The Father: What if I see that I am weak?

The Mother: Then you will know where it lives.

The father looks, sees his tired face, sighs, sets the mirror aside.

The Father: Come out, all of you.

2 / 4 The Departure الرَّحِيلُ

Sindbad and Juha on the donkey, the transparent mirror in his pocket.

Juha: Are you happy?

Sindbad: I am not happy. But I am no longer afraid.

On the horizon: black smoke, and screaming.

Juha: The Isle of Bounty. Its name suggests generosity… but its people are the stingiest of God's creation.

صَوْتُ الْجَائِعِ: «سَآكُلُكَ… قَرِيبًا.»

Voice of the Hungry One: "I will eat you… soon."

The Isle of Bounty · a scent of generosity, a scream behind the granaries

جَزِيرَةُ الْجُودِ

3 / 1 The Scent of Bounty, and the Screaming رَائِحَةُ الْجُودِ وَالصُّرَاخُ

Plate · Ashʻab, puffed with stolen air

"Bounty never stops!" Ashʻab screams, while the workers' backs bend beneath their loads.

«الْجُودُ لَا يَتَوَقَّفُ!» يَصْرُخُ أَشْعَبُ، وَالْعُمَّالُ تَحْنِي ظُهُورَهُمُ الْأَحْمَالُ

A scent reaches the sea before the island does: a perfume of bread and honey and ripe fruit, sweet and heavy to the point of nausea. But beneath it runs a sour thread, the smell of something rotting in silence. And from the land rises a rhythmic screaming, neither swelling nor fading, as though it were part of the place's pulse. Sindbad listened, and felt his shoulders tighten without being ordered to.

Sindbad: That screaming is not fear. It is surrender.

And he was right; fear trembles, but this was level as a drawn wire. They leapt down into sprawling fields where the workers' backs were bent like bows that had forgotten how to straighten, their feet sinking into heavy mud. In their midst stood Ashʻab, puffed up like a full waterskin, screaming into the face of an exhausted worker: "Bounty never stops!"

Ashʻab: I feed the hungry and clothe the naked, and they serve me of their own free will.

He said it while licking his fingers, the whip dangling from his wrist as though it were a natural extension of his hand.

Juha: The just man needs no whip.

Juha said it quietly, then bit into his apple. And Sindbad felt something losing its balance behind his ribs: the name says "Bounty," and the scene says its exact opposite, which pan of the scale should he believe?

3 / 2 The Granaries of Bounty مَخَازِنُ الْجُودِ

Ashʻab led them, boasting, to his granaries, certain they would be dazzled. He pushed the door open, and a suffocating wave of rot burst out: food heaped to the ceiling, half of it green with mold; moth-eaten, threadbare clothes, all of it hoarded for a day that never comes.

Ashʻab: I feed the hungry… tomorrow. I clothe the naked… the day after.

Sindbad: This is greed dressed up as generosity.

Ashʻab could not bear the weight of the word. He shoved Sindbad into a dark room at the back of the granary and slammed the door with a heavy bolt. The darkness swallowed him whole. And there, where the eye can see nothing, he heard a soft voice coming from every direction and from inside him at once, his own voice, from when he was a small child.

The Child's Voice: Why do you take and never give?

He froze where he stood. The dark resembled the dark of another room in his memory, from a day when he himself had hoarded what should have been shared. He shuddered. He stretched out his hands, feeling along the walls for a way out, but his palms met nothing except solid wood. So he began to strike, breaking the doors with his bare hands, plank after plank, until the skin of his palms split and blood ran warm down his wrists, and he did not stop.

The Silence of the Question

تِسْعُ ثَوَانٍ · Nine seconds

نَفَسَانِ… · Two breaths… then notice

When his child-self asked him, "Why do you take and never give?", where did the question grip you in your body? Did a small fist clench in your chest or your belly? Do not name it; only touch the place where it lives.

حِينَ سَأَلَهُ طِفْلُهُ: «لِمَاذَا تَأْخُذُ وَلَا تُعْطِي؟»، أَيْنَ أَمْسَكَكَ السُّؤَالُ فِي جَسَدِكَ؟ هَلِ انْقَبَضَتْ قَبْضَةٌ صَغِيرَةٌ فِي صَدْرِكَ أَوْ بَطْنِكَ؟ لَا تُسَمِّهَا؛ فَقَطِ الْمِسْ مَوْضِعَهَا.

It clenches يَنْقَبِضُ

3 / 3 The Fall of False Bounty سُقُوطُ الْجُودِ الْمُزَيَّفِ

The door finally opened, or broke. Sindbad emerged with bleeding palms and found Ashʻab waiting for him, trembling with rage.

Ashʻab: You have destroyed my treasures!

Sindbad: They are not your treasures. They are what these people grew with their own hands.

The word fell like a stone into a deep well. Ashʻab began to wither before his eyes, shrinking like a waterskin leaking its air, until he was an ordinary thin man with nothing frightening left in him.

Ashʻab: Where is my bounty? I… I am hungry.

Juha tossed an apple toward him. Ashʻab reached out, but his fingers, which had learned nothing but taking, did not know how to catch. The apple fell to the ground. A worker picked it up, calmly split it in two, and handed half to the worker beside him. It was the first true act of bounty the island had seen in an age.

An Old Man (singing) : O sailor of God… return, renew the covenant of the forefathers… bounty has no bounds…

No one took up the song. But the workers raised their heads a little and exchanged glances, as though remembering that they had necks that could straighten. Sindbad looked at his bleeding hands, then lifted his gaze to the horizon, where an island lay wrapped in grey fog.

Juha: The Isle of Peace. But the war there never ends.

صَوْتُ الْجَائِعِ: «سَأَجْمَعُ كُلَّ قَطْرَةِ دَمٍ… الْحَرْبُ أَلَذُّ طَعَامِي.»

Voice of the Hungry One: "I will gather every drop of blood… war is my most delicious food."

The Isle of Peace · a wall of names, a song that stops the swords

جَزِيرَةُ السَّلَامِ

4 / 1 A Fog That Does Not Resemble Peace ضَبَابٌ لَا يُشْبِهُ السَّلَامَ

Plate · The wall of the dead

A long wall filled with names, and two armies screaming in the fog as though it were a dance.

جِدَارٌ طَوِيلٌ مَلِيءٌ بِالْأَسْمَاءِ، وَجَيْشَانِ يَصْرُخَانِ فِي الضَّبَابِ كَأَنَّهَا رَقْصَةٌ

A grey sea, and a fog so thick it swallows every boundary, until Sindbad cannot tell where the water ends and the land begins; he steps and his foot betrays him, and his head turns one light circle, like a man who has lost the horizon he steadies himself by. The sand is rough as ash beneath his feet. From inside the fog comes the singing of children, thin and trembling: "O Isle of Peace, what have you done to us? Our war begins every day, and no one knows why."

The fog parts slightly around a long wall filled with names, all of them recent.

Juha: The wall of the dead. The children come to sing for grandfathers they do not remember.

The Soldier: You are a stranger. Therefore you are a spy.

Sindbad: A spy for whom?

The Soldier: I don't remember… but my father said the enemy deserves to be killed.

4 / 2 The Camp of Forgetting مُخَيَّمُ النِّسْيَانِ

Sindbad: Why do you fight?

The Commander: No one knows. For a hundred years we have fought every stranger. The forefathers said so.

Juha: Fear of the forefathers is stronger than fear of death.

Sindbad: Then I will be the stranger.

4 / 3 The Absurd Battle الْمَعْرَكَةُ السَّخِيفَةُ

On a field of trampled ash the two armies shout at each other in step, left foot, right foot, like dancers who have forgotten the music. Sindbad walks into the gap between them.

Sindbad: Do you weep over dead you never knew? An army marching with no banner, fighting with no enemy.

A child begins to sing; a whisper spreads; soldiers drop their swords.

A Soldier: There was a woman… she sang this song… she was my mother…

Sindbad: The forefathers died, and their last wish was peace. But you forgot.

The commander looks at him a long while, then weeps for the first time in decades.

4 / 4 The Price of Peace ثَمَنُ السَّلَامِ

A Young Soldier: Will the fear end?

Sindbad: No. But now you know that it is fear. That is the difference.

Juha: The Isle of Heroes. Its statues weep.

Sindbad: Why do the heroes weep?

Juha: Because they believed immortality was better than living.

صَوْتُ الْجَائِعِ: «هَلْ تَحْزَنُ عَلَى الَّذِينَ مَاتُوا؟ أَنَا أَيْضًا أَحْزَنُ.»

Voice of the Hungry One: "Do you grieve for those who died? I grieve too."

The Isle of Heroes · marble that does not age, a chisel that hunts the living

جَزِيرَةُ الْأَبْطَالِ

5 / 1 An Island of Marble جَزِيرَةٌ مِنَ الرُّخَامِ

Plate · The chisel that freezes

"You are excellent raw material. I shall immortalize you", while the statues weep petrified tears.

«أَنْتَ مَادَّةٌ خَامٌ مُمْتَازَةٌ. سَأُخَلِّدُكَ»، وَالتَّمَاثِيلُ تَبْكِي دُمُوعًا مُتَحَجِّرَةً

A calm sea, and an island of marble statues. A colossal statue of a man spearing a dragon, and a guide shouting: "The greatest hero!"

A teenage boy laughs, then stops. He looks into the statue's eyes, closes his own and opens them, and grabs Sindbad's sleeve.

The Teenager: Take it… I don't want to become like him.

Sindbad: What did you see?

The Teenager: I saw… myself. Standing here. Not moving. Ever.

The old sculptor appears, his eyes a cold blue.

The Old Man: You are excellent raw material. I shall immortalize you.

He seizes the teenager, and Sindbad wrenches him free: "Run." They run, and the statues shatter behind them.

5 / 2 The Painter Girl الْفَتَاةُ الرَّسَّامَةُ

A neglected quarter, statues overgrown with moss, and a girl painting sorrowful faces.

The Girl: The sculptor hunts me because my brush undoes his chisel: every time he freezes someone, I paint him breathing again.

She gives the teenager a drawing of a smiling woman: "My mother. She was a hero to me. Keep her safe." Sindbad takes out an old drawing of his own mother, and holds the two together.

5 / 3 True Immortality الْخُلُودُ الْحَقِيقِيُّ

Frozen dancing statues, and a plaque: "The Immortal Heroes of the Dance." A statue of a little girl, her tears turned to stone.

The Old Man: There is no escape. Everyone petrifies in the end.

The girl does not argue with him. She steps up to the statue of the dancing child and paints a blush onto her marble cheek, and a spark into her eye. The child trembles, shakes off the dust, and laughs a laugh that fills the whole place, the first living sound on the island in ages.

The Girl: No one is immortalized by stopping. I paint them while they breathe, not while they stand still.

Sindbad: And I choose to live: to grieve, to err, and to change.

The old man raises his chisel toward the girl, and it is his own hand that petrifies, and the chisel drops from a fist that has turned to marble.

5 / 4 The Statue of the Mother تِمْثَالُ الْأُمِّ

A small statue, inscribed beneath: "To the Unknown Mother." Sindbad lays the drawing of his mother beside the pedestal and whispers: "I have not forgotten."

Juha: That is the Isle of Freedom. Its name suggests liberty… but its people hide from choosing.

Sindbad: Why do you never ask me about my mother?

Juha: Because you never asked me about me.

صَوْتُ الْجَائِعِ: «أَنَا أَجْمَعُ دُمُوعَ مَنْ يَعْتَقِدُونَ أَنَّهُمْ لَا يَمُوتُونَ.»

Voice of the Hungry One: "I gather the tears of those who believe they do not die."

The Isle of Freedom · no walls, and windows barred from within

جَزِيرَةُ الْحُرِّيَّةِ

6 / 1 An Island Without Prisons جَزِيرَةٌ بِلَا سُجُونٍ

Plate · A shelter for those afraid of choices

An open island without walls, and a family hiding behind a window from "Freedom" itself.

جَزِيرَةٌ مَفْتُوحَةٌ بِلَا أَسْوَارٍ، وَعَائِلَةٌ تَخْتَبِئُ خَلْفَ نَافِذَةٍ مِنَ «الْحُرِّيَّةِ» نَفْسِهَا

An open island, no walls and no ramparts, empty markets and not a soul. A whisper from a window. Sindbad opens it, and finds a family hiding beneath a table.

The Father: Get out… don't draw their attention.

Sindbad: Whose?

The Father: Freedom is watching us. If it learns we are here… it will force us to come out.

6 / 2 The Shelter of the Fearful مَلْجَأُ الْخَائِفِينَ

A sign: "Refuge for Those Afraid of Decisions." A young man, his shirt reading: "Don't ask me, I only follow."

Sindbad: Isn't the fear killing you in here?

Juha: If you are all so happy, why are you hiding?

The Young Man: We are afraid of discovering that we are not happy.

6 / 3 The Rule of Freedom قَاعِدَةُ الْحُرِّيَّةِ

A sign: "The Rule of Freedom, here we protect you from your decisions." A fat governor smiles and hands him the list of permitted choices: "Live safe, take no risks, choose what we choose for you."

Sindbad: This is a soft prison.

The Governor: People do not know what they want.

Sindbad: And who said that safety is a destination?

Juha rolls up the list and folds it into a paper hat.

6 / 4 The Seed of Choosing بَذْرَةُ الِاخْتِيَارِ

Sindbad stands in the square.

Sindbad: I will not tell you what you must do. I will simply leave. Whoever wants to follow me, let him.

He walks, without looking back. A step. One person. Then another. Then a little girl.

The Little Girl: Will it hurt if you choose a wrong road?

Sindbad: Yes. But the pain will teach me.

Juha: You will not free all the people.

Sindbad: It is enough for me that two walked behind me.

صَوْتُ الْجَائِعِ: «الْحُرِّيَّةُ طَعْمُهَا مُرٌّ. سَأَتْرُكُكَ لَهَا الْيَوْمَ. غَدًا… سَآكُلُ قَرَارَاتِكَ.»

Voice of the Hungry One: "Freedom tastes bitter. I will leave you to it today. Tomorrow… I will eat your decisions."

The Isle of Wisdom · a thousand books titled Truth, their pages blank

جَزِيرَةُ الْحِكْمَةِ

7 / 1 A Library Where No One Reads مَكْتَبَةٌ لَا يُقْرَأُ فِيهَا

Plate · The first sage, bound by fear of error

The statue of the first sage, eyes closed and hands bound; he knew everything, and his fear of being wrong imprisoned him.

تِمْثَالُ أَوَّلِ حَكِيمٍ، عَيْنَاهُ مُغْمَضَتَانِ وَيَدَاهُ مُقَيَّدَتَانِ؛ عَرَفَ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ فَسَجَنَهُ خَوْفُهُ مِنَ الْخَطَإِ

A calm sea and a pyramid-shaped island. At the entrance, the statue of a bald man, his eyes closed and his hands bound.

Juha: The first sage. He knew everything, and his fear of being wrong imprisoned him.

A chaotic library, sages weeping, and a teenage boy carrying an apple.

The Teenager: Are you a sage?

Sindbad: No. I am searching.

Juha takes the apple and eats it: "Answers are usually a stolen apple."

Al-Khwarizmi: Do you want wisdom?

Sindbad: I want to understand.

7 / 2 The Room of Answers غُرْفَةُ الْأَجْوِبَةِ

A round room, its walls mirrors, holding a thousand books all titled "The Complete Truth." Sindbad opens one, and its pages are blank.

Al-Khwarizmi: Close your eyes.

He closes them, and sees his own memories. He opens his eyes, and his nose is bleeding.

Al-Khwarizmi: Truth is a weapon.

The Teenager: I am not ready. But I want to learn.

7 / 3 The Inverted Pyramid الْهَرَمُ الْمَقْلُوبُ

A spiral staircase winds down into the pyramid's point, as though the whole building stood on its tip, and at the bottom waits an ancient well. Sindbad sees two futures: an old man alone, and himself, laughing with Juha.

Sindbad: Which of them is wisdom?

Al-Khwarizmi: That is your question.

Sindbad chooses the second image, and the room shakes, the books catch fire and attack like birds of prey.

Juha: Wisdom without humility paralyzes!

7 / 4 The Breaking of the Books تَحْطِيمُ الْكُتُبِ

Sindbad tears a book apart, screaming; his nose bleeds and he does not stop. The teenager helps him, then everyone is tearing, a collective pain, screaming, and then a dance of liberation.

Al-Khwarizmi: I will not thank you. But I will not stop you.

The Teenager: Will you remember me?

Sindbad: No. You will remember yourself.

On the horizon: an enormous moon.

صَوْتُ الْجَائِعِ: «جَزِيرَةُ الْقَمَرِ… الذَّاكِرَةُ الَّتِي لَا تَمُوتُ. سَأَنْتَظِرُكَ.»

Voice of the Hungry One: "The Isle of the Moon… the memory that does not die. I will be waiting for you."

The Moon · where longing is stronger than truth

الْقَمَرُ

8 / 1 A Sea That Remembers بَحْرٌ يَتَذَكَّرُ

Plate · The moon reflects memory

A night sea, its surface smooth as a black mirror, and on the moon's rim two eyes that wait.

بَحْرٌ لَيْلِيٌّ سَطْحُهُ أَمْلَسُ كَمِرْآةٍ سَوْدَاءَ، وَعَلَى حَافَّةِ الْقَمَرِ عَيْنَانِ تَنْتَظِرَانِ

The sea has gone black and still. An enormous moon hangs over it, and its light lies on the water like a road toward somewhere Sindbad already knows.

Sindbad: The moon… my father used to say it is the earth's mirror.

Juha: Your father was a poet.

Sindbad takes out a drawing: a woman smiling, a man walking away. The moon flares, and a far-off song drifts across the water: "O sailor of God, return…"

Sindbad: Mother…

Juha: The moon reflects memory. Beware, longing is stronger than truth.

8 / 2 The Descent الْهُبُوطُ

The moon is a white land: an abandoned city, and drawings on the walls. The voice of Sindbad's mother: "Do not be afraid. The sea is vast, but you are greater than your fear." Sindbad weeps, and Juha looks away into the distance.

8 / 3 The Cave of Memory كَهْفُ الذَّاكِرَةِ

A cave whose walls are mirrors. Sindbad sees himself as an old man, and Juha sees himself laughing alone. A statue of a weeping child stands beneath a frozen light.

Juha: These are the children whose futures the Hungry One devoured; they stopped dreaming.

Sindbad touches the statue and feels the cold.

Sindbad: I never stopped.

The statue melts. The ground shudders. Juha: "Run!"

8 / 4 The Apparition, and the Ending الظُّهُورُ وَالْخَاتِمَةُ

On the moon's rim the darkness thickens, and two red eyes open. The Hungry One appears: not a monster, but Sindbad himself, grown old.

The Hungry One: At last I see you. You are no hero. You are a frightened child.

Sindbad: You are nothing but a hunger that is never filled.

Sindbad takes out the broken compass, and its needle is pointing at his own chest.

Sindbad: I was searching for you in the sea… and you were here, inside me.

Voice of the Hungry One: I will taste your tomorrow…

سِنْدِبَاد: «لَقَدْ وَجَدْتُ مَا أَبْحَثُ عَنْهُ.»

Sindbad: "I have found what I was searching for."

The Silence of the Question

تِسْعُ ثَوَانٍ · Nine seconds

نَفَسَانِ… · Two breaths… then notice

When you learned that what he had been fleeing was his own face, where did the weight of your body settle? Did your feet hold firm on the floor, or did something tremble in your chest and then grow still? Notice its movement, not its meaning.

حِينَ عَرَفْتَ أَنَّ مَا كُنْتَ تَهْرُبُ مِنْهُ هُوَ وَجْهُكَ أَنْتَ، أَيْنَ اسْتَقَرَّ ثِقْلُ جَسَدِكَ؟ هَلْ ثَبَتَتْ قَدَمَاكَ عَلَى الْأَرْضِ، أَمِ ارْتَجَفَ شَيْءٌ فِي صَدْرِكَ ثُمَّ هَدَأَ؟ لَاحِظْ حَرَكَتَهُ، لَا مَعْنَاهَا.

It holds steady يَثْبُتُ

The Isle of Return · an open house, and a teapot waiting

جَزِيرَةُ الْعَوْدَةِ

9 / 1 The Sea That Does Not End الْبَحْرُ الَّذِي لَا يَنْتَهِي

Plate · The house that was never haunted

An island, a small house, a sandy shore, a lone palm tree, the smell of bread.

جَزِيرَةٌ، بَيْتٌ صَغِيرٌ، شَاطِئٌ رَمْلِيٌّ، نَخْلَةٌ وَحِيدَةٌ، رَائِحَةُ خُبْزٍ

A sea without an edge, grey water.

Juha: The compass points inside you. The journey is over. Or it has not begun.

Sindbad: The sea does not end.

Juha: Because you have not yet returned.

Then the grey thins. A small island rises out of it: a sandy shore, one palm, a low house with its door ajar, and the smell of bread on the air.

Sindbad: My island…

Juha: Its truth is not what you think.

9 / 2 The House That Was Never Haunted الْبَيْتُ الَّذِي لَمْ يَكُنْ مَسْكُونًا

The sands are warm and every step leaves a deep print. The house stands open, and the teapot is cold as ice.

Juha: The island wants to make you feel a false safety.

The shadow of a woman smiles; the shadow of a man watches. A small room, a child's bed, a drawing on the wall. He reaches out his hand, and it turns to dust. Sindbad weeps, and Juha lays a hand on his shoulder.

Sindbad: I was searching for the one who left, and I believed that those who stayed were not enough.

Silence.

Sindbad: But they are sufficient.

He felt a weight descend from his throat into his chest, then settle, like a stone that has found its resting place at last.

9 / 3 Zaynab Returns زَيْنَبُ تَعُودُ

Zaynab appears from behind the palm tree, carrying the mask.

Zaynab: You came back. And I have not slept, not waiting for you, but because I became the one who wakes the island. My father died, and the people fell asleep again, so I took the mask and sat beside every sleeper until he remembered how to wake.

Sindbad: Alone?

Zaynab: Not everyone who stays is waiting for someone's return; some of us stay to work. And was arriving ever the answer?

Sindbad: No. The answer is to ask another question.

9 / 4 The Meeting with the Hungry One, and the Departure لِقَاءُ الْجَائِعِ وَالْخُرُوجُ

The air turns red, and the eyes of the Hungry One appear.

The Hungry One: I gathered every pain of this voyage into my hunger, but you have ruined my appetite. You kept refusing to become me.

Sindbad: And now?

The Hungry One: You are not hungry, and that puts me to sleep. But my sleep is short.

He vanishes. Zaynab waves the mask, and Sindbad and Juha move away.

Juha: If you find what you are searching for… you will wish you had never searched.

A white screen; a slow darkening; the sound of a mirror breaking; an apple falling; and Juha's laughter.

سِنْدِبَاد: «لَكِنِّي سَأَبْحَثُ مُجَدَّدًا… لِأَنَّ الْبَحْثَ هُوَ أَنَا.»

Sindbad: "But I will search again… because the search is who I am."

A Whisper to the Companion

هَمْسٌ لِلْمُرَافِقِ

Read after, never during.

This page is for the teacher, the parent, or the companion, not for the young reader. It is read after the journey, not in its midst; the text is sufficient unto itself, and the body reads before the mind.

The Key of Balance

The whole arc is about Balance : when is a decision courage, and when is it escape? The compass that points outward lies; only the broken one points inward. And every island bears the name of a virtue whose reality conceals its opposite, so the first lesson is: "Names may be the first of lies."

Who is "the Hungry One"?

Do not unmask him before his time. The Hungry One is not an external monster but, as is revealed on the Isle of the Moon, Sindbad himself grown old: the hunger that is never filled when we search outside for what exists only within. Let the reader arrive at this alone; the surprise is part of the meaning.

Where do you touch Balance in the text?

Balance here is not merely a mental idea but a bodily sensation planted in the scenes. Guide the reader, after the reading, to revisit three moments and ask his body about each: Sindbad standing on the tilting mast with the water at his chest (1/1), where was his weight? The needle that spins and finds no north, did something spin in his own head with it? And the weight that descended from throat to chest and settled, in the Return (9/2), when did his balance finally come true? This is the epic's sense, pulsing beneath the events.

Why do the islands return?

The reader will notice that the same islands (Mirrors, Bounty, Peace…) return in the following voyages with a deeper awareness. This is intentional: "The voyages are not chapters of one continuous novel; each Safar begins the sea anew, because the sea only remembers." What repeats is the search itself, not its events. Encourage the reader to ask: what changed in me between one visit and the next?

«الْبُوصَلَةُ الْمَكْسُورَةُ وَحْدَهَا تُشِيرُ إِلَى الدَّاخِلِ.»

Only the broken compass points inward.

Courage is not to storm ahead, but to know when to stand still, and to know that names may be the first of lies.
