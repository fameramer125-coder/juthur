Translator's Threshold

Fascicle III continues the circle-by-circle English rendering of the Four Arcs from the ratified Arabic edition, Tree Stage (12+) of the Juthur project, grounded in Concrete Reading Theory (CRT; Amer, 2024). All conventions of the previous fascicles hold: proprietary terms romanized; ritual and pull lines bilingual; the author's original English subtitle lines preserved verbatim.

The pauses of this Safar are deeper and longer than in the two before it, because weight needs time to settle. Six Silence-of-the-Question stations descend and rise as an arc of Weight: it descends, stills, balances, is borne, flows, takes root. Their durations (eight to twelve seconds) and breath counts are exact. The author's gentleness note before the Floor of Guilt is preserved in place.

A Threshold · where the past does not pass

عَتَبَةٌ

After Sindbad learned that names lie, and that truth burns or warms according to the hand, his compass leads him to the deepest and slowest of lands: the Land Beyond Time, where the past does not pass, but settles on the seafloor like sunken cities waiting to be visited. Here words are of no use; the Hungry One himself said it: "I will wait for you where the heart grows so heavy it cannot speak."

The sense of this Safar is Weight , and it deepens by degrees: the weight of loss, then the weight of a time that will not pass, then the weight of guilt and regret. And a new companion appears: Sarmad , the keeper of time and memory, who broke his own hourglass so that the day of his loss would never pass, Sindbad's mirror in time: one who mastered remembering, and forgot how to live.

Weight is felt in the shoulders, the chest, and the breath. It deepens from a weight that drags you downward to a weight that becomes a root and steadies you. And gold is the gathering thread, as the root binds its branches to the earth.

The pauses here are deeper and longer than in the two Safars before, because weight needs time to settle. They invite you to notice where the weight descends in your body, and how it changes when you no longer carry it alone. Six pauses, preparing for their completion as eight in The Eternal Day .

The Golden Rule of Juthur: «لَا تَصِفْ لَهُ مَا سَيُحِسُّ بِهِ؛ دَعْهُ يُحِسَّ، ثُمَّ صِفْ مَا حَدَثَ»، Do not describe to him what he is about to feel; let him feel, then describe what happened. And Juha's thread remains: "If you find what you are searching for, you will wish you had never searched."

«الثِّقَلُ الْمُتَقَاسَمُ لَيْسَ ثِقَلًا، بَلْ جَذْرٌ.»

A weight that is shared is not a weight, but a root.

The Harbour of Anchored Ships · the weight of loss

مَرْفَأُ السُّفُنِ الرَّاسِيَةِ

1 / 1 A Sea That Does Not Move بَحْرٌ لَا يَتَحَرَّكُ

Plate · They arrived, then turned back

A sea still as quicksilver, the ships at anchor, and every passenger gazing backward at a point on the horizon.

بَحْرٌ سَاكِنٌ كَالزِّئْبَقِ، وَالسُّفُنُ رَاسِيَةٌ، وَكُلُّ رَاكِبٍ يَنْظُرُ إِلَى الْوَرَاءِ نَحْوَ نُقْطَةٍ فِي الْأُفُقِ

The boat enters a sea that does not breathe: water heavy as quicksilver, smooth as a sheet of lead, without a wave and without a sound, as though the sea had held its breath an age ago and forgotten to release it. The ships lie at anchor without motion, their sails slack like shoulders grown tired of waiting; and on every ship there are people gazing backward, at a point on the horizon where there is nothing, nothing except what they lost. The boat itself grows heavy beneath the two of them, and Sindbad finds his shoulders bowing though he carries nothing, as though the air here had weight.

Juha: Here no one sails. All of them arrived, then they turned to look back, and never looked forward again.

1 / 2 Sarmad, the Keeper of Time سَرْمَد، حَارِسُ الزَّمَنِ

On a stone quay sits an old man, in his hand a broken hourglass, the sand inside it suspended mid-fall, never completing its descent.

Sarmad: I am Sarmad. I guard what has passed. I remember every face that departed, every word that was never said, even what has not yet happened. I broke my hourglass myself, so that the day I lost the one I love would never pass. And since that day, nothing passes.

1 / 3 The Mother's Image Floats صُورَةُ الْأُمِّ تَطْفُو

In the still water, images from Sindbad's past float up. He sees his mother on a quay, waving to him, drawing away. He stretches his hand into the water to seize the image, and his arm grows so heavy he can barely lift it. The weight descends from his shoulder into his chest.

Sarmad: You could stay. Many have stayed. You gaze at the image forever, and you never lose it again.

Sindbad: But I would never live, either.

The Silence of the Question

نَفَسَانِ… · Two breaths… then notice

When you imagined a face you have lost waving to you and then drawing away, where did the weight descend in your body? Onto the shoulders? Into the lower chest? Into the eyelids? Notice its place; do not name it.

حِينَ تَخَيَّلْتَ وَجْهًا فَقَدْتَهُ يَلُوحُ لَكَ ثُمَّ يَبْتَعِدُ، أَيْنَ نَزَلَ الثِّقَلُ فِي جَسَدِكَ؟ عَلَى الْكَتِفَيْنِ؟ فِي أَسْفَلِ الصَّدْرِ؟ فِي الْجَفْنَيْنِ؟ لَاحِظْ مَكَانَهُ، لَا تُسَمِّهِ.

It descends يَنْزِلُ

The Sunken City · the time that will not pass

الْمَدِينَةُ الْغَارِقَةُ

2 / 1 The Descent to the Floor النُّزُولُ إِلَى الْقَاعِ

With Sarmad they dive down to a city beneath the water, whole, lit by a cold blue light. The clocks in every tower are stopped at the same moment. The people there move with terrible slowness, repeating the same instant over and over: a farewell that never completes, a hand held out that never arrives.

Juha: The city of "If only." All of them live inside a sentence that begins with "If only I had…" The past here is not a memory, it is a prison.

2 / 2 A Man at a Bolted Door رَجُلٌ عِنْدَ بَابٍ مُوصَدٍ

A man has been knocking at a door for an age, his hand raised and frozen in the air a hair's breadth from the wood, it has neither knocked and finished, nor come down and rested. The dust on his shoulders has become a layer like a garment, and still his fingers are taut, as though a single moment had swallowed his whole life.

The Man: Behind this door I said a cruel word to my brother before he departed. I knock so that I may apologize. But the door does not open, because there is no one there anymore.

Sindbad: An apology that cannot reach its owner… where does it go?

Sarmad: To the one who says it. An apology frees the speaker first.

Sindbad whispers something to the man, and the raised hand comes down at last, and the man sits, and weeps: the first new movement in the city in an age.

The Silence of the Question

تِسْعُ ثَوَانٍ · Nine seconds

نَفَسَانِ… · Two breaths… then notice

A moment from your past that you wish you could change, when you summon it, does something in your body freeze, or move? Stay with the stillness a moment without fleeing it.

لَحْظَةٌ مِنْ مَاضِيكَ تَتَمَنَّى لَوْ غَيَّرْتَهَا، حِينَ تَسْتَحْضِرُهَا، هَلْ يَتَجَمَّدُ شَيْءٌ فِي جَسَدِكَ أَمْ يَتَحَرَّكُ؟ ابْقَ مَعَ السُّكُونِ لَحْظَةً دُونَ أَنْ تَهْرُبَ مِنْهُ.

It stills يَرْكُدُ

The Library of Ages · time is a sea, not a line

مَكْتَبَةُ الْأَزْمِنَةِ

3 / 1 A Library Beneath the Galaxies مَكْتَبَةٌ تَحْتَ الْمَجَرَّاتِ

Plate · Shelves not of books, but of moments

A hall whose ceiling opens onto turning galaxies, and whose shelves hold not books, but moments.

قَاعَةٌ سَقْفُهَا مَفْتُوحٌ عَلَى مَجَرَّاتٍ تَدُورُ، وَرُفُوفُهَا تَحْمِلُ لَا كُتُبًا بَلْ لَحَظَاتٍ

A vast hall whose ceiling opens onto turning galaxies, its shelves holding not books but moments. There sits an ancient sage, one of the old sages of al-Andalus, reading in a book whose pages are blank, filling whenever he contemplates, and emptying when he lifts his gaze.

The Sage: Time is not a line, boy, it is a sea. The past does not pass; it sinks. And wisdom is not to forget, but to know which past you carry with you, and which past you allow to settle on the floor.

3 / 2 Sindbad's Choice اخْتِيَارُ سِنْدِبَادَ

The sage gives Sindbad two moments from his shelf: the moment of his mother's farewell, and the moment of a laugh with her that he had not remembered in years.

The Sage: Take one to carry, and leave the other. Which of the two makes you live?

Sindbad: I will leave neither. I will carry the farewell so I never forget that I loved, and I will carry the laugh so I never forget that I lived. The same weight becomes lighter when it is not the weight of loss alone, but the weight of love as well.

The Sage: The first visitor who chooses to carry instead of to forget. Go, the floor awaits you.

The Silence of the Question

عَشْرُ ثَوَانٍ · Ten seconds

نَفَسَانِ… · Two breaths… then notice

A sweet memory and a painful memory of the same person, carry them together in your chest for a moment. Does the weight of the sorrow change when you carry it beside the joy? Notice the difference; do not explain it.

ذِكْرَى حُلْوَةٌ وَذِكْرَى مُؤْلِمَةٌ مِنَ الشَّخْصِ نَفْسِهِ، احْمِلْهُمَا مَعًا فِي صَدْرِكَ لِلَحْظَةٍ. هَلْ يَتَغَيَّرُ ثِقَلُ الْحُزْنِ حِينَ تَحْمِلُهُ بِجِوَارِ الْفَرَحِ؟ لَاحِظِ الْفَرْقَ، لَا تُفَسِّرْهُ.

It balances يَتَوَازَنُ

The Floor of Guilt · where the heart is too heavy to speak

قَاعُ الذُّنُوبِ

4 / 1 Where the Heart Is Too Heavy to Speak حَيْثُ يَثْقُلُ الْقَلْبُ فَلَا يَتَكَلَّمُ

Plate · Each carries a stone the size of a regret

A heavy blue darkness, silent; every human being carries a stone on his chest the size of his guilt.

ظَلَامٌ أَزْرَقُ ثَقِيلٌ صَامِتٌ، كُلُّ إِنْسَانٍ يَحْمِلُ حَجَرًا عَلَى صَدْرِهِ بِحَجْمِ ذَنْبِهِ

The deepest point of the floor: a heavy blue darkness, silent. Here no one speaks; every human being carries a stone on his chest the size of his guilt. And in the middle waits the Hungry One, but here he does not whisper words; it is enough for him to add stone upon stone.

Voice of the Hungry One (heavy, slow) : Here I do not need to say anything. Guilt does my work. Every regret you carry alone becomes a stone, and I pile up the stones.

4 / 2 Sindbad's Stone حَجَرُ سِنْدِبَادَ

Sindbad feels a stone weighing on his chest, growing larger the closer he comes. And when he looks at it, he knows it: his old guilt. He was not with his mother when she departed. And more bitter than that: he was angry with her on the last day, and did not say goodbye, believing there was room enough in tomorrow.

He bows beneath the stone. He does not try to lift it, and he does not try to justify. He lets it weigh. His knees sink to the floor, and he stays there a long while, in a darkness with no sound but his own heavy breathing. This stone will not vanish no matter what he does; some wrongs cannot be set right, because the one we wronged is gone. This is the first thing he learned at the floor: to sit with a weight that has no solution.

And after a long silence, Sarmad draws near. Not to take the stone, no one can take another's stone, even if he wished to, but to slide his palm slowly beneath Sindbad's hand, so that they carry the stone together.

Sarmad: I cannot lift it from you. I will not lie to you and say it will fade. But I can carry it with you. This is all the living have for one another: not to remove the weight, but never to leave you under it alone.

Sindbad: I thought I had to either carry it alone until I broke, or cast it off and forget that I loved her. But there is a third way… to carry it, and not carry it alone.

The stone does not vanish. Its weight is its weight. But Sindbad's back straightens a little beneath it, not because the stone grew lighter, but because another hand has come beneath it.

(إِنْ كَانَ هَذَا الْمَوْضِعُ ثَقِيلًا عَلَيْكَ، فَلَكَ أَنْ تَمُرَّ بِهِ بِرِفْقٍ، أَوْ تَقْرَأَهُ مَعَ مَنْ تَثِقُ بِهِ.)

(If this passage weighs on you, you may pass through it gently, or read it with someone you trust.)

The Silence of the Question

إِحْدَى عَشْرَةَ ثَانِيَةً · Eleven seconds

نَفَسَانِ… · Two breaths… then notice

A heavy load you carried alone, and a load you carried with a hand beneath your hand, where is the difference: in your back, in your shoulders, in your breath? Notice how the weight itself changes when you are not alone.

حِمْلٌ ثَقِيلٌ حَمَلْتَهُ وَحْدَكَ، وَحِمْلٌ حَمَلْتَهُ وَيَدٌ تَحْتَ يَدِكَ، أَيْنَ الْفَرْقُ فِي ظَهْرِكَ، فِي كَتِفَيْكَ، فِي نَفَسِكَ؟ لَاحِظْ كَيْفَ يَتَغَيَّرُ الثِّقَلُ نَفْسُهُ حِينَ لَا تَكُونُ وَحْدَكَ.

It is borne يُحْتَمَلُ

Sarmad and the Broken Hourglass · the keeper revealed

سَرْمَد وَالسَّاعَةُ الْمَكْسُورَةُ

5 / 1 Why Time Stopped لِمَاذَا تَوَقَّفَ الزَّمَنُ

Plate · The sand begins to fall again

The broken hourglass is turned over, and the sand begins to run again, not to forget, but to live.

السَّاعَةُ الْمَكْسُورَةُ تُقْلَبُ، فَيَبْدَأُ الرَّمْلُ يَجْرِي مِنْ جَدِيدٍ، لَا لِيَنْسَى، بَلْ لِيَعِيشَ

Sindbad follows Sarmad to a room at the floor, and discovers his secret: Sarmad lost his daughter, and broke his hourglass so the day would not pass, so he could stay close to the last day he saw her. Since then he has guarded everyone's memories, so as never to live his own.

Sarmad: I mastered remembering because I was afraid to live. To live means that the day passes; and for the day to pass means I move one step further from her. So I stopped time. But by that… I did not keep her. I buried myself with her.

5 / 2 Sindbad Mends the Hourglass سِنْدِبَادُ يُصْلِحُ السَّاعَةَ

Sindbad takes the broken hourglass. He does not mend it so that Sarmad will forget, he turns it over, and the sand begins to run again, slowly.

Sindbad: You will not forget her when the sand runs. You will carry her with you while you live. She does not want to stay inside one day; she wants to walk with you through all the days.

Sarmad: Every visitor asked me to show him the past. You are the first to give me back tomorrow.

The Silence of the Question

When you imagined stopped sand beginning to run again, did something move in your chest, or did you breathe deeper? Notice the movement, not its meaning.

حِينَ تَخَيَّلْتَ رَمْلًا مُتَوَقِّفًا يَبْدَأُ يَجْرِي مِنْ جَدِيدٍ، هَلْ تَحَرَّكَ شَيْءٌ فِي صَدْرِكَ أَمْ تَنَفَّسْتَ أَعْمَقَ؟ لَاحِظِ الْحَرَكَةَ، لَا مَعْنَاهَا.

The Ascent · a shared weight becomes a root

الصُّعُودُ

6 / 1 Facing the Hungry One in Silence مُوَاجَهَةُ الْجَائِعِ فِي الصَّمْتِ

Plate · A river of light, far above

They rise toward a light that never goes out, and the anchored ships begin to move, one after another.

يَصْعَدُونَ نَحْوَ ضَوْءٍ لَا يَنْطَفِئُ، وَالسُّفُنُ الرَّاسِيَةُ تَبْدَأُ تَتَحَرَّكُ وَاحِدَةً تِلْوَ الْأُخْرَى

Sindbad returns to the Floor of Guilt, where the Hungry One waits. He does not argue with him in words, he has learned that speech is of no use here. He sits beside him, and carries his stone before him quietly, neither breaking beneath it nor casting it off.

Voice of the Hungry One: Why do you not break? Everyone who carried his stone has broken.

Sindbad: Because I do not carry it alone. Sarmad carries it with me, and Juha, and the memory of my mother, which has begun to shine instead of to weigh. A weight that is shared is not a weight, but a root.

The Hungry One grows smaller, not to disappear, but to grow quiet. For whoever carries his weight with companionship is no longer food for him.

And Sindbad thought, watching the small Hungry One: "I have not defeated you, and I never will. You sleep when I do not let you eat my loneliness, and you wake when I forget to ask for a hand. You are not an enemy I kill, but a hunger I feed with companionship whenever it returns."

6 / 2 The Ascent, and the Far River of Light الصُّعُودُ وَنَهْرُ الضَّوْءِ الْبَعِيدُ

Sindbad, Sarmad, and Juha rise from the floor. The anchored ships begin to move, one after another, each time a passenger sees that he can carry his past and still set sail. On the horizon glimmers a strange light, one that never goes out. A perpetual day.

Sarmad: I stay here, but from now on I will guard tomorrow, not yesterday. Go. There, where night never comes, the last of what awaits you.

Juha: The land of the Eternal Day. There the name does not lie, the mirror does not burn, and loss does not weigh… there, there is only a hand reaching toward a glow, and you must learn when to hold it back.

صَوْتُ الْجَائِعِ (هَامِسًا، أَخِيرًا): «سَأَنْتَظِرُكَ فِي النُّورِ. هُنَاكَ لَنْ أَحْتَاجَ ثِقَلًا وَلَا اسْمًا… يَكْفِي أَنْ تَنْحَنِيَ قَلِيلًا.»

Voice of the Hungry One (whispering, at last): "I will wait for you in the light. There I will need no weight and no name… it is enough that you bend a little."

The Silence of the Question

اثْنَتَا عَشْرَةَ ثَانِيَةً · Twelve seconds

نَفَسَانِ… · Two breaths… then notice

A weight you carried a long time, then you imagined a hand carrying it with you. What happened to your back? And now, as you rise from the floor toward a light, where does your body grow light first? Stay in its lightness a moment.

ثِقَلٌ حَمَلْتَهُ طَوِيلًا، ثُمَّ تَخَيَّلْتَ يَدًا تَحْمِلُهُ مَعَكَ، مَاذَا حَدَثَ لِظَهْرِكَ؟ وَالْآنَ، وَأَنْتَ تَصْعَدُ مِنَ الْقَاعِ نَحْوَ ضَوْءٍ، أَيْنَ يَخِفُّ جَسَدُكَ أَوَّلًا؟ ابْقَ فِي خِفَّتِهِ لَحْظَةً.

It takes root يَرْسَخُ

A Whisper to the Companion

هَمْسٌ لِلْمُرَافِقِ

Read after, never during.

This page is for the teacher, the parent, or the companion, not for the young reader. It is read after the journey, not in its midst; the text is sufficient unto itself, and the body reads before the mind.

The Key of Weight

The whole arc is about Weight as a bodily experience of loss, of time, and of guilt. The gathering lesson: "Weight is carried with companionship, not cast off." Whoever casts off his weight loses his roots (he forgets that he loved); whoever carries it alone breaks; and whoever carries it with a companion, his back straightens. A weight that is shared becomes a root.

The Gradation of the Seals

Six pauses, deeper and longer than in the two Safars before: it descends (loss) ← it stills (stopped time) ← it balances (love beside sorrow) ← it is borne (a hand beneath the hand) ← it flows (time returns) ← it takes root (the weight is a root). Let the reader trace the transformation of the weight in his shoulders, his chest, and his breath.

Who Is Sarmad?

Sarmad is Sindbad's mirror in time: one who mastered remembering and forgot how to live. He stopped his hourglass so as never to move a step away from his daughter, and so buried himself with her. When he slides his palm beneath Sindbad's stone (not lifting it from him, but carrying it with him), the whole lesson takes flesh: no one can take another's stone, but companionship makes it bearable. And when Sindbad mends his hourglass, he gives him back tomorrow, not yesterday.

One Question That Opens the Conversation

If the companion wishes to open a door, not to deliver a lesson, a single concrete, bodily question is enough, asked gently, and then the reader is left to answer or to stay silent: "Have you ever carried something heavy alone, and then someone came and put their hand beneath your hand? What changed in your back at that moment?" Do not correct his answer, and do not complete it; the question itself is the gift. And leave a silence after it, for the silence is part of the answer.

«الثِّقَلُ الْمُتَقَاسَمُ لَيْسَ ثِقَلًا، بَلْ جَذْرٌ.»

A weight that is shared is not a weight, but a root.

The past does not pass, it sinks; and wisdom is to know which past you carry with you, not to forget.
