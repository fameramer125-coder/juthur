Translator's Threshold

Fascicle IV completes the circle-by-circle English rendering of the Four Arcs from the ratified Arabic edition, Tree Stage (12+) of the Juthur project, grounded in Concrete Reading Theory (CRT; Amer, 2024). All conventions of Fascicles I to III hold unchanged.

This crown jewel is a single continuous epic in eight scenes rather than an archipelago, and it carries the fullest set of Silence-of-the-Question stations: eight, whose durations lengthen, as the author's threshold says, whenever the temptation grows stronger. Their body-verbs: it glows, bends, pulls, they stay, it watches, it empties, it steadies, it settles. The author's bilingual seal-verbs are his own and are preserved verbatim, as is his English rendering of Juha's thread.

Draft status: as stated in Fascicle I.

A Threshold · how this book is read with the body

عَتَبَةٌ

This is an epic read with the body before the eye. Its hero is Sindbad, a sailor who drowned seven times and learned not fear but the question, carrying a broken compass that points only inward, accompanied by Juha, who tells the truth in the shape of a joke. Sindbad sailed an archipelago where every island bears the name of a virtue whose reality conceals its opposite, searching for a "lost treasure" he believed to be a thing that exists, and it turned out to be a question that is lived.

And on every sea a shadow followed him, whom Juha calls the Hungry One : not a monster to be seen, but a hunger never filled, devouring wakefulness and presence, always whispering: "I will eat your tomorrow." And this is the last of the voyages: no island lying by its name, but a daylight that never goes out, a glow in every palm that draws the far so near it swallows the stars, and necks that bend until they forget how to raise the head.

Every epic of the "Tree" stage leans on one inner sense: Balance, Heat, Weight, and Touch. This epic's sense is Touch : a hand reaching in the dark toward a glow, and another hand that holds it back.

Here the pauses reach their fullest form: eight of them, and each one lasts longer whenever the temptation grows stronger. This is the method made flesh, not a lesson glued on.

«لَا تَصِفْ لَهُ مَا سَيُحِسُّ بِهِ؛ دَعْهُ يُحِسَّ، ثُمَّ صِفْ مَا حَدَثَ.»

Do not describe to him what he is about to feel; let him feel, then describe what happened.

The skeleton is hidden, the literature is visible, and the body reads before the mind. The endings here are therefore left open, and the meanings are left without a lock.

One thread remains, returning on Juha's tongue. You will hear it at the journey's end:

«إِذَا وَجَدْتَ مَا تَبْحَثُ عَنْهُ، سَتَتَمَنَّى أَنَّكَ مَا بَحَثْتَ.»

If you find what you are searching for, you will wish you had never searched.

Is it a warning? Or does it, when we arrive, turn into an answer? That is the journey of this book.

I The Horizon That Will Not Darken الْأُفُقُ الَّذِي لَا يُظْلِمُ

Plate · A horizon that will not darken

He waited for the first star, and the sky was a grey page, lit from below, that had swallowed every distant light.

انْتَظَرَ النَّجْمَةَ الْأُولَى، فَإِذَا السَّمَاءُ صَفْحَةٌ رَمَادِيَّةٌ مُضَاءَةٌ مِنْ تَحْتِهَا، ابْتَلَعَتْ كُلَّ ضَوْءٍ بَعِيدٍ

The sea leaned toward evening, and Sindbad waited for the night as a sailor waits for an old friend; he waited for the first star, to rest his grandfather's compass upon it. But the night did not come.

At the horizon's edge a whiteness appeared; not a dawn, but resembling dawn. It stayed there, never becoming morning, never returning to night.

Sindbad took out his grandfather's compass. The needle that had led him across every sea trembled first, then spun, then spun and found no north. He raised his head, searching for a single star to fix it by, and the sky was a smooth grey page, lit from below, as though someone had kindled an enormous lamp behind it that swallowed every distant point of light.

And beneath the hum of that light he heard a whisper, barely audible: Stay… do not look far… He turned. No one was there. And the boat drew near an island lit like high noon, in the heart of the night.

II A City That Never Lifts Its Eyes مَدِينَةٌ لَا تَرْفَعُ عَيْنَيْهَا

Plate · A city that never lifts its eyes

Every human being carries a small piece of the glow in his palm, and walks with his head bent toward it.

كُلُّ إِنْسَانٍ يَحْمِلُ فِي كَفِّهِ قِطْعَةً مِنَ الْوَهَجِ، وَيَمْشِي وَرَأْسُهُ مُنْحَنٍ إِلَيْهَا

The boat moored. Sindbad and Juha walked through streets lit like daytime, crowded with people, and no one looked at them. Every human being carried in his palm a small piece of the glow, and walked with his head bent toward it. No one lifted his eyes, no one looked at the sea, and no one, ever, bumped into anyone: they flowed around one another as water flows around stones, curving and never touching.

Sindbad stopped at a woman sitting on a doorstep. In her palm a glow, and in the glow the face of a distant man smiling at her; her eyes were wet, and her lips moved with a name he could not hear. Not everything in the glow is deception, Sindbad thought; in some of it there are loved ones who are truly far. And this, not the lying, is what makes the matter harder.

Juha whispered, he too, without noticing, had begun to bend toward a small light in his own hand:

Juha: How quiet they are… and how many… and why do I feel more alone among them than I ever was on the open sea?

And Sindbad felt his own neck tilting too, slightly, toward his empty hand.

III The River of Light نَهْرُ الضَّوْءِ

Plate · The river of light

His grandfather's face gleamed in his palm, and every time he tried to look at it alone, a flash blinked at the corner of his eye.

الْتَمَعَ فِي كَفِّهِ وَجْهُ جَدِّهِ، وَكُلَّمَا حَاوَلَ أَنْ يَنْظُرَ إِلَيْهِ وَحْدَهُ، وَمَضَتْ شَاشَةٌ فِي طَرَفِ عَيْنِهِ

In the city's center a river of light flows, luminous water streaming without pause, images and voices and colors floating in it, all of them astonishing, all of them vanishing before they can be held. Sindbad stood on its bank. It was beautiful; its flowing never wearied.

Then, out of all that noise, he heard a voice he knew: his grandfather's voice, the grandfather who had taught him the names of the stars and died before finishing the teaching. The voice was there, in the river, calling him by his old name. He reached out his hand, and his grandfather's face gleamed in his palm, and his heart stopped, and he never wanted to lift his eyes from it again.

And every time he tried to look at the face alone, small flashes sprang from the river, each one blinking at the corner of his eye: look here, no, here, no, here. So he would turn, and the grandfather's face would vanish for a moment, and he would go back searching for it, and another would flash, until his head was spinning the way the needle had spun in his grandfather's compass.

The Silence of the Question

عَشْرُ ثَوَانٍ · Ten seconds

نَفَسَانِ… · Two breaths… then notice

Close your eyes. The flashes tug at you from every side. From what place in your body do they tug first, from the stomach? from behind the eyes? from the palms of the hands? Then open them slowly: what is still glowing behind your eyelids?

أَغْمِضْ عَيْنَيْكَ. الْوَمَضَاتُ تَشُدُّكَ مِنْ كُلِّ طَرَفٍ. مِنْ أَيِّ مَوْضِعٍ فِي جَسَدِكَ تَشُدُّكَ أَوَّلًا، مِنَ الْمَعِدَةِ؟ مِنْ خَلْفِ الْعَيْنَيْنِ؟ مِنْ رَاحَتَيِ الْيَدَيْنِ؟ ثُمَّ افْتَحْهُمَا بِبُطْءٍ: مَاذَا بَقِيَ يَتَوَهَّجُ خَلْفَ جَفْنَيْكَ؟

It pulls يَشُدُّ

IV Zaynab and the Raised Head زَيْنَبُ وَالرَّأْسُ الْمَرْفُوعُ

Plate · The only raised head

Zaynab was the only one who raised her head, and carried no glow in her palm.

كَانَتْ زَيْنَبُ الْوَحِيدَةَ الَّتِي تَرْفَعُ رَأْسَهَا، وَلَا تَحْمِلُ فِي كَفِّهَا وَهَجًا

Zaynab was the only one on the island who raised her head. Her eyes were larger than her small face, and she carried no glow in her palm. She said, looking up at the grey sky:

Zaynab: The stars never went away, Sindbad. They are above you right now, all of them. But the glow is nearer and stronger, and the eye that has grown used to the near has forgotten how to see the far.

Sindbad: Then how do I bring them back?

Zaynab: You do not bring them back. They never left, you are the one who left them. To see them, someone must sit in the dark long enough for his eye to remember how to look far.

She fell silent, then added in a lower voice:

Zaynab: I did it once. I set down my glow and sat in the dark, and there was a voice in it that I love, and I let it go so the star could return.

She gazed at her empty palm a long while.

Zaynab: And even now, every night, my hand reaches for it. Do not think it becomes easy; it only becomes possible.

And for the first time Sindbad understood why his grandfather's compass had broken: the needle never broke. The star that makes the north is what could no longer be seen.

V The Hungry One الْجَائِعُ

Plate · The face was his own

The face was his own, older, the neck bent with a bending that never straightens.

كَانَ الْوَجْهُ وَجْهَهُ هُوَ، أَكْبَرَ، رَقَبَتُهُ مُنْحَنِيَةٌ انْحِنَاءً لَا يَسْتَقِيمُ أَبَدًا

In the heart of the island, where the glow is at its strongest, the Hungry One waited for him. He was not a monster; he was a man. And when Sindbad came close and saw his face, his breath stopped: the face was his own, older, pallid, sleepless, the eyes reddened by a light he had not put out in an age, and the neck bent with a bending that never straightens.

The Hungry One: I will eat your tomorrow, Sindbad. Give me the now. Only the now. Always the now.

Then the Hungry One raised his hand and laid it on his bent neck, rubbing it like one who aches and cannot lift his head. And without meaning to, Sindbad found his own hand rising to his own neck, to the same place, to the same tilt. Neither of them said anything. There was nothing to be said.

The Silence of the Question

عَشْرُ ثَوَانٍ · Ten seconds

نَفَسَانِ… · Two breaths… then notice

Do not think about his face. Only notice: where has your breath gone right now? Did it climb to the top of your chest and quicken, or descend into your belly and grow calm?

لَا تُفَكِّرْ فِي وَجْهِهِ. لَاحِظْ فَقَطْ: أَيْنَ ذَهَبَ نَفَسُكَ الْآنَ؟ هَلْ صَعِدَ إِلَى أَعْلَى صَدْرِكَ وَتَسَارَعَ، أَمْ نَزَلَ إِلَى بَطْنِكَ وَهَدَأَ؟

It watches يُرَاقِبُ

VI Sitting in the Dark الْجُلُوسُ فِي الظَّلَامِ

Plate · Sitting in the dark

He laid the glow face-down, gripped his hand with his other hand, and let it tremble.

وَضَعَ الْوَهَجَ مَقْلُوبًا، وَأَمْسَكَ يَدَهُ بِيَدِهِ الْأُخْرَى، وَتَرَكَهَا تَرْتَجِفُ

Zaynab did not tell Sindbad to smash the glow; who can smash the sea? She asked for what is harder:

Zaynab: Set it down. And sit in the dark. And do not lift it, no matter what happens.

Sindbad: Even my grandfather's voice?

She looked at him a long while and did not answer. There was an answer in her silence, and it hurt.

Sindbad laid the piece of glow on the sand, face-down, and his grandfather's voice choked beneath it. The darkness rushed in. And from the very first moment his hand began reaching toward it on its own, so he gripped it with his other hand. His pulse was beating in his thumb, hot, insistent; his throat went dry; his shoulder hunched, wanting to bend down toward the sand where the glow lay. Behind his eyes a heat, and in his stomach a small, terrifying certainty that he was missing, now, in this exact moment, something that would never come again. His hand crawled out a second time, so he pinned it to his knee, and let it tremble.

The Hungry One screamed, then began, for the first time, to shrink; for he feeds only on the gaze, and Sindbad had lifted his gaze from him. And the first seconds were longer than every sea he had ever crossed.

The Silence of the Question

VII The Star That Was There النَّجْمَةُ الَّتِي كَانَتْ هُنَاكَ

Plate · The star that was always there

One star, small, trembling, the grandfather was never in the glow; he was here, above him.

نَجْمَةٌ وَاحِدَةٌ، صَغِيرَةٌ، مُرْتَجِفَةٌ، لَمْ يَكُنِ الْجَدُّ فِي الْوَهَجِ قَطُّ، كَانَ هُنَا، فَوْقَهُ

Then, very slowly, Sindbad's eyes remembered how to look far. One star, small, trembling, at the very top of the sky. He knew it; his grandfather had taught him its name. It was not his voice in the river; it was the sky his grandfather had loved and taught him to read. The grandfather was never in the glow. He was here, above him, in the star he had thought was gone.

The needle of his grandfather's compass moved, held toward it for a moment, then trembled a little as though it were still learning, but, for the first time since the white horizon, it knew where to look. Juha raised his head too, gazed at the star a long while, then said in a voice that was not his usual voice:

Juha: It was above my head the whole time.

And he did not add a joke, this time.

VIII A Sea Where the Stars Return بَحْرٌ تَعُودُ فِيهِ النُّجُومُ

Plate · A sea where the stars return

He held the glow the way an oar is held, not the way one holds a helm that steers you, and raised his eyes to the star first.

أَمْسَكَ الْوَهَجَ كَمَا يُمْسَكُ الْمِجْدَافُ، لَا كَمَا يُمْسَكُ مِقْوَدٌ يَقُودُكَ، وَرَفَعَ عَيْنَيْهِ إِلَى النَّجْمَةِ أَوَّلًا

The Hungry One did not die. He curled up small, exactly the size of the piece of glow, and sat on the sand waiting, calm and patient, knowing that the gaze will drop again, and again, and that raising the head is a labor that never ends.

Sindbad bent down and picked up the piece of glow from the sand, and his grandfather's voice came back to him faintly. He did not throw it into the sea, things that have become part of the world are not thrown away, and he held it the way an oar is held, not the way one holds a helm that steers you. Then he raised his eyes to the star first, before looking at it.

And he set sail. Not toward certainty, the searcher never arrives at certainty, but toward a sea where the stars return every night, for whoever can bear to sit a little while in the dark.

A Last Invitation دَعْوَةٌ أَخِيرَةٌ

When was the last time you saw a real star?

Tonight, if you wish: lay the glow, face-down, for ten slow breaths. Do nothing else. Let your eyes remember how to see far. Then write one single word of what you felt. Show it to no one; it is yours.

«الْمَعَانِي تَتَقَاطَعُ بِالْحِسِّ، وَاللُّغَةُ تُعْرَفُ بِالسِّيَاقِ»

Meanings cross through the senses, and language is known through context.

انْتَهَتِ الْمَلَاحِمُ الْأَرْبَعُ

The Four Arcs are complete.

A Whisper to the Companion

هَمْسٌ لِلْمُرَافِقِ

Read after, never during.

For the teacher, parent or companion, as in Fascicle I: read after the story or before sleep, never in its midst.

The Key of the Eternal Day

"The Eternal Day" = a light that never goes out and swallows the stars: the glow in the palm draws the far so near that it makes us forget it. And "the Hungry One" is not a monster, but a person's own face if he never raises his head; he is not defeated, he shrinks when we lift our gaze from him. In the body: the hand reaches toward the glow, then holds itself back, and comes to know the lightness of the empty palm.

How to Accompany the Pauses

Do not answer the question on the reader's behalf, and do not ask him "What did you understand?" After each pause, be content with two silent breaths together; then, if he speaks, listen without correcting. The bilingual seal ("يَتَوَهَّجُ · It glows") describes a movement in the body, not a meaning in the mind; so let it describe his movement.

The Last Invitation of Touch

Offer the invitation once, beside the child, and only as an invitation: any screen face-down, ten breaths, and notice, do not name, where the hand reaches. Never use it as a rule or a punishment; a child whose phone has been taken away has not been given a star. Do not ask for perfection, ask for the repeated attempt.

«النُّجُومُ لَمْ تَذْهَبْ؛ أَنْتَ مَنْ غَابَ عَنْهَا.»

The stars never left; you are the one who left them.
