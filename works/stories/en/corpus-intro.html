Introduction

المقدّمة

English edition

بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

In the name of God, the Merciful, the Compassionate

Young reader, and older reader,

In this book there is a sailor who cannot sit still. His name is Sindbad. He likes to arrive first, and to tell everyone how he did it. Beside him walks a clever vixen called Kalila, who asks him one small question at the very moment he forgets to ask it of himself. Along the road they meet a girl who can hear the quietest sounds, a strong boy who carries everything in his two hands, and guests who have stepped out of our old tales.

There are sixty short stories, and each takes about two minutes to read aloud. They hold wells and wind, rain, a bridge and a snail, and the moments we all know: being slower than the others, fearing a sound in the night, losing something we love, getting it wrong and beginning again. Some of the stories are funny. Some are a little sad, and then pat you on the shoulder.

The journey is called Silence and Awareness. By silence we do not mean that a child must keep quiet. We mean giving a story time to arrive, and noticing what is around us: the sound of water in a jug, the smell of bread, the weight of a stone in the hand. Every home and every classroom can find these things.

Who is it for? For children from six to eight, who hear it in a voice they love. For children from nine to twelve, who read it themselves in a quiet corner. For parents looking for a few lovely minutes before sleep. For teachers who want a gentle start to a lesson.

How should you read it? One story a day, and no more. Choose a regular time and a place with soft light. Read slowly, and leave a short pause between one paragraph and the next, because the pause is part of the tale. After each story come a small activity and two questions for the adult to ask; take whatever suits your day. If the day has been tiring, the story alone is enough. If a child asks for yesterday’s story, read it again, for on the second hearing children catch what they missed on the first. It is best to read the stories in order, because the journey runs from a forest of glass to a last crossing.

Nothing in this book has to be memorised, and there is no test at the end. These are tales to be heard, after which we return to our day carrying a little of them with us.

The guests are Antara, Baydaba the Sage, Juha and Scheherazade, and each of them has a story in which they arrive as a guest of the sailor and his companion.

This is a book of stories, not a therapy. It does not promise to change anyone’s nature, and it does not take the place of a doctor or a specialist. If you notice that a child’s sadness has lasted, or that a fear is wearing the child out, talk to someone who knows how to help.

When the story ends and the child stays quiet for a while, do not hurry them. They may have something to think about.

And success comes from God.

The Characters

  • Sindbad: a sailor who cannot sit still. He likes to arrive first and to boast, and he learns slowly.
  • Kalila: a wise vixen with a slightly sharp tongue. She teaches by deed and by question more than by explanation.
  • Talia: a girl who listens well and catches the small sounds that others miss.
  • Ali: a strong, practical boy who learns that carrying things is not the only kind of strength.
  • Guests from the tradition: Antara in story 6, Baydaba in story 8, Juha in story 25 and Scheherazade in story 28. Yasmina appears in story 7.
