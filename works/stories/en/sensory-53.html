1. The Stretch of the Thread مَدَّةُ الخَيْطِ

Root: m–d–d يَمُدُّ، المَدُّ، مَمْدُودٌ · Vessel: omniscient narrator

～ Anchor

A cotton thread on the table. Its two ends far apart.

Transformation

The boy stretches the thread between his palms. It tightens. Suddenly it coils around his finger. ☆ A short laugh escapes him.

He loosens his grip a little. His shoulders relax. The thread grows softer. The distance between his hands widens.

□ (4 seconds… what changed in your shoulders?)

～ Integration

The boy returns the thread to the table. A faint trace of the coil stays around his finger, then slowly comes undone.

Mentor's Box (adults only)

Articulation: the mīm and dāl in a light, drawn-out tone.

Movement: stretching a thread slowly, then easing the grip by degrees.

Relational warmth: the coiling is the beginning of discovery, not a mistake. ☆ Share his laugh if it breaks out.

Alternatives: sensory-sensitive → a smooth silk thread. | high-energy → a short elastic band. | Transition criterion: he eases his grip unmodeled in two consecutive sessions.

Discovery marker: the child eases his grip spontaneously after the first coil.

English bridge: Thread pulls… shoulders drop… hands rest.

2. An Axis That Turns مِحْوَرٌ يَدُورُ

Root: d–w–r يَدُورُ، الدَّوَرَانُ، دَائِرٌ · Vessel: monologue of the spine (a non-human voice)

～ Anchor

Two feet planted. A torso beginning to turn around itself.

Transformation

I am the spine. When you turn, I lean with you.

And when you speed up, your head sways like a branch in a wind.

You slow. You stop suddenly. You fix your eyes on a point.

The muscles of your back draw your head upward, and your feet sink into the ground.

□ (5 seconds… where is the stillness in your body now?)

～ Integration

The stillness stands in your axis. The ground beneath you, the point before you, and the dizziness withdrawing like water going back.

Mentor's Box (adults only)

Articulation: the dāl , wāw , and rāʾ in a rounded tone.

Movement: slow turning, a sudden stop, fixing the gaze on a point.

Relational warmth: the swaying is a laboratory of balance, not a danger.

Alternatives: weak balance → turning while seated. | high-energy → a hand rhythm while turning. | Transition criterion: he fixes his gaze spontaneously on stopping, three times.

Discovery marker: the child fixes his gaze spontaneously at the moment of stopping.

English bridge: Body turns… axis steadies… feet root.

3. A Weight That Lightens ثِقَلٌ يَخِفُّ

Root: th–q–l يَثْقُلُ، الثِّقَلُ، ثَقِيلٌ · Vessel: a diary (first person, told after the event)

～ Anchor

A light pillow in one palm, a small stone in the other. Eyes closed.

Transformation

Today I lifted a pillow and a stone, with my eyes closed.

I tensed my muscles for the pillow… as if it were a boulder! ☆ I nearly laughed at my hand's trick.

Then I lifted the stone, and my hand dipped a little.

I opened my eyes, and I understood: my muscles had been "fooled" by size, not by weight.

□ (4 seconds… if you lifted the pillow again, what would your grip be like?)

～ Integration

I set down the stone and the pillow. In my palms a small difference remained, fading with every breath.

Mentor's Box (adults only)

Articulation: the thāʾ , qāf , and lām in a descending tone.

Movement: lifting two objects of different weight with eyes closed, noticing the difference in effort.

Relational warmth: the "muscle trick" is cause for a smile, not embarrassment. ☆

Alternatives: weak discrimination → very contrasting weights. | high-energy → quick switching between palms. | Transition criterion: he describes the difference in a word of his own.

Discovery marker: the child adjusts his grip spontaneously on the second lift.

English bridge: Muscle tenses… weight reveals… effort eases.

I set out into the air, and could no longer be heard… so where did I go? I did not vanish. I became a faint tremor in the chest. The body keeps the tune by the stillness that follows it. I let the air carry the tremor, and my chest quivers with every echo that returns, then grows calm.

30. The Seedling That Refused to Hurry النَّبْتَةُ الَّتِي رَفَضَتِ العَجَلَةَ Squatting down. Two palms on soil. A gentle press, like asking permission. Kalila was digging around a seedling, then she pulled its stem., Kalila: "Come on, come up faster!", Sindbad: "Don't pull her… the root pushes from below.", Kalila (laughing): "Ah! I always want everything now !" Beneath the soil there is no silence, but a steady pushing. The seedling came up not because Kalila pulled it, but because she let the earth push. Beneath her palm, something small and green splits the soil, slowly.

31. A Dialogue Between a Palm and a Weight حِوَارٌ بَيْنَ كَفٍّ وَثِقَلٍ The end of a rope. A slow pull. Watching the weight. The rope was heavy in Sindbad's palm. Whenever he yanked it violently, the weight sank deeper. He loosened his palm a little, and pulled to the rhythm of his breath: inhale… pull… exhale… hold. The rope began to walk with him, step by step, and the weight softened beneath his palm.

32. Two Hands Wide Enough for Emptiness يَدَانِ تَتَّسِعَانِ لِلْفَرَاغِ Two palms cupped like a bowl. A small space between them. A deep breath. You look at your hands. There is nothing in them to touch, yet they are asking for a place. The fingers part slowly. The palms open. The emptiness settles into them. Your chest keeps the breath, and your hand keeps this emptiness without gripping it. The emptiness sits in your palms, light, like water that does not spill.

33. The First Dot النُّقْطَةُ الأُولَى A pen raised above a surface. A deep breath. A descent with the exhale. My notebook is white… and the pen in my palm grows heavy before it touches the paper. I am afraid of leaving a wrong mark. I exhale… and set down a small dot, like a raindrop. I do not gather my thoughts first; I let the pen touch, and the rest comes with the movement. On the paper is a single dot, and the pen is already waiting for the second.

34. At the Boundary عِنْدَ الحَدِّ Two index fingers facing each other. Drawing closer, very slowly., Yasin: "Bring your finger closer… slowly.", Nour: "Leave a space between us.", (Yasin runs out of patience and touches!) Yasin (laughing): "I rushed it!", Nour: "I can feel the warmth of your finger… and we have not touched." The more they breathed together, the more the tension eased, and the air between the two fingers grew warm, thin as a thread.

35. What I Cannot See مَا لَا أَرَاهُ The end of a light cloth. Moving it slowly with the breath. A light cloth in my hand. The air comes… and it quivers. The air stops… and it stills. I cannot see the air with my eye… but my cloth sees it for me. The cloth quivers, so I know the air is passing; it stills, so I know it has stopped. I keep the cloth in my palm, waiting for another gust to move it.

36. The Sound of "Here" صَوْتُ «هُنَا» A wooden bead. Turning it between fingers., Sindbad (hurrying, and it falls): "Ah! Why won't it settle?", Kalila (laughing): "Because you're rushing, grandfather!", Sindbad: "You're right… let me slow down." (a light tap) "Yes… a tap that says: here." The slower he went, the clearer the sound of settling became. The bead turns between his fingers, then stops, and the last tap says: here .

37. A Trace in the Air أَثَرٌ فِي الهَوَاءِ A fine mist on a hand. An index finger moved slowly through the air. Mist on my hand… cold, light, shining. I raise my index finger and move it through the air: a loop… a wave… a circle! Rishrish… rishrish… I did not draw on paper… I drew in the air, and my skin kept the path. Rishrish… the cold line dries on my index finger, and its place stays a little warm.

38. Listen Downward أَنْصِتِي إِلَى أَسْفَلَ Standing firm. A palm on a knee. I used to think that rising meant reaching the top quickly. I raised my hand, proud: "I've arrived!", and I wobbled, and laughed. Sindbad gestured: "Listen downward first." I surrendered my weight to the ground first, then carried it upward, station by station. And when the sensation reached my head, I was light, as if I had grown up out of my own feet.

39. The Scent Surprises الرَّائِحَةُ تُفَاجِئُ A flower, or a peel. A quiet smelling. I bring the peel close and smell… I was expecting a sharp perfume, and instead I find the scent of wet earth! I laugh at the surprise. The scent climbs with my breath, curls around my throat, then settles in my chest. The scent departs with the exhale, and its place in the chest stays warm.

40. Layer After Layer طَبَقَةٌ بَعْدَ طَبَقَةٍ A piece of fruit on the tip of a tongue. Melting. I place the piece… and I scowl! The sourness invades. My lips pucker. Then the warmth comes: the sharpness melts, and the sweetness flows in. The taste transforms, layer after layer. I taste at leisure, to follow the journey. The last layer is a light sweetness, staying on the tip of my tongue.

41. A Sound in the Chest صَوْتٌ فِي الصَّدْرِ Two hands on a wooden box. Tapping., Sindbad (striking hard): TUMM! "Ow! An awful sound!", Kalila (laughing): "Because you're fighting it! Tap gently…", Sindbad (tapping softly): "Ah… the tremor climbs up my arm!" Sound does not enter the ear alone; it is a tremor that climbs from the wood to the arm, and then the chest. Sindbad gives one last gentle tap, and it trembles in his chest, then grows still.

42. The Eye That Widens العَيْنُ الَّتِي تَتَّسِعُ Staring at something near for five seconds. Then lifting the gaze to the farthest point. You stare… and your forehead tightens. The world narrows. Then, hup!, you lift your gaze. Shhh… shhh… your brow breathes. You do not chase the details; the view comes to you. The cord between your eyebrows loosens, and the gaze widens until the far enters it without effort.

43. A Palm Wide Enough for Opposites رَاحَةٌ تَسَعُ الضِّدَّيْنِ A right hand on something warm and smooth. A left hand on something cold and rough. You touch the soft warmth… and the left hand is startled by the rough cold! It recoils… then returns. You do not have to choose; you let the two converse. The skin does not fear contradiction; it draws a map of warmth and cold at once. The two hands meet in your lap, and the warmth mingles with the cold into one quiet, middling temperature.

44. The Ankle's Dance رَقْصَةُ الكَاحِلِ Standing on one foot. Eyes closed. The world teeters! You lean, you nearly fall, and you laugh. You sway with your shadow. The more your ankle loosens, the more the weight finds its way. You do not stiffen your leg, the ankle plays. You place your palm on your chest, and breathe. The foot is steady, and the ankle still sways lightly beneath you, as if it were dancing.

45. The Air That Waits الهَوَاءُ الَّذِي يَنْتَظِرُ A deep inhale. Holding the air for one short moment. His chest fills… he holds it. His cheeks puff out. Holding is not a prison; it is a short pause. Then comes the long exhale, and the air goes out free, and the chest descends, and the cheeks soften.

46. A Sting, Then Warmth لَسْعَةٌ ثُمَّ دِفْءٌ Clapping، once hard, once lightly., Sindbad (clapping hard): BUTBUT! "What is that sound?", Kalila (laughing): "Grandfather claps like a duck! Try gently…", Sindbad (clapping lightly): "Ah… a sting, then warmth!" The clap is a tremor that travels; the sting wakes, and the warmth soothes. Sindbad lays his reddened palms on his chest, and the sting turns into a still warmth.

47. The Bend That Does Not Break الانْحِنَاءُ الَّذِي لَا يَكْسِرُ Bending forward, very slowly. Stopping at the first tightness. You bend slowly… your back tightens. "Ah!" Tiktik… so speak your vertebrae. You stop at the first tightness. You do not pull yourself down; the breath lengthens you. One vertebra softens, then another. You come back up to standing, vertebra by vertebra, and in your back a quiet length remains.

48. The Throat's Secret سِرُّ الحَلْقِ A hand on a throat. Whispering a long word. I raise my hand to my throat and whisper: "a little secret…" My voice is faint, but my throat trembles under my fingers. I nearly laugh at the tickle of the tremor. Whispering is not weakness; it is a small strength touching the wall of the throat. And when I stop whispering, my throat grows still beneath my hand, and a light warmth remains where the tremor was.

49. The Hold of the Stone مَسْكَةُ الحَجَرِ A small stone in the hand. A hard squeeze, then an easing. He gripped the stone hard… his fingers whitening from the pressure. Suddenly the stone slips! He laughs at the surprise. He picks it up again, and holds it gently. The stone becomes as if it were an extension of his hand. Holding is not squeezing; when the joints relax, the pressure lightens. He puts the stone back, lays his palm on his chest, and breathes deeply. In his palm the trace of the stone remains, warm.

50. The Push of Steadiness دَفْعَةُ الثَّبَاتِ Standing before a wall, two palms on it, knees bent. He pushes the wall hard from up high… and his foot slips! He nearly falls, and laughs. He tries again, pressing his heels into the ground. The push climbs from the ground up through his body. When he pushed from up high his foot slipped; and when he pressed with his heels he felt the ground pushing him back. He takes his hands away and stands, and the weight returns to his feet, quiet.

51. The Twist of the Torso لَيُّ الجِذْعِ Standing, hands on hips. Twisting the torso right and left, slowly. He twists his torso fast… and his head spins. He laughs as he staggers. He twists again, slowly. He stops at the first tightness in his waist. The fast twist spins the head; the slow twist reveals the range. He comes back to standing, and his sides remain supple, breathing.

52. A Leap That Absorbs وَثْبَةٌ تَمْتَصُّ Bending two knees. A light jump. He jumps… he lands hard. A jolt in his feet. His knees are rigid. He laughs at the shake. He jumps again, and this time he bends his knees before the landing and through it. The impact lightens. Jumping with a rigid knee is impact; with a soft knee, lightness. He stands, and his legs stay light, as if the ground had returned the jump to him gently.

53. The Rippling of the Air تَمَوُّجُ الهَوَاءِ Standing or sitting. Moving the arms in slow swimming strokes. He moves his arms… and a soft sound comes: wash-wash . He smiles. When he speeds up, the breath breaks up. He slows, and lets the arm widen with the inhale and return with the exhale. When he hurried his chest narrowed, and when he slowed the whole body began to breathe. He loosens his arms and lays his palms on his chest. The wave calms in his arms, and his chest goes on widening and narrowing, like still water.
