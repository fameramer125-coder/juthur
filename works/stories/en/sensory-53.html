A note on this edition

This volume gives the fifty-three Prize Stories in English. Each story keeps the author’s unified pulse: Anchor, Transformation (with the silent question kept where it falls, in the middle of the story) and Integration. In Part One the story is followed by the complete Mentor’s Box: articulation, movement, relational warmth, safety notes where the activity calls for them, alternatives with the transition criterion, the discovery marker, and the author’s own English bridge, kept as written. The ☆ marks the author’s shared-joy cue and stays in place.

Part Two is the child’s layer: the same fifty-three stories as literary text alone, with the scaffolding hidden. The Mentor’s Box is for adults only and is never read to the child.

Every activity is done on level ground with an adult close by. Any child may stop, sit or simply watch at any moment, and a child is never pressed to speak, to touch or to taste anything.

1. The Stretch of the Thread

مَدَّةُ الخَيْطِ

Root m-d-d يَمُدُّ، المَدُّ، مَمْدُودٌ · Vessel omniscient narrator

Anchor

A cotton thread on the table. Its two ends far apart.

Transformation

The boy stretches the thread between his palms. It tightens. Suddenly it coils around his finger. ☆ A short laugh escapes him.

He loosens his grip a little. His shoulders relax. The thread grows softer. The distance between his hands widens.

Silent question 4 seconds what changed in your shoulders?

Integration

The boy returns the thread to the table. A faint trace of the coil stays around his finger, then slowly comes undone.

Mentor’s Box adults only

Articulation the mīm and dāl in a light, drawn-out tone.

Movement stretching a thread slowly, then easing the grip by degrees.

Relational warmth the coiling is the beginning of discovery, not a mistake. ☆ Share his laugh if it breaks out.

Alternatives

  • sensory-sensitive → a smooth silk thread.
  • high-energy → a short elastic band.
  • Transition criterion: he eases his grip unmodelled in two consecutive sessions.

Discovery marker the child eases his grip spontaneously after the first coil.

English bridge Thread pulls… shoulders drop… hands rest.

2. An Axis That Turns

مِحْوَرٌ يَدُورُ

Root d-w-r يَدُورُ، الدَّوَرَانُ، دَائِرٌ · Vessel monologue of the spine (a non-human voice)

Anchor

Two feet planted. A torso beginning to turn around itself.

Transformation

I am the spine. When you turn, I lean with you.

And when you speed up, your head sways like a branch in a wind.

You slow. You stop suddenly. You fix your eyes on a point.

The muscles of your back draw your head upward, and your feet sink into the ground.

Silent question 5 seconds where is the stillness in your body now?

Integration

The stillness stands in your axis. The ground beneath you, the point before you, and the dizziness withdrawing like water going back.

Mentor’s Box adults only

Articulation the dāl, wāw, and rāʾ in a rounded tone.

Movement slow turning, a sudden stop, fixing the gaze on a point.

Relational warmth the swaying is a laboratory of balance, not a danger.

Safety note a clear space with no hard edges nearby; two or three slow turns are enough.

Safe-exit protocol if the child feels dizzy, he sits down at once and looks at a fixed point.

Alternatives

  • weak balance → turning while seated.
  • high-energy → a hand rhythm while turning.
  • Transition criterion: he fixes his gaze spontaneously on stopping, three times.

Discovery marker the child fixes his gaze spontaneously at the moment of stopping.

English bridge Body turns… axis steadies… feet root.

3. A Weight That Lightens

ثِقَلٌ يَخِفُّ

Root th-q-l يَثْقُلُ، الثِّقَلُ، ثَقِيلٌ · Vessel a diary (first person, told after the event)

Anchor

A light pillow in one palm, a small stone in the other. Eyes closed.

Transformation

Today I lifted a pillow and a stone, with my eyes closed.

I tensed my muscles for the pillow… as if it were a boulder! ☆ I nearly laughed at my hand’s trick.

Then I lifted the stone, and my hand dipped a little.

I opened my eyes, and I understood: my muscles had been “fooled” by size, not by weight.

Silent question 4 seconds if you lifted the pillow again, what would your grip be like?

Integration

I set down the stone and the pillow. In my palms a small difference remained, fading with every breath.

Mentor’s Box adults only

Articulation the thāʾ, qāf, and lām in a descending tone.

Movement lifting two objects of different weight with eyes closed, noticing the difference in effort.

Relational warmth the “muscle trick” is cause for a smile, not embarrassment. ☆

Alternatives

  • weak discrimination → very contrasting weights.
  • high-energy → quick switching between palms.
  • Transition criterion: he describes the difference in a word of his own.

Discovery marker the child adjusts his grip spontaneously on the second lift.

English bridge Muscle tenses… weight reveals… effort eases.

4. A Flicker of Light

وَمْضَةُ نُورٍ

Root ḍ-w-ʾ يُضِيءُ، الضَّوْءُ، مُضِيءٌ · Vessel a dialogue between the eye and the light (a double non-human voice)

Anchor

A faint light flickering on the wall. Two eyes following it.

Transformation

The Eye: I stare at the light… and it escapes me!

The Light: Do not strain yourself. Blink.

The Eye: I blinked. I let the light pass. I began to see it without effort.

Silent question 4 seconds how do your eyelids feel now?

Integration

The eye closes, then opens. The light is still there, flickering at its leisure, and the eyelid is soft.

Mentor’s Box adults only

Articulation the ḍād, wāw, and hamza with a light opening.

Movement following a flickering light, and slow deliberate blinking.

Relational warmth missing the flicker is a chance to slow down, not a failure.

Safety note a torch or lamp on a low setting; never a laser, a flashing light or the sun.

Alternatives

  • light-sensitive → a very dim light.
  • high-energy → tracking the light with a hand movement.
  • Transition criterion: he blinks spontaneously before straining his eye.

Discovery marker the child blinks spontaneously before the light “escapes” him.

English bridge Light flickers… blink softens… gaze rests.

5. A Letter in Hiding

حَرْفٌ يَخْتَبِئُ

Root b-y-n يَبِينُ، البَيْنُ، بَائِنٌ · Vessel participating narrator (describing what the child does)

Anchor

A page filled with tangled lines. A letter hiding among them.

Transformation

The boy stares at the page · and the letters hide all the more!

☆ He smiles at the tiredness of it, then blinks slowly.

Suddenly he looks at the empty space around the lines. The letter stands out. The shape becomes clear without effort.

Silent question 4 seconds where was your eye when the letter appeared?

Integration

He closes his eyes, then opens them onto something familiar. The empty space around him has become visible, the way things are visible.

Mentor’s Box adults only

Articulation the bāʾ, yāʾ, and nūn in a light, open tone.

Movement fixing the gaze on a cluttered background, then blinking, then shifting focus to the empty space.

Relational warmth eye strain is the sign of an attempt, and the smile dissolves the tension. ☆

Alternatives

  • weak discrimination → a light background and a dark letter.
  • high-energy → flipping the page, then stopping.
  • Transition criterion: he shifts his gaze to the empty space spontaneously.

Discovery marker the child shifts his gaze to the empty space spontaneously after the third blink.

English bridge Stare softens… background fades… shape appears.

6. The Warmth of the Word

دِفْءُ الكَلِمَةِ

Root d-f-ʾ يَدْفَأُ، الدِّفْءُ، دَافِئٌ · Vessel a temporal-sensory experience (second person)

Anchor

A warm stone in one palm, a wrapped piece of ice in the other. Eyes closed.

Transformation

You touch the warm… you touch the cold. The two palms differ.

You open your eyes. You read the word “warmth” with your palm on the stone.

You read the word “coldness” with your palm on the ice. The word quivers in the palm.

Silent question 5 seconds in which palm do you feel the word more?

Integration

He sets down the stone and the ice, and empties his palms. In one of them a stray warmth remains; in the other, a cold that is withdrawing.

Mentor’s Box adults only

Articulation the dāl, fāʾ, and hamza in a warm, open tone.

Movement holding a cold and a warm object with eyes closed, then reading the word after the touch.

Relational warmth the frown and the smile are both part of the discovery.

Safety note the ice stays wrapped in cloth and touches the skin for a few seconds only; the “warm” stone is warmed in a hand or on a sunny sill, never heated.

Safe-exit protocol if either object is uncomfortable, he puts it down at once.

Alternatives

  • heat-sensitive → cool and warm water in two bags.
  • high-energy → quick switching between palms.
  • Transition criterion: he links the word to the temperature unprompted.

Discovery marker the child reads the word unprompted after the second touch.

English bridge Skin touches… warmth spreads… word lands.

7. The Pen’s Dialogue

حِوَارُ القَلَمِ

Root q-b-ḍ يَقْبِضُ، القَبْضُ، قَابِضٌ · Vessel a dialogue between the hand and the pen (a double non-human voice)

Anchor

A pencil between the fingers. The paper waiting.

Transformation

The Hand: I gripped the pen hard.

The Pen: “I feel the pressure!”

The Hand: I ease my grip.

The Pen: “That’s better. Let me glide.”

☆ The hand listens, the pen flows, and the line grows smoother.

Silent question 4 seconds how do your fingers feel around the pen now?

Integration

He sets down the pen and stretches his fingers. On the paper a soft line remains, and in the fingers, a light rest.

Mentor’s Box adults only

Articulation the qāf, bāʾ, and ḍād in a balanced tone.

Movement holding the pen, an exaggerated squeeze, then a gradual easing.

Relational warmth the pen’s “protest” is a voice-game that makes children laugh. ☆

Alternatives

  • touch-sensitive → a thick wax crayon.
  • high-energy → big fast lines.
  • Transition criterion: he regulates his pressure without reminders.

Discovery marker the child adjusts his pressure spontaneously after the pen’s “protest.”

English bridge Fingers squeeze… pen whispers… grip lets go.

8. The Dance of the Step

رَقْصَةُ الخُطْوَةِ

Root m-sh-y يَمْشِي، المَشْيُ، مَاشٍ · Vessel a diary (first person, told after the event)

Anchor

Heavy steps on the ground. The sound of pounding fills the place.

Transformation

Today I walked heavily, my feet striking the ground. ☆ I laughed at their sound, like a little drum.

Then I tried walking quietly. I rolled my weight from the heel to the ball of the foot.

The pounding sound disappeared. I began to glide.

Silent question 4 seconds how does your foot touch the ground now?

Integration

I stand, and breathe. The ground beneath me is silent, and in the soles of my feet a light warmth from the walking.

Mentor’s Box adults only

Articulation the mīm, shīn, and yāʾ with a light fluidity.

Movement slow walking, rolling the weight from heel to ball.

Relational warmth the “penguin walk” is an invitation to play. ☆

Safety note bare feet or flat shoes on a level, non-slip floor, with no stairs or edges nearby.

Alternatives

  • weak balance → walking on a foam mat.
  • high-energy → walking to the rhythm of a song.
  • Transition criterion: he rolls his weight unmodelled.

Discovery marker the child rolls his weight spontaneously after the first heavy steps.

English bridge Heel lifts… weight rolls… foot glides.

9. The Edge of the Distance

حَافَّةُ المَسَافَةِ

Root l-m-s يَلْمِسُ، المَلْمَسُ، لَامِسٌ · Vessel omniscient narrator

Anchor

An index finger passing along a wooden table. Moving forward slowly. The wood is smooth, then it ends, suddenly.

Transformation

The boy touches the edge. The finger slides off into the air. His lips part.

He passes his finger again. He stops before the fall. The skin tightens, then relaxes.

Silent question 4 seconds where does your finger stand now?

Integration

He lifts his hand and looks at the dividing line. He touches it gently. The empty space does not frighten him; it is a boundary that breathes. The finger settles on the edge, and the tension leaves his shoulders.

Mentor’s Box adults only

Articulation the lām, mīm, and sīn in a light, balanced tone.

Movement running the finger along a surface to the edge, stopping before the fall, noticing the skin’s tension and release.

Relational warmth slipping is not a mistake, but a discovery of boundaries.

Alternatives

  • sensitive → a surface covered in soft cloth.
  • high-energy → a fast pass, then a sudden stop.
  • Transition criterion: he stops before the edge without warning.

Discovery marker the child stops spontaneously before the edge.

English bridge Finger glides… skin tenses… edge breathes.

10. Steadying the Axis

تَرْجِيحُ المِحْوَرِ

Root ʿ-d-l يَعْدِلُ، العَدْلُ، مُعْتَدِلٌ · Vessel a dialogue (the board and the ground)

Anchor

A wooden board that tilts. Two feet standing on it. The world tilts. A head sways.

Transformation

The boy stands. The board tilts… and he staggers. He lets his knees bend. He shifts his weight. His feet converse with the ground.

Silent question 5 seconds where does your weight settle now?

Integration

The axis comes level again. The boy steps down and stands on the ground. His legs sink into the earth, and his shoulders drop without a sound.

Mentor’s Box adults only

Articulation the ʿayn, dāl, and lām in a balanced tone.

Movement standing on a tilting surface and shifting weight slowly.

Relational warmth swaying is the language of balance, not weakness.

Safety note a low, nearly flat board with a non-slip underside, set on the floor, with an adult within arm’s reach.

Safe-exit protocol if he loses his balance he steps down at once; no one holds him up by force.

Alternatives

  • weak balance → an almost-fixed board.
  • high-energy → a quick tilt, then stillness.
  • Transition criterion: he shifts his weight without help.

Discovery marker the child shifts his weight spontaneously to recover balance.

English bridge Board tilts… knees soften… feet root down.

11. The Firmness of the Base

ثُبُوتُ القَاعِدَةِ

Root th-b-t يَثْبُتُ، الثَّبَاتُ، ثَابِتٌ · Vessel a diary

Anchor

A foot trembling. Two feet set apart. The boy stands on one leg. He sways. His eyes fly open.

Transformation

He opens his feet. His pelvis drops. His thighs relax. The effort lightens. Standing becomes wide as a tree.

Silent question 4 seconds where is your weight distributed now?

Integration

He stays standing, and closes his eyes. The weight spreads through his feet like roots, and nothing can shake him.

Mentor’s Box adults only

Articulation the thāʾ, bāʾ, and tāʾ in a descending tone.

Movement moving from a narrow stance to a wide one.

Relational warmth swaying is not failure, but an exploration of the base.

Safety note stand beside a wall or a chair that can be touched, with an adult within reach.

Safe-exit protocol he puts the foot down whenever he wishes.

Alternatives

  • weak balance → a cushion under the heels.
  • high-energy → a light jump, then stillness.
  • Transition criterion: he widens his stance spontaneously.

Discovery marker the child adjusts his stance to become wider, spontaneously.

English bridge Feet spread… pelvis drops… bones settle.

12. Coldness and Warmth

بُرُودَةٌ وَدِفْءٌ

Root b-r-d يَبْرُدُ، البَرْدُ، بَارِدٌ · Vessel a dialogue (between the two palms)

Anchor

A cold glass surface. A warm cloth surface. Two palms reaching out. The right contracts; the left relaxes.

Transformation

The right hand waits on the cold, and the coldness fades, little by little.

The skin grows used to it. The two palms meet.

Silent question 5 seconds is the cold still cold?

Integration

The boy rubs his palms together and lays them on his cheeks. The cold and the warmth meet on his cheek in a single temperature.

Mentor’s Box adults only

Articulation the bāʾ, rāʾ, and dāl in a contrasting tone.

Movement touching two surfaces of different temperature, and waiting until the skin adjusts.

Relational warmth coldness is not an enemy, but a passing sensation.

Safety note “cold” as in room-cold, not ice; “warm” as in ablution water, not hot.

Alternatives

  • heat-sensitive → lukewarm water.
  • high-energy → quick switching between surfaces.
  • Transition criterion: he keeps his hand on the cold without withdrawing it.

Discovery marker the child keeps his hand on the cold without pulling it away.

English bridge Cold meets skin… fingers curl… palms soften.

13. The Stop After Running

تَوَقُّفُ الرَّكْضِ

Root r-k-ḍ يَرْكُضُ، الرَّكْضُ، رَاكِضٌ · Vessel a direct movement scene

Anchor

Two feet running. Suddenly they stop. The boy overshoots the point! His eyes widen.

Transformation

He tries again. He bends his knees the instant he stops. The jolt disappears.

He stands, and puts his hands on his knees. He feels a light warmth. The ground carries him.

Silent question 4 seconds where did the jolt go?

Integration

The weight descends into the ground, and his shoulders drop. The knees are soft, waiting for a new run.

Mentor’s Box adults only

Articulation the rāʾ, kāf, and ḍād in a sharp tone, then a soft one.

Movement a light run, a sudden stop, bending the knees to absorb the jolt.

Relational warmth overshooting is not a mistake, but the beginning of learning absorption.

Safety note a clear, level run-up of a few metres with a soft stop line; no running on wet or polished floors.

Safe-exit protocol if he is out of breath he stands and breathes slowly until he is ready.

Alternatives

  • sensitive → a foam mat.
  • high-energy → a shorter run, then a longer stop.
  • Transition criterion: he bends his knees spontaneously on stopping.

Discovery marker the child bends his knees spontaneously on coming to a stop.

English bridge Feet strike… knees bend… weight sinks.

14. The Length of the Arm

Discovery marker the child stops before the tremble the next time.

English bridge Arm reaches… tremor hums… shoulder drops.

15. A Tidy Fall

سُقُوطٌ مُرَتَّبٌ

Root s-q-ṭ يَسْقُطُ، السُّقُوطُ، سَاقِطٌ · Vessel participating narrator

Anchor

A big cushion on the floor. A back drawing near. “Shall we fall together?” “Yes, we fall.”

Transformation

The boy falls backward. He bounces a little. “The floor is moving!” “No, it is welcoming you.”

He falls again, slowly. The weight distributes. The back settles. The cushion carries him.

Silent question 5 seconds which part of your back touched the cushion first?

Integration

He closes his eyes and stays lying there. His chest rises and falls, and the cushion beneath him rises and falls with it.

Mentor’s Box adults only

Articulation the sīn, qāf, and ṭāʾ in a descending tone.

Movement a slow lying-down, distributing the weight over broad bony surfaces.

Relational warmth the small bounce is part of the physics of a safe landing. ☆

Safety note the child falls only onto a thick cushion or mat, one child at a time, from a sitting or kneeling height; no one falls onto another child and no one is pulled down.

Safe-exit protocol a child who prefers not to fall may lie down gently instead.

Alternatives

  • sensitive → two cushions to soften the stretch.
  • high-energy → a descent from sitting on a ball.
  • Transition criterion: he distributes his weight unguided.

Discovery marker the child distributes his weight over shoulders and pelvis spontaneously.

English bridge Body leans… weight spreads… back melts down.

20. The Sound of the Drop

Anchor

A source of steady dripping. Eyes closed. Listening before all else.

Transformation

I count… ☆ I stumble and laugh! I let go of counting.

I exhale with every falling sound. The sound counts itself, and my chest follows it.

Silent question 5 seconds does your exhale descend with the drop?

Integration

I open my eyes. The sound is still falling, and my chest rises and falls at its own pace.

Part Two · The Child’s Layer · The Literary Text Alone

Fifty-three stories for reading: the same texts with the scaffolding hidden, as the Arabic edition gives them for the child’s hands. No beats, no boxes, no questions: only the literature.

1. The Stretch of the Thread

مَدَّةُ الخَيْطِ

A cotton thread on the table. Its two ends far apart.

The boy stretches the thread between his palms. It tightens. Suddenly it coils around his finger. A short laugh escapes him.

He loosens his grip a little. His shoulders relax. The thread grows softer. The distance between his hands widens.

The boy returns the thread to the table. A faint trace of the coil stays around his finger, then slowly comes undone.

2. An Axis That Turns

مِحْوَرٌ يَدُورُ

Two feet planted. A torso beginning to turn around itself.

I am the spine. When you turn, I lean with you.

And when you speed up, your head sways like a branch in a wind.

You slow. You stop suddenly. You fix your eyes on a point.

The muscles of your back draw your head upward, and your feet sink into the ground.

The stillness stands in your axis. The ground beneath you, the point before you, and the dizziness withdrawing like water going back.

3. A Weight That Lightens

ثِقَلٌ يَخِفُّ

A light pillow in one palm, a small stone in the other. Eyes closed.

Today I lifted a pillow and a stone, with my eyes closed.

I tensed my muscles for the pillow… as if it were a boulder! I nearly laughed at my hand’s trick.

Then I lifted the stone, and my hand dipped a little.

I opened my eyes, and I understood: my muscles had been “fooled” by size, not by weight.

I set down the stone and the pillow. In my palms a small difference remained, fading with every breath.

4. A Flicker of Light

وَمْضَةُ نُورٍ

A faint light flickering on the wall. Two eyes following it.

The Eye: I stare at the light… and it escapes me!

The Light: Do not strain yourself. Blink.

The Eye: I blinked. I let the light pass. I began to see it without effort.

The eye closes, then opens. The light is still there, flickering at its leisure, and the eyelid is soft.

5. A Letter in Hiding

حَرْفٌ يَخْتَبِئُ

A page filled with tangled lines. A letter hiding among them.

The boy stares at the page · and the letters hide all the more!

He smiles at the tiredness of it, then blinks slowly.

Suddenly he looks at the empty space around the lines. The letter stands out. The shape becomes clear without effort.

He closes his eyes, then opens them onto something familiar. The empty space around him has become visible, the way things are visible.

6. The Warmth of the Word

دِفْءُ الكَلِمَةِ

A warm stone in one palm, a wrapped piece of ice in the other. Eyes closed.

You grip it hard… and it slips from between your fingers! The pebble leaps like a little fish.

You pick it up, and make your palm a bowl. The fingers calm. The pebble stays still in its slipperiness.

You leave the pebble on your open palm. The water runs off it, cold, and it does not slip.

22. The Melting of the Taste

ذَوَبَانُ الطَّعْمِ

A small piece on the tip of the tongue. No chewing.

A sharp sting! You laugh at the surprise.

You let the piece melt. The sting transforms. Something spreads. A faint trace remains.

You swallow gently, and breathe. On the tip of your tongue a trace remains, lightening with every breath.

23. The Direction of the Gust

اتِّجَاهُ الهَبَّةِ

The end of a light scarf in the hand. The rest belongs to the air.

You hold the end… and the scarf wraps around your arm! You laugh.

You loosen your wrist. A gentle pull comes, changing with every gust. Your wrist knows the direction before you do.

You slacken the scarf, and stand. The air passes, and your wrist sways lightly with it, hearing it, but not with your ear.

24. The Glint of the Light

وَمِيضُ الضَّوْءِ

A faint light source moving slowly.

You stare… and the glint passes, and you cannot catch it!

You let your eyes move without straining. Something behind your eyes lightens.

You close your eyes, then open them onto something familiar. The glint passes, and your eye follows it without tiring.

25. The Beats After Running

دَقَّاتُ مَا بَعْدَ الجَرْيِ

Fast movement for fifteen seconds. Then standing. Two palms on the chest.

The beats are so fast! I nearly laugh at their racket.

I exhale slowly. With every exhale, something slows beneath my palm.

I loosen my hands. The beats return to their leisure, and beneath my palm a light warmth remains, pulsing.

26. The Edge of the Two Surfaces

حَافَّةُ السَّطْحَيْنِ

The edge of two different surfaces. Two feet feeling the difference.

You shift your foot quickly… and slip! You laugh.

You come back. You stand on the edge. You wait. You shift the weight slowly. Something in the sole of your foot finds the ground.

You stand still on the new surface, and breathe. Your feet know now where one surface ends and another begins.

27. The Boat’s Wave

مَوْجَةُ القَارِبِ

A paper boat in a basin. A hand moving the water.

You push it hard… and it heels over, and the water splashes! You laugh.

You move it again, slowly. The water lifts it. Your wrist flows with the rhythm.

You steady the boat with a finger, and watch the water settle. The last wave touches the rim of the basin, then falls asleep.

28. The Shadow’s Standstill

وَقْفَةُ الظِّلِّ

A light run for fifteen seconds. Then a sudden stop.

You run after your shadow… and stumble a little! You laugh, panting.

You stand firm. The shadow falls beneath you. With every exhale, something in your chest widens. The shadow does not move.

You breathe freely. The shadow lies still at your feet, and your chest widens and narrows at its own pace.

29. An Echo That Dwells in the Bone

صَدًى يَسْكُنُ العَظْمَ

A light clap. Fingertips on a throat. A small whisper.

I am an echo. I was born of a laugh, or of a footfall on stone.

I set out into the air, and could no longer be heard… so where did I go?

I did not vanish. I became a faint tremor in the chest. The body keeps the tune by the stillness that follows it. I let the air carry the tremor, and my chest quivers with every echo that returns, then grows calm.

30. The Seedling That Refused to Hurry

النَّبْتَةُ الَّتِي رَفَضَتِ العَجَلَةَ

Squatting down. Two palms on soil. A gentle press, like asking permission.

Kalila was digging around a seedling when she pulled at its stem.

Kalila: “Come on, come up faster!”

Sindbad: “Don’t pull her… the root pushes from below.”

Kalila (laughing): “Ah! I always want everything now!”

Beneath the soil there is no silence, but a steady pushing. The seedling came up not because Kalila pulled it, but because she let the earth push. Beneath her palm, something small and green splits the soil, slowly.

31. A Dialogue Between a Palm and a Weight

حِوَارٌ بَيْنَ كَفٍّ وَثِقَلٍ

The end of a rope. A slow pull. Watching the weight.

The rope was heavy in Sindbad’s palm. Whenever he yanked it violently, the weight sank deeper.

He loosened his palm a little, and pulled to the rhythm of his breath: inhale… pull… exhale… hold. The rope began to walk with him, step by step, and the weight softened beneath his palm.

32. Two Hands Wide Enough for Emptiness

يَدَانِ تَتَّسِعَانِ لِلْفَرَاغِ

Two palms cupped like a bowl. A small space between them. A deep breath.

You look at your hands. There is nothing in them to touch, yet they are asking for a place.

The fingers part slowly. The palms open. The emptiness settles into them.

Your chest keeps the breath, and your hand keeps this emptiness without gripping it. The emptiness sits in your palms, light, like water that does not spill.

33. The First Dot

النُّقْطَةُ الأُولَى

A pen raised above a surface. A deep breath. A descent with the exhale.

My notebook is white… and the pen in my palm grows heavy before it touches the paper. I am afraid of leaving a wrong mark.

I exhale… and set down a small dot, like a raindrop. I do not gather my thoughts first; I let the pen touch, and the rest comes with the movement. On the paper is a single dot, and the pen is already waiting for the second.

34. At the Boundary

عِنْدَ الحَدِّ

Two index fingers facing each other. Drawing closer, very slowly.

Yasin: “Bring your finger closer… slowly.”

Nour: “Leave a space between us.”

(Yasin runs out of patience and touches!)

Yasin (laughing): “I rushed it!”

Nour: “I can feel the warmth of your finger… and we have not touched.”

The more they breathed together, the more the tension eased, and the air between the two fingers grew warm, thin as a thread.

35. What I Cannot See

مَا لَا أَرَاهُ

The end of a light cloth. Moving it slowly with the breath.

A light cloth in my hand. The air comes… and it quivers. The air stops… and it stills.

I cannot see the air with my eye… but my cloth sees it for me.

The cloth quivers, so I know the air is passing; it stills, so I know it has stopped. I keep the cloth in my palm, waiting for another gust to move it.

36. The Sound of “Here”

صَوْتُ «هُنَا»

A wooden bead. Turning it between fingers.

Sindbad (hurrying, and it falls): “Ah! Why won’t it settle?”

Kalila (laughing): “Because you’re rushing, grandfather!”

Sindbad: “You’re right… let me slow down.” (a light tap) “Yes… a tap that says: here.”

The slower he went, the clearer the sound of settling became. The bead turns between his fingers, then stops, and the last tap says: here.

37. A Trace in the Air

أَثَرٌ فِي الهَوَاءِ

A fine mist on a hand. An index finger moved slowly through the air.

Mist on my hand… cold, light, shining.

I raise my index finger and move it through the air: a loop… a wave… a circle!

Rishrish… rishrish…

I did not draw on paper… I drew in the air, and my skin kept the path. Rishrish… the cold line dries on my index finger, and its place stays a little warm.

38. Listen Downward

أَنْصِتِي إِلَى أَسْفَلَ

Standing firm. A palm on a knee.

I used to think that rising meant reaching the top quickly.

I raised my hand, proud: “I’ve arrived!”, and I wobbled, and laughed.

Sindbad gestured: “Listen downward first.”

I surrendered my weight to the ground first, then carried it upward, station by station. And when the sensation reached my head, I was light, as if I had grown up out of my own feet.

39. The Scent Surprises

الرَّائِحَةُ تُفَاجِئُ

A flower, or a peel. A quiet smelling.

I bring the peel close and smell…

I was expecting a sharp perfume, and instead I find the scent of wet earth! I laugh at the surprise.

The scent climbs with my breath, curls around my throat, then settles in my chest. The scent departs with the exhale, and its place in the chest stays warm.

40. Layer After Layer

طَبَقَةٌ بَعْدَ طَبَقَةٍ

A piece of fruit on the tip of a tongue. Melting.

I place the piece… and I scowl! The sourness invades. My lips pucker.

Then the warmth comes: the sharpness melts, and the sweetness flows in.

The taste transforms, layer after layer. I taste at leisure, to follow the journey. The last layer is a light sweetness, staying on the tip of my tongue.

41. A Sound in the Chest

صَوْتٌ فِي الصَّدْرِ

Two hands on a wooden box. Tapping.

Sindbad (striking hard): TUMM! “Ow! An awful sound!”

Kalila (laughing): “Because you’re fighting it! Tap gently…”

Sindbad (tapping softly): “Ah… the tremor climbs up my arm!”

Sound does not enter the ear alone; it is a tremor that climbs from the wood to the arm, and then the chest. Sindbad gives one last gentle tap, and it trembles in his chest, then grows still.

42. The Eye That Widens

العَيْنُ الَّتِي تَتَّسِعُ

Staring at something near for five seconds. Then lifting the gaze to the farthest point.

You stare… and your forehead tightens. The world narrows.

Then, hup!, you lift your gaze. Shhh… shhh… your brow breathes.

You do not chase the details; the view comes to you. The cord between your eyebrows loosens, and the gaze widens until the far enters it without effort.

43. A Palm Wide Enough for Opposites

رَاحَةٌ تَسَعُ الضِّدَّيْنِ

A right hand on something warm and smooth. A left hand on something cold and rough.

You touch the soft warmth… and the left hand is startled by the rough cold! It recoils… then returns.

You do not have to choose; you let the two converse.

The skin does not fear contradiction; it draws a map of warmth and cold at once. The two hands meet in your lap, and the warmth mingles with the cold into one quiet, middling temperature.

44. The Ankle’s Dance

رَقْصَةُ الكَاحِلِ

Standing on one foot. Eyes closed.

The world teeters! You lean, you nearly fall, and you laugh. You sway with your shadow.

The more your ankle loosens, the more the weight finds its way.

You do not stiffen your leg, the ankle plays. You place your palm on your chest, and breathe. The foot is steady, and the ankle still sways lightly beneath you, as if it were dancing.

45. The Air That Waits

الهَوَاءُ الَّذِي يَنْتَظِرُ

A deep inhale. Holding the air for one short moment.

His chest fills… he holds it. His cheeks puff out.

Holding is not a prison; it is a short pause. Then comes the long exhale, and the air goes out free, and the chest descends, and the cheeks soften.

46. A Sting, Then Warmth

لَسْعَةٌ ثُمَّ دِفْءٌ

Clapping · once hard, once lightly.

Sindbad (clapping hard): BUTBUT! “What is that sound?”

Kalila (laughing): “Grandfather claps like a duck! Try gently…”

Sindbad (clapping lightly): “Ah… a sting, then warmth!”

The clap is a tremor that travels; the sting wakes, and the warmth soothes. Sindbad lays his reddened palms on his chest, and the sting turns into a still warmth.

47. The Bend That Does Not Break

الانْحِنَاءُ الَّذِي لَا يَكْسِرُ

Bending forward, very slowly. Stopping at the first tightness.

You bend slowly… your back tightens. “Ah!”

Tiktik… so speak your vertebrae. You stop at the first tightness.

You do not pull yourself down; the breath lengthens you. One vertebra softens, then another. You come back up to standing, vertebra by vertebra, and in your back a quiet length remains.

48. The Throat’s Secret

سِرُّ الحَلْقِ

A hand on a throat. Whispering a long word.

I raise my hand to my throat and whisper: “a little secret…”

My voice is faint, but my throat trembles under my fingers. I nearly laugh at the tickle of the tremor.

Whispering is not weakness; it is a small strength touching the wall of the throat. And when I stop whispering, my throat grows still beneath my hand, and a light warmth remains where the tremor was.

49. The Hold of the Stone

مَسْكَةُ الحَجَرِ

A small stone in the hand. A hard squeeze, then an easing.

He gripped the stone hard… his fingers whitening from the pressure.

Suddenly the stone slips! He laughs at the surprise.

He picks it up again, and holds it gently. The stone becomes as if it were an extension of his hand.

Holding is not squeezing; when the joints relax, the pressure lightens. He puts the stone back, lays his palm on his chest, and breathes deeply. In his palm the trace of the stone remains, warm.

50. The Push of Steadiness

دَفْعَةُ الثَّبَاتِ

Standing before a wall, two palms on it, knees bent.

He pushes the wall hard from up high… and his foot slips! He nearly falls, and laughs.

He tries again, pressing his heels into the ground. The push climbs from the ground up through his body.

When he pushed from up high his foot slipped; and when he pressed with his heels he felt the ground pushing him back. He takes his hands away and stands, and the weight returns to his feet, quiet.

51. The Twist of the Torso

لَيُّ الجِذْعِ

Standing, hands on hips. Twisting the torso right and left, slowly.

He twists his torso fast… and his head spins. He laughs as he staggers.

He twists again, slowly. He stops at the first tightness in his waist.

The fast twist spins the head; the slow twist reveals the range. He comes back to standing, and his sides remain supple, breathing.

52. A Leap That Absorbs

وَثْبَةٌ تَمْتَصُّ

Bending two knees. A light jump.

He jumps… he lands hard. A jolt in his feet. His knees are rigid. He laughs at the shake.

He jumps again, and this time he bends his knees before the landing and through it. The impact lightens.

Jumping with a rigid knee is impact; with a soft knee, lightness. He stands, and his legs stay light, as if the ground had returned the jump to him gently.

53. The Rippling of the Air

تَمَوُّجُ الهَوَاءِ

Standing or sitting. Moving the arms in slow swimming strokes.

He moves his arms… and a soft sound comes: wash-wash. He smiles.

When he speeds up, the breath breaks up. He slows, and lets the arm widen with the inhale and return with the exhale.

When he hurried his chest narrowed, and when he slowed the whole body began to breathe. He loosens his arms and lays his palms on his chest. The wave calms in his arms, and his chest goes on widening and narrowing, like still water.
