Before You Begin — for Teacher & Parent

❧  Proactive caution. This book grows contentment. The contentment meant here is a heart's satisfaction and thankfulness together with lawful striving — not surrender to injustice or want, and not the killing of ambition. Teach the learner to be content in his heart and to strive with his hand, and never to stay silent about a right or a genuine need.
❦  How to use this book. Two tellings — brief (3 minutes) and full (7 minutes). The tale opens the Fruit: Noor has ripened, and now learns the first fruit that gladdens the heart — to be content with what she has.

The Heart of the Book

Noor entered the stage of the ripe Fruit, where the fruits of all she learned are gathered. The first fruit is contentment. Having become beautiful and fruiting, greed came whispering to her: more, more! But whoever always seeks more never rejoices in what he has. Contentment is not idleness, but a satisfaction that bequeaths a deep peace, and makes both speech and heart calm and grateful.

❦  Historical footnote. Umar ibn al-Khattab (d. 23 AH), the Commander of the Faithful, al-Fārūq, joined justice to abstemiousness and contentment and a rough simplicity of life; he would patch his garment and eat coarse food while he was the master of a nation. From him we draw that contentment is a dignity and a rest of heart, and that the richest of people is the most content. (In this fruit-world he is honoured through Grandmother Ramla's telling, not present in person.)

The Root & Its Kin — a Skill and a Language

❧  Body before symbol (the first CRT principle). The child meets this root through the body of contentment — a hand laid on the chest, a deep and easy breath, eyes that rest on the blessings at hand rather than straining toward the far-off. He does not parse letters; he feels his heart settle when he looks at what he has with satisfaction. The morphology below is for the teacher alone.
Root (the skill)Root family (words of the same lineage)
q – n – ʿqanāʿa · qāniʿ · qaniʿa · yaqnaʿu · qanūʿ
❦  How to explain it to the child. “The word qanāʿa (contentment) has siblings! One satisfied with what he has is qāniʿ; one much given to contentment is qanūʿ; and when you were satisfied with what you had, you qaniʿta. All one family from one father: q-n-ʿ.”
❦  Morphological note for the teacher. The root (q-n-ʿ) is sound. From it come qanāʿa (satisfaction with sufficiency) and iqnāʿ (leading another to be satisfied with a view). Point out to the teacher that contentment is an active, thankful satisfaction — not surrender, nor a giving-up of lawful striving for the better.
✦  Seed of Debate (a thread for the coming books). From the very same root (q-n-ʿ) comes iqnāʿ — persuasion, the leading of another to be satisfied with a view by proof and argument. And just as the learner is content with what he has (qanāʿa), he is content with the truth whenever it appears, even on another's tongue (contentment with the truth) — and that is the first manner of debate (munāẓara), the highest degree of cultivated dialogue, which grows in the books to come.

The Full Telling: “Noor's Contentment”

For reading aloud with the learner · seven minutes · with five original plates in the fruit palette

I. Noor has become a lovely fruiting tree, but greed whispers: more!

Noor had become a fruiting tree, bearing sweet and lovely fruits. She ought to have been glad. But she looked at a distant tree bearing more, and said: “Why are my fruits so few? I want more! I want as many as hers — no, more!”

And Noor forgot the beauty of her own fruit as she gazed at another's. Whatever she gained, she wanted more. She no longer rejoiced in anything, for her eye was always on what she did not have. Comparison stole her joy in what she owned.

II. The Greedy Mirage chases an illusion that never ends and is never content

And in the desert there was the Greedy Mirage. It gleamed in the far distance as though it were water and palms, so every thirsty one ran toward it; but the nearer he came, the farther it fled. It said to Noor as it shimmered:

“Run after me, Noor! More is always lovelier! Never be satisfied with what you have! Ask for more, then more, then more! Do not stop!”

Noor ran after the mirage, always asking for more. But she grew weary and gasped and never arrived, for greed is a mirage: whatever of it you gain, it asks for more. She discovered that whoever runs after more without contentment tires and never arrives, and loses his joy in what he has.

III. Grandmother Ramla brings Noor Umar ibn al-Khattab's wisdom of contentment

And Noor remembered what is told of the Commander of the Faithful, Umar ibn al-Khattab, of his contentment, his abstemiousness, and his satisfaction with sufficiency. And Grandmother Ramla was beside her, reminding her of his wisdom, and she said as if carrying his meaning:

“Noor, contentment is a treasure that never runs out. Whoever is content with what he has is the richest of people, and whoever always seeks more is the poorest of them, though he own the world. Look at what you have and give thanks, and you will be glad. Contentment is not that you cease to strive, but that your heart is satisfied and at peace.”

And Grandmother Ramla added: “Look at your fruits, Noor — each fruit was content with its place on the branch, and so ripened sweet. Had it spent its life envying another fruit, it would never have ripened. Be content with your place, and ripen sweet, and give thanks for every fruit within you.”

IV. Noor looks at her fruits with contentment and smiles, satisfied and thankful

Noor looked at her fruits — this time with the eye of contentment, not greed. And suddenly she saw how lovely they were! Sweet fruits, lush leaves, a strong trunk. She stopped looking at others' and began to give thanks for all she had. And when she looked at the other tree, she did not compete with it this time, but acknowledged the truth without needing to win: “Her fruit is sweeter in one kind, and mine is sweeter in another — each has its own beauty.” So she was content with the truth as she was content with her portion — and that is the first step toward a noble dialogue.

And a lovely thing happened: when Noor grew content, her heart settled, and the weariness of greed left her, and a deep rest took its place. She came to enjoy her fruits and to share them generously — for the content one finds at hand what suffices him and overflows. Contentment made her little much.

V. Noor is content and thankful, and discovers her water was near — and the mirage's gleam dies

Noor became content and thankful: satisfied with what she had, giving thanks for it, no longer comparing herself to others with regret. She said with peace: “I am content and satisfied.” — and she became among the gladdest in the garden, for her wealth was in her heart, not in her hand.

And the wonder happened: the Greedy Mirage saw Noor's peace when she ceased to run after it. And suddenly Noor noticed that her roots reached a sweet water beneath her — it had been near her all along, but she had looked far away and could not see it! And the mirage's gleam died down, for no one ran after it any longer. And for the first time it spoke truly and did not deceive, and whispered before it faded: “I used to promise you water in the distance, so you tired and never arrived; and the water was with you. Whoever is content, sees what he has.”

And the mirage learned — and the thirsty one with it — that contentment does not change the ground, but opens the eye to the blessing near at hand; so no far-off thing deceives us, and we drink our fill from what we have. And so Noor gathered the first fruit of wisdom: contentment — and her heart was at peace, and her fruit ripened sweet, and she made ready for what is more eloquent still.

❦  The Silence of the Bodily Question (4–5 seconds). “Look around you now, and count three things you have that you are thankful for. How does your heart feel when you look at what you have with contentment?”

Sub-Degree & Mastery Criterion (graduated)

A two-stage criterion. A ripening fruit (beginning) — gives thanks for what he has with a reminder. ← A ripening fruit (mastery) — expresses, unprompted, his contentment and gratitude, is freed from envious comparison and greed, and his heart is at peace.

❧  CRT Can-Do (a communicative criterion; CEFR B2 / ACTFL Intermediate High). The child can express an affective stance of contentment and gratitude in connected sentences, name the blessings he holds, resist envious comparison, and distinguish a peaceful contentment from a lawful striving for the better.

CASEL Behavioral Criteria (Self-Awareness & Self-Management)

  • Expresses his gratitude for what he has.
  • Does not compare himself to others with regret or envy.
  • Rejoices in what he has and enjoys it.
  • Distinguishes contentment from a lawful striving for the better.

The Four Inner Senses of Contentment

Contentment, too, is felt in the body before it is a skill. CRT awakens the child's interoception of the rest that comes when the heart is satisfied. Guide the child to notice, without naming the terms:

Inner senseWhat the child feels in contentmentA guiding whisper
The settling heartThe rest that spreads when he stops straining for more“Feel your heart settle, like a ripe fruit resting on its branch.”
The near blessingThe noticing of the good already at hand“Look near, not far — what good is right here with you?”
The eased breathThe deep, unhurried breath of one who is satisfied“Breathe slow and full — the breath of one who has enough.”
The overflowing handThe ease of sharing when the heart feels it has enough“Feel how, when you are content, your hand opens to give.”
❦  Why this matters. A child who feels the settling heart, the near blessing, the eased breath, and the overflowing hand owns the first fruit of wisdom — for contentment is a treasure that never runs out, and whoever is content with what he has is the richest of people.

Safe-Stop Protocol & Inclusion

❧  Safe-stop protocol. The contentment meant here is the heart's satisfaction and thankfulness — not surrender to injustice or want. We teach the learner to be content in his heart while he strives lawfully for the better, and that contentment does not mean staying silent about a right that has been taken or a genuine need.
ConditionSuggested alternative
Hard economic circumstancesPresent contentment as a rest for the heart, never as a justification of deprivation; take care that the needy learner feels no blame, but the worth of what he has beyond material things.
Autism spectrum (ASD)Embody contentment with concrete examples and a visual gratitude journal, and honour his special interests as a source of satisfaction.
A leaning to comparison (social media)Discuss the effect of comparison on social media, and train him to turn his gaze from others to his own blessings.
Anxiety or dissatisfactionLink to gratitude and breathing exercises, and reassure him that satisfaction is a skill learned by degrees.
Excessive ambitionBalance a heart's contentment with lawful striving, so ambition is not killed nor greed fed.
Cultural differenceRespect the differing expressions of gratitude and contentment across cultures.

Guide for Parent & Teacher

i. Before the session — the screen-box. Put devices away. Screens and social media feed comparison and greed by displaying what others have; we grow a content and thankful heart.

ii. During the session — the gratitude journal. Write together, each day, three things you are thankful for. Notice how contentment grows the more you count the blessings.

iii. After the session — the bedtime thanks. Before sleep, ask the learner: what is the loveliest thing you have that you are thankful for today? Make gratitude the habit you close the day with.

❦  Make it measurable. Record on the fridge ledger: “How many times did our child express contentment and thanks for what he has, instead of complaint or comparison?” A star for each. Count the stars at week's end.

“The screen shows you what others have, and makes you greedy. We teach the child to see his blessings and be content.”

The Juthur Scale (JPS) — Book Sixteen Unit

❧  Important. The Juthur Scale is a pedagogical observation tool under development — not a psychological or medical diagnosis. Its readings are advisory and pass no verdict upon the child.
❦  How the measure grows. The Juthur Scale (JPS) has five cumulative dimensions. The four earlier stages are complete. This book — the first of the Fruit — adds a mature dimension of self-awareness: contentment and gratitude.

Filled twice, by two independent raters where possible. The marks are behaviorally anchored:

MarkBehavioral anchor (what you actually observe)
○  rarelyComplains and compares; is never satisfied with what he has.
◐  sometimesExpresses thanks only when reminded; is not yet freed from envious comparison unprompted.
●  oftenUnprompted, expresses contentment and gratitude, is freed from comparison, and his heart is at peace.
❦  Discrepancy-resolution protocol (inter-rater reliability). On agreement, record it. On a one-step difference, record the lower and note it. On a two-step difference, the two observe one shared session together, reconcile against the anchors, and record the agreed mark. Note the count of two-step disagreements.
