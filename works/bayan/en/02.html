Before You Begin · for Teacher & Parent

❧  Proactive health caution. This book involves making sounds and touching the throat. Review health conditions: a child with throat sensitivity, hoarseness, or inflammation keeps to whispering without straining the voice. Never force a child with Selective Mutism to make a sound, it is enough for him to touch his own throat, or a vibrating surface (a tapped wooden box), to feel the tremor. Touching another person's throat is only ever done with their permission.
❦  How to use this book. Two tellings · brief (3 minutes, for the youngest) and full (7 minutes). The tale continues Book One directly: having learned to breathe, Noor now learns that her breath can become a voice.

The Heart of the Book

After discovering his breath, the child discovers that this breath can tremble and become a voice. Voice is not loud noise, it is an honest vibration that carries the self into the world. And the smallest true voice is more eloquent than the loudest hollow thunder.

❦  Historical footnote. Al-Khalil ibn Ahmad al-Farahidi was an Arab scholar of the second Hijri century, founder of the science of prosody (ʿarūḍ) and of the phonetic study of Arabic; he arranged the letters by their points of articulation, from the throat to the lips. From him we draw here the precise listening to how a sound is born.

The Root & Its Kin · a Skill and a Language

❧  Body before symbol (the first CRT principle). The child meets this root through the body, never through analysis. He does not parse letters; he feels the voice being born · the phonetic sensation of ṣ-w-t: the airy hiss of ṣ passing with no tremor, and the humming t and the round w that make the throat quiver. He learns by laying a palm on his throat, not by naming a pattern. The morphology below is for the teacher alone.
Root (the skill)Root family (words of the same lineage)
ṣ – w – tsawt · aṣwāt · ṣawwata · muṣawwit
❦  How to explain it to the child. “The word sawt (voice) has siblings! When you make a sound you are ṣawwata-ing, and you are a muṣawwit (a sound-maker). All the sounds together we call aṣwāt. One family from one father: ṣ-w-t.”
❦  Morphological note for the teacher. The root (ṣ-w-t) is hollow · its middle radical is the weak letter wāw. The wāw is silent in “sawt” and vowelled in the plural “aṣwāt.” This is a natural variation of the hollow root, noted for the teacher without burdening the child.

The Full Telling: “Noor's Voice”

For reading aloud with the child (ages 5–6) · seven minutes · with five original plates in the seed palette

I. Noor has awakened and learned the breath, but has no voice yet

After Noor learned to breathe, she opened her eyes for the first time. She felt something small stirring in her chest, wanting to come out… but she did not know how.

She tried to say “Good morning” to the rain, but only air came out, with no sound, a faint whisper like a breeze. Noor grew a little sad and said to herself: “Do I even have a voice?”

She laid her palm on her chest, as she had learned last time, and felt the breath fill it. Then she traced it with her palm as it rose: from the chest… upward… to the throat, warm, as if searching for a way out. “My breath rises to here… so what if it trembled?”

II. The Hollow Thunder roars, and Noor feels her voice is worthless

Suddenly the Hollow Thunder roared in the sky. Inside, it feared no one would hear it, so it thought shouting was the answer. Its voice was very loud, filling the place:

“I am the loudest voice in the sky! BOOM! Who hears your tiny voice, Noor? The beautiful voice is the loudest, the strongest, the biggest! BOOM!”

Noor hid behind a leaf. Her small voice seemed worthless before that huge roar. And every time she tried to make a sound, she remembered the Thunder and fell silent.

III. Al-Khalil and Grandmother Ramla place their palms on their throats and teach Noor

Beside the soil sat the wise Al-Khalil ibn Ahmad, the man who gathers sounds as others gather pearls. And with him Grandmother Ramla, whom we know from before.

Al-Khalil said calmly: “Noor, a voice is not a size, but a vibration. Put your hand on your throat, or feel your trunk, and see what happens.”

Grandmother Ramla laid her palm on her throat and said: “Try with me. When I say ssssss… · touch: does my throat quiver?”

“No… it doesn't quiver. This is a sound that does not vibrate the throat (adults call it voiceless) · air passing without a tremble.”

Then Grandmother said: “Now I say: zzzzzz… or I hum mmmmm… touch my throat now.”

“It's quivering! Your throat trembles, Grandmother! This is a sound with a pulse that vibrates the throat (adults call it voiced) · a breath that trembles into a voice!”

IV. Noor touches her throat and feels the tremor for the first time

Noor laid her small palm on her throat. She took a breath, as she had learned last time, then made it tremble: “mmmmm…”

And suddenly… she felt the tremor beneath her palm! A small, warm vibration. Her voice! Her very own, not the Thunder's, not anyone else's. Small, yes, but real, and hers alone. And so Noor made a sound for the first time, she became a little sound-maker, her breath trembling into a voice.

V. Noor releases her first voice, and the Thunder quiets to listen

Noor said, in a small, trembling but clear voice: “Good morning.” It was the first real voice of her life.

And something wondrous happened: the Hollow Thunder stopped roaring… and listened. At first it did not understand why it had stopped. Noor's voice was too small to be heard, and yet… it heard it. It asked itself, puzzled: “Why did I listen to a voice so small? I, who fill the sky with roaring!”

It listened again… and discovered the secret for itself: “Ah… her voice has something in it, a meaning, a light. But my roar, however loud… is empty. I shouted because I feared no one would hear me. But a voice is not heard by its loudness, rather by what it carries.” And for the first time, the Thunder lowered its roar, not from shame, but because it had understood.

And the Thunder learned, from itself, not from anyone, that the true voice is not the loudest, but the one that carries a heart.

❦  The Silence of the Bodily Question (4–5 seconds). “Place your palm on your throat. Say: mmmm… Do you feel the tremor? That is your voice waking up, your voice, yours.”

The Brief Telling (for the youngest · 3 minutes)

For a child of four · short sentences, the selfsame meaning.

Noor had learned to breathe. But when she wanted to speak, air came out with no sound.

The Thunder roared loudly: “BOOM! Your voice is small, it can't be heard!” So Noor fell silent.

Grandmother Ramla said: “Put your hand on your throat, and say: mmmm.” Noor did… and felt the tremor!

Noor said in a small, true voice: “Good morning!” The Thunder listened, and learned that a small, honest voice is more beautiful.

Sub-Degree & Mastery Criterion (graduated)

A two-stage criterion. An awakening seed (beginning)– makes a simple sound with a reminder. ← An awakening seed (mastery)– touches his throat and tells for himself the sound that vibrates it (zzz) from the one that does not (sss). This paves the way to Book Three (the Gaze).

❧  CRT Can-Do (a communicative criterion; CEFR pre-A1 / ACTFL Novice Low). The child can make a small honest voiced sound, hand on the throat, to send another the message “I am here, and this is my voice,” choosing truth over loudness, even without full words.

CASEL Behavioral Criteria (Self-Awareness)

  • Names his voice as “his” and expresses pride in it.
  • Pauses before raising his voice, realizing the loud is not the more beautiful.
  • Tells, by touching his throat, the sound that vibrates it from the one that does not.
  • Shows readiness to repeat making a sound and asks for it himself.

The Four Inner Senses of the Voice

The voice is felt before it is heard. CRT awakens the child's interoception– the inner senses by which the body knows its own sound. Guide the child to notice, without naming the terms:

Inner senseWhat the child feels in the voiceA guiding whisper
The tremor (interoception)The small quiver under the palm when the sound is voiced“Where under your hand does the buzz live?”
WarmthThe warm breath turning into a warm sound at the throat“Is your ‘mmm’ warm inside your throat?”
Weight (soft vs. strong)The difference between a whisper's lightness and a hum's fullness“Make it as light as a feather, then as full as a bee.”
BalanceSteadying a sound so it is neither a shout nor a vanishing breath“Hold your voice level, like carrying water without spilling.”
❦  Why this matters. A child who can feel the tremor, warmth, weight, and balance of his own voice owns it from the inside, and will never mistake loudness for truth.

Safe-Stop Protocol & Inclusion

❧  Safe-stop protocol. If the child's voice is strained or his throat is uncomfortable, stop at once and return to whispering or comfortable silence. Voice is an invitation, never an obligation, and never breath-holding or shouting.
ConditionSuggested alternative
Hearing impairmentFocus entirely on tactile vibration: palm on the throat or on a vibrating instrument, the lesson is originally sensory, not auditory.
Selective MutismNo sound is ever required; he touches his own throat or a tapped box to feel the vibration, and that suffices without pressure.
Hoarseness / throat sensitivityKeeps to whispering (voiceless) only; voiced sounds wait until the throat is rested.
Hyperactivity / attentionTie it to a game: “make the bee buzz mmmm” for a short spell.
Autism spectrum (ASD)Use a visual timer, reduce sudden sounds; the Thunder scene may be skipped if it distresses him.
Social anxietyMakes his sound alone first, then with a single companion, before any group.

Guide for Parent & Teacher

i. Before the session · the screen-box, then listening. First place all devices in a closed box, as in Book One; a true voice cannot be discovered amid screen-noise. Then sit a moment in silence and listen together for the smallest sound in the room (a clock, a breath, a bird), to ready the child's ear to prize small sounds.

ii. During the session · the shared vibration. Place the child's palm on your throat as you hum, then his palm on his own throat. The sensory comparison is the lesson. Do not correct the “beauty” of his voice, celebrate merely its existence.

iii. After the session · the voiced/voiceless game. Through the day, play: “Does this sound vibrate the throat or not?” (zzz voiced, sss voiceless, fff voiceless, mmm voiced).

❦  Make it measurable. Record on the fridge ledger: “How many new sounds did our child discover today?”, a star for each voiced sound he tells apart himself. Count the stars at week's end.

The Juthur Scale (JPS) · Book Two Unit

❧  Important. The Juthur Scale is a pedagogical observation tool under development · not a psychological or medical diagnosis. Its readings are advisory and pass no verdict upon the child.
❦  How the measure grows. The full Scale (JPS) has five dimensions raised cumulatively. Book One measured body awareness and the dawn of presence. This book adds a new dimension · vocal expression– and briefly reviews the prior one. Thus the scale grows with the child.

Filled twice · before beginning, and after three weeks · by two independent raters. The marks are behaviorally anchored:

MarkBehavioral anchor (what you actually observe)
○  rarelyFalls silent or only whispers air; does not link breath to a voiced sound.
◐  sometimesMakes a voiced sound only when the guide cues it; not yet on his own.
●  oftenUnprompted, lays a hand on his throat and makes a clear voiced sound, and tells it from a voiceless one.
❦  Discrepancy-resolution protocol (inter-rater reliability). Compare the two raters item by item. Where they agree, record the mark. Where they differ by one step, record the lower and note it. Where they differ by two steps (○ vs ●), do not average: the two observe one shared session together, reconcile against the anchors, and record the agreed mark. Note the count of two-step disagreements.

Questions of Understanding

  • Why did only air come out, with no sound, when Noor first tried to speak? recall
  • Why did the Hollow Thunder listen to Noor's small voice in the end? inference
  • Place your palm on your throat and make a sound that vibrates it, then one that does not, which is the voiced one? sensory-motor
  • Have you ever felt both shy and proud when you spoke up softly? simultaneous emotions
  • The Hollow Thunder was afraid no one would hear it, so it shouted. Have you ever felt you had to be loud to be noticed? What could we say to the Thunder to help it feel heard without shouting? empathy for the other
  • When is your small voice more beautiful than a loud one? personal link
My voice is an honest tremor, not loud noise; and the smallest voice with my heart in it is more eloquent than a hollow thunder.

❦  Behavioral translation (not a slogan only). Teach the child the rule in three steps: (1) Place your palm on your throat. (2) Take a breath, then let it tremble into a clear sound. (3) Say what you want with truth, not loudness. The Thunder listened to Noor because her voice carried meaning, not because it was loud.

The Twelve Zero-Standards

The covenant of “nothing missing, nothing forced”, twelve zeroes the book must hold.

  • Zero logical error– the tale runs silence → discouragement → discovery → true voice, unbroken.
  • Zero moral coercion– making a sound is an invitation; the mute child is never pressed.
  • Zero structural contradiction– the two sub-degrees are consistent with the arc.
  • Zero psychological gap– Noor feels “worthless,” then owns her voice; the emotion fits the age.
  • Zero disconnected episode– a bidirectional bridge binds this book to One and Three.
  • Zero passive activity– every skill becomes a throat-touch, a felt tremor.
  • Zero stock icon– the seal and all five plates are original vector art.
  • Zero symbolic error– breath → tremor → voice; the metaphor holds throughout.
  • Zero psychological miscue– the Thunder learns to listen; it is understood, not defeated.
  • Zero architectural break– identity → root → tale → application → measure → review is whole.
  • Zero cognitive coercion– “a voice is a vibration, not a size” is discovery, not dictation.
  • Zero cultural imbalance– a male scholar (Al-Khalil) and a female elder (Ramla) share the wisdom.
