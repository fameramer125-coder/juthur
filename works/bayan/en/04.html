The Juthur system · The Art of Expression: the twin skills of speaking and dialogue, in twenty books. Noor has become a bud. Carrying a breath, a voice, and a gaze, she now begins the first true skill of dialogue: to listen. The teaching runs through Concrete Reading Theory (CRT; نَظَرِيَّةُ الْقِرَاءَةِ الْمَلْمُوسَةِ, Amer 2024), a language lived through the body and presence.

The Single Thread · the cord binding the twenty books is Presence. In the Seed, presence turned inward and toward the eye: breath, voice, gaze. Now, in the Bud, presence turns fully toward the other: to listen, to receive a whole self before answering it. The gaze opened the eyes to the other; listening opens the heart. It reaches back to the gaze and forward to the question.

Before You Begin · for Teacher & Parent

❧  Proactive caution. This book trains attentive listening and settled sitting. A child with hyperactivity may find long stillness hard. Accept a short listening (two seconds), increase it gradually, and do not read movement as a failure to listen.
❦  How to use this book. Two tellings: a brief telling (3 minutes) and a full telling (7 minutes). The tale continues directly: Noor has become a bud, carrying breath, voice, and gaze, and now she learns to listen.

The Heart of the Book

Having learned breath, voice, and gaze, and having become a bud, Noor begins the first skill of real communication: listening. Speech is half the conversation; its lovelier half is to listen. Whoever does not listen speaks much and understands nothing.

❦  Historical footnote. Badr al-Din Ibn Jama'a (d. 733 AH) was a great judge and scholar. He composed Tadhkirat al-Sāmiʿ wa'l-Mutakallim fī Adab al-ʿĀlim wa'l-Mutaʿallim (“A Reminder for the Listener and the Speaker on the Manners of the Scholar and the Student”), a treatise that gave the manners of learning, listening included, firm foundations. From him we draw that fine listening is the key to knowledge. What Judge Ibn Jama'a says in the story is the author's own composition, not a text transmitted from him; Grandmother Ramla is a fictional character.

The Root & Its Kin · a Skill and a Language

❧  Body before symbol (the first CRT principle). The child meets this root through the body of listening, never through analysis. He does not parse letters; he listens: a straight back, a face turned to the speaker, a held silence until the end. The anchor is a felt act of receiving. The morphology below is for the teacher alone.
Root (the skill)Root family (words of the same lineage)
s-m-ʿsamʿ · sāmiʿ · masmūʿ · sammāʿa · istamaʿa
❦  How to explain it to the child. “The word samʿ (hearing) has siblings! One who listens well is a sāmiʿ; words that arrived are masmūʿ; the tool a doctor hears with is a sammāʿa (a stethoscope); and when you listen with all of you, you say istamaʿtu. One family from one father: s-m-ʿ.”
❦  Morphological note for the teacher. The root (s-m-ʿ) is sound. From it come samʿ (perceiving sound with the ear) and istimāʿ (listening with intent and attention). The difference is worth noting gently: we hear much, but we listen little.

The Full Telling: “Noor's Listening”

For reading aloud with the child · seven minutes · with five original plates in the bud palette

I. Noor has become a bud; she talks a great deal and listens to no one

Noor had become a lovely green bud! And with her was all she had learned: a breath, a voice, and a gaze. She grew to love talking very much. She talked and talked; but whenever anyone else spoke, she was busy preparing her reply and did not listen.

The rain told her something important, but she did not listen; she was busy with what she herself would say. So she did not understand what the rain wanted, and answered with a reply that did not fit.

II. The Garbled Echo answers before it hears, and chaos spreads

That day the Garbled Echo drifted by. It answered everything before hearing it whole, for it feared being thought slow, and so its words got mixed and clashed. Someone asked it, “Have you eaten?” and it answered before the end of the question: “Yes, the weather is nice!” It had replied to half of what it heard.

“Listening is slow, Noor! Reply fast like me, don't wait for anyone to finish. The quickest to answer is the cleverest!”

Noor tried the Echo's way: she began replying before anyone finished. But her replies fell out of place, and everyone laughed, and she was sad. No one understood her, because she had understood no one.

III. Judge Ibn Jama'a and Grandmother Ramla listen to Noor with full attention

Beside the soil sat the judge Ibn Jama'a, who wrote down for people the manners of the scholar and the student, and taught how a learner listens with courtesy in order to learn. And with him Grandmother Ramla, whom we know from before.

Noor spoke, and Ibn Jama'a and Grandmother listened to her calmly, without interrupting, until she finished. And for the first time, Noor felt that her words… arrived.

Then Ibn Jama'a said gently: “Noor, do you know why your words arrived just now? Because we listened to you. This is how knowledge is built: by the manners of listening! Whoever listens well learns more. Try: sit with a straight back, look at me, and listen until I finish before you reply.”

IV. Noor sits upright and listens, and a leaf opens from her like an ear

Noor sat with a straight back, fixed her eyes on the face of the one speaking to her, as she had learned in the gaze, and listened calmly until he finished. And a lovely thing happened: a small leaf unfurled from her, like an ear that receives the world.

And for the first time, Noor heard what she had never heard before: she heard what lay behind the words. She heard the rain saying it was tired, and the wind saying it was lonely. Listening opened for her a door into the hearts of others.

V. Noor answers beautifully after listening, and the Echo quiets and learns

After Noor had listened well, she spoke. And her answer this time was lovely and fitting, because she had understood first. She said, in the voice she had learned and with an honest gaze: “I listen to you, so I understand.”

Then the Garbled Echo stopped… and listened, for the first time. And as it listened, its words became clear and ordered. It murmured: “I used to reply fast, so I neither understood nor was understood. Now I have listened… and I understand.”

And the Echo learned that a lovely reply begins with lovely listening. From that day, Noor the bud listened before she spoke, and everyone around her loved her.

❦  The Silence of the Bodily Question (4–5 seconds). “Sit with a straight back, and listen to any sound around you for five seconds. What did you hear that you never noticed before?”

The Brief Telling (for the youngest · 3 minutes)

Short sentences, the selfsame meaning.

Noor became a bud, and loved talking very much, but she listened to no one.

The Garbled Echo passed, replying before it heard; Noor copied it, and no one understood her.

Judge Ibn Jama'a said: “Sit with a straight back, and listen until I finish before you reply.”

Noor listened… and heard what lay behind the words! Then she said, “I listen to you, so I understand.” And the Echo listened, and learned.

Sub-Degree & Mastery Criterion (graduated)

A two-stage criterion. A closed bud (beginning): listens a moment before replying, with a reminder. ← A closed bud (mastery): sits with a straight back, listens until the speaker finishes, then replies unprompted, and paraphrases what he heard. (The child may count silently to five if he likes: a gentle suggestion, not a rule.)

❧  CRT Can-Do (a communicative criterion; CEFR A1 / ACTFL Novice Mid). The child can listen to a partner to the end, then show he understood by briefly restating what he heard (“I heard that you…”) before he gives his own reply.

CASEL Behavioral Criteria (Social Awareness)

  • Listens without interrupting the speaker until he finishes.
  • Waits his turn to speak, without haste.
  • Paraphrases what he heard before he answers (“I heard that you…”).
  • Sits with a straight back, his face toward the speaker.

The Four Inner Senses of Listening

Listening, too, is felt in the body before it is a skill. CRT awakens the child's interoception of attention. Guide the child to notice, without naming the terms:

Inner senseWhat the child feels in listeningA guiding whisper
Stillness (settling)The quiet of a body that has grown still to receive“Let your body grow calm, like a bud in the rain.”
The waiting (the pause)The held pause of five seconds before a reply“Wait, and count softly to five before you answer.”
Hearing behind wordsSensing the feeling beneath what is said“What did his voice feel, under the words?”
The upright bodyThe alert steadiness of a straight back turned to the speaker“Sit tall and turned toward your friend, like an open ear.”
❦  Why this matters. A child who feels the stillness, the waiting, the meaning beneath words, and the upright turning owns the door to every heart, and answers only after he has truly received.

Safe-Stop Protocol & Inclusion

❧  Safe-stop protocol. If the child feels bored or unable to sit, stop and shorten the listening time. Listening is an invitation, not a punishment, and long sitting is not the goal, but attending to the other with affection is.
ConditionSuggested alternative
Hyperactivity / attentionA quiet movement (a stress ball) is allowed while listening; shorten to two seconds and increase gradually.
Hearing impairmentRely on watching the speaker's face, lip-reading, and gesture; here listening is visual, not only auditory.
Autism spectrum (ASD)Show what is expected with a picture or a schedule, reduce distractions, and accept listening without direct eye contact.
Social anxietyBegin listening in a familiar pair before a group, without demanding an immediate reply.
Language-learning difficultySimplify the paraphrase sentence; accept expression in a single word or a gesture.
Cultural differenceRespect differing norms of listening; some cultures listen in full silence, some with encouraging echoes.

Guide for Parent & Teacher

i. Before the session · the screen-box. Put devices away. The screen speaks and does not listen, so we teach the child what the screen cannot: to listen to a human being.

ii. During the session · the game of the lovely echo. After one of you speaks, the child repeats what he heard: “I heard that you…” This turns listening from passive silence into active understanding.

iii. After the session · five golden seconds. Train the habit: after the speaker stops, we wait five seconds before replying. Notice how speech grows calmer and deeper.

❦  Make it measurable. Record on the fridge ledger: “How many times did our child listen to the end without interrupting?” Give a star for each. Count the stars at week's end.

“The screen teaches the child to reply fast. We teach him to listen deeply.”

Questions of Understanding

  • What did the closed bud do in silence before it opened? recall
  • Why did the Garbled Echo not understand what was said to it? inference
  • Listen to a sound around you for five seconds. What did you hear that you never noticed before? sensory
  • Have you ever felt eager to reply and unsure at once, before someone finished speaking? simultaneous emotions
  • The Echo replied fast because it feared being slow. Have you ever rushed to answer to seem clever? What could help the Echo feel calm enough to listen first? empathy for the other
  • When did you feel someone truly listened to you? How did it feel? personal link
Before I answer, I listen; for whoever listens well, answers beautifully.

❦  Behavioral translation (not a slogan only). Teach the child: “When another is speaking, count silently to five after he stops, then speak.” Listening begins with not rushing the reply, as Noor learned.

Craft & Media

The drawing. Ask the child to draw a large ear, and around it all the lovely sounds he loves to listen to (his mother's voice, the rain, a bird, a story).

The play. Two children: one tells something that happened to him, the other listens, then repeats what he heard. Did he get it right? Then they trade roles.

Listening. There is no recording of this book yet. Read the tale aloud, slowly, and keep the pauses. The Listening room gathers the recordings that exist.

The Bud's Listening

To give children a calm place beside the screens, and return them to the body, the voice, the gaze, and the gift of true listening.

Noor has become a bud. She can breathe, speak, and see, but she loves talking so much that she listens to no one, and her replies fall out of place. When the Garbled Echo urges her to answer before hearing, a wise judge and a grandmother teach her, by an upright body and a held silence, to listen to the very end, until a new leaf opens from her like an ear and she hears what lies behind the words. A tangible, sensory tale in the tradition of Concrete Reading Theory: no screen, no drill, only two selves, and the gift of being truly heard.

“I listen to you, so I understand.”
