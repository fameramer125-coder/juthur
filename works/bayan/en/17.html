Before You Begin · for Teacher & Parent

❧  A principle not to be broken. Emphasis is not raising the voice. He who stresses every word is like one who stresses nothing: the meaning is lost in the din. Emphasis is to light one word so the listener turns to it. This builds on the “tone” (nabra) the learner touched in the reading journey (the Sentence-Bridge), and raises it into a conscious skill of oratory.
❦  How to use this book. Two tellings · brief (3 minutes) and full (7 minutes). In the age of screens everything shouts: bold headlines, all-capital words, jostling notifications. The adolescent may lose the sense of the lit word. We restore the art of the quiet highlight, so that a speech has one summit people remember.

The Heart of the Book

Noor now learns emphasis: to light the important word. In the age of screens, everything is bold and everything is loud, so nothing stands out. The skill is the quiet highlight: a speech with one summit that the listener carries away. He who lights the whole lamp leaves no shadow for his stars to shine in; dim the light a little, and light up one word.

❦  Historical footnote. Quss ibn Saʿida al-Iyadi, the orator of the Arabs, was proverbial for an eloquence delivered from the minbar and remembered by all who heard it. From him we draw that the mark of the master speaker is not a flood of raised words, but the one lit word that turns every face. (In this fruit-world he is honoured through Grandmother Ramla's telling, not present in person.)

The Root & Its Kin · a Skill and a Language

❧  Body before symbol (the first CRT principle). The child meets this root through the body of the lifted hand: a hand that rises a little at the one important word and then returns to rest, not a hand that flails at every word. He does not parse letters; he feels the one word lift, as if it stood on a small pulpit within his sentence. The morphology below is for the teacher alone.
Root (the skill)Root family (words of the same lineage)
n-b-rnabr · nabra · minbar · nabara · manbūr
❦  How to explain it to the child. “From n-b-r comes the minbar, a raised place that lights up the one who stands on it. And a nabra is a small rise in your voice that lights up a word. When you emphasize (nabara) a word, you set it on a little pulpit in the middle of your sentence.”
❦  Morphological note for the teacher. Distinguish for the learner between emphasis (lighting a word by meaning) and raising the voice (an aimless loudness). The first is a skill; the second, as he learned in Book Two, is noise pollution. Emphasis serves the listener; loudness overwhelms him.

The Full Telling: “Noor's Emphasis”

For reading aloud with the learner · seven minutes · with five original plates in the fruit palette

I. Noor stresses every word with force, and it becomes a wall of noise

At the recitation contest, Noor, now grown, stood to deliver her piece about the tree. She spoke it stressing every word with a high force… so the speech came out as one solid wall of noise, and the listeners remembered none of it.

She wondered: “I said everything with force… so why did nothing stick?” When every word blazes at the same brightness, the eye finds no summit to rest upon, and the whole is forgotten. A speech with no lit word is a wall, not a window.

II. The Lightning flashes without cease, until eyes go blind to it

And in the sky the Lightning flashed, boasting: “I flash without cease! My light burns from beginning to end!” So it flashed without a pause… until the eyes went blind from the endless glare, and could no longer see a single flash.

Noor tried the Lightning's way: she lit every word. But when all is lit, nothing is lit; when all is loud, nothing is heard. She discovered that a light that never rests is a light that is never seen.

III. Grandmother Ramla brings Noor the wisdom of Quss ibn Saʿida and the pulpit

And Noor remembered what is told of Quss ibn Saʿida al-Iyadi, the orator of the Arabs, whose one lit word from the pulpit turned every face. And Grandmother Ramla was beside her, reminding her of his eloquence, and she said as if carrying his meaning:

“Noor, when you light the whole lamp, no shadow is left for your stars to shine in. Dim your light a little… and light up one word. Set one word upon its little pulpit, and let the rest rest quietly around it, and every face will turn to it.”

And when the Lightning marvelled, “How can a single quiet flash overcome all my glare?” Grandmother Ramla said: “The flash that comes after stillness is seen; a constant light is not. So it is with the emphasized word among quiet words.”

IV. Noor lights up one word only, and every face turns to it

So Noor delivered her sentence again, and lit up one word only:

“The tree gives… and awaits no thanks.”

The word “thanks” rose a little above the rest, and every face turned to it, and it lodged in the hearts. Noor saw that one lit word, set among quiet words, is more eloquent than a whole sentence shouted, for the summit is seen only when the slopes are still.

V. The Lightning learns to flash once after stillness; Noor chooses her lit word before every sentence

And the Lightning grew calm. It came to flash one flash after a stillness: so people awaited it and saw it, and loved it. It whispered: “I used to flash without cease, so no one saw me; now I flash once after quiet… and every eye finds me.”

And from that day, Noor came to choose, before every sentence, which word she would light. She learned that the place of the emphasis changes the whole meaning, and that one lit word, quietly raised, does what a hundred shouted words cannot. And so Noor gathered the second fruit of eloquence: emphasis, and made ready to order her whole speech to persuade.

❦  The Silence of the Bodily Question (4–5 seconds). “Take a sentence you want to say. Which single word matters most in it? Lift only that word a little with your voice, and feel how the meaning sharpens.”

The Brief Telling (for the youngest · 3 minutes)

The same tale, in shorter sentences.

Noor delivered her speech stressing every word with force, so nothing stuck in the listeners' minds.

The Lightning flashed without cease, until the eyes went blind and saw no single flash.

Grandmother Ramla, carrying the eloquence of Quss ibn Saʿida, taught her: “Light up one word.”

So Noor emphasized the word “thanks” alone, and every face turned and remembered. Do not raise every word; light up one.

Sub-Degree & Mastery Criterion (graduated)

A two-stage criterion. A fruit taking colour (beginning): emphasizes the important word with guidance. A fruit taking colour (mastery): chooses the key word himself, and moves the place of the emphasis to change the meaning on purpose.

❧  CRT Can-Do (a communicative criterion; CEFR B2 / ACTFL Intermediate High). The child can select the key word in an utterance, mark it with a measured prosodic emphasis, move that emphasis to alter meaning intentionally, and justify his choice by the listener's need.

CASEL Behavioural Criteria (Relationship Skills & Social Awareness)

  • Understands that highlighting a word helps the listener, and does not impose upon him.
  • Chooses what he emphasizes by what matters to his listener, not by his own excitement alone.
  • Distinguishes a respectful emphasis from a domineering shout.
  • Uses emphasis to make his meaning clear, not to overwhelm.

The Emphasis Lab · One Sentence, Many Meanings

The very same sentence changes with the place of the emphasis. Try “I love playing with you today” · and light up one word each time:

The emphasized wordThe meaning the listener understands
I love playing with you todayI myself · not someone else.
I love playing with you todayI truly love it · I do not merely put up with it.
I love playing with you todayPlaying in particular · not something else.
I love playing with you todayWith you · not with anyone else.
I love playing with you todayToday · perhaps another day is not the same.
❦  The lesson of the Lab. The place of the emphasis changes the meaning, and one lit word is more eloquent than a shouted sentence. In the Arabic master, the same is shown with «أَنَا أُحِبُّ اللَّعِبَ مَعَكَ الْيَوْمَ», the stress moving from word to word to unfold different meanings. The English sentence has five stressable units, so it unfolds five.

The Four Inner Senses of Emphasis

Emphasis, too, is felt in the body before it is a skill. CRT awakens the child's interoception, the inner sense of the one word that lifts. Guide the child to notice, without naming the terms:

Inner senseWhat the child feels in emphasisA guiding whisper
The chosen summitThe single word that matters most, felt before it is spoken“Feel which word is the summit · the one your meaning rests on.”
The settling of the restThe calm of the other words resting quietly around it“Let the other words rest low, so the one can rise.”
The small liftThe little rise of voice and hand at that one word“Lift just a little · a small pulpit, not a shout.”
The return to restThe easy drop back to quiet after the lit word“Feel your voice come back down to rest, the flash after the stillness.”
❦  How to use these whispers. Say a short sentence, then say it again lighting a different word each time, and let the child hear the meaning move. Keep the lift small: a hand, never a shout.

Safe-Stop Protocol & Inclusion

❧  Safe-stop protocol. If the learner slips into raising his voice, or grows tense delivering before others, we return to a whisper and to choosing one word with no audience. Emphasis is a quiet lift, never a strain.
ConditionSuggested alternative
Social anxietyHe emphasizes in writing (colouring the important word) before he emphasizes aloud in front of people.
Autism spectrum (ASD)Fix a clear visual sign for the important word, and avoid exaggerated facial expression.
Difficulty with vocal controlA raised hand or an enlarged card suffices instead of a vocal emphasis.
Quickness to loudnessPractise the difference between a small lift and a shout with the whisper game; the lift is felt, not blasted.
A child mocked for his voice or accentHis tone is not corrected in front of others, and mocking imitation among children is not allowed. A variety of voices and accents is natural, and bullying about the voice is dealt with at once.
Language difficultyAccept marking the key word by pointing or by a coloured card within the utterance.
Cultural differenceRespect the differing conventions of emphasis and delivery across cultures.

Guide for Parent & Teacher

i. Before the session · the screen-box. Put devices away. Then listen together to a skilled orator (or a beautifully recited verse) and ask: which word did he light? How did we know it was the important one?

ii. During the session · the lit-word game. One sentence, and each person emphasizes a different word; discover together how the meaning changed each time.

iii. After the session · the evening reading. During the evening reading, let the learner choose one word in each sentence to emphasize, and the reading comes alive.

❦  Make it measurable. Record on the fridge ledger: “How many times did our child light one key word instead of stressing all, and say why?” A star for each. Count the stars at week's end.

“Screens make everything bold, so nothing stands out. We teach the child the one lit word.”

The Juthur Scale (JPS) · Book Seventeen Unit

❧  Important. The Juthur Scale is a pedagogical observation tool under development · not a psychological or medical diagnosis. Its readings are advisory and pass no verdict upon the child.
❦  How the measure grows. The Fruit opened with contentment. This book adds a skill of expression: intentional highlighting: the emphasis that lights the key word.

Filled twice, by two independent raters where possible. The marks are behaviourally anchored:

Al-kalima al-miftāḥ (“the key word”) is a phrase and not a member of the root family. It sits here because emphasis begins with choosing it.

Output (Arabic): «أَضِئْ كَلِمَةً وَاحِدَةً، لَا كُلَّهَا. مَوْضِعُ النَّبْرِ يُغَيِّرُ المَعْنَى.»
English Bridge: “Light up one word, not all of them.” Where you stress changes the meaning.

Questions of Understanding

  • Why did the listeners remember nothing of Noor's speech at first? recall
  • Why is “the flash after the stillness seen,” while a constant light is not? inference
  • Take a sentence and emphasize two different words in it. How did the meaning change? sensory
  • When did lighting one word help someone understand exactly what you meant? personal link
  • The Lightning flashed without cease because it feared being unseen if it stopped. Have you ever said everything loudly, afraid a quiet word would be missed? What helped the Lightning trust the stillness? empathy for the other
  • When did your voice rise for no need, and no one understood you? Which single word would have been enough to light? second link
  • When did a single quiet word carry more than a whole loud sentence? reflection

◆  A closing wisdom · the quiet highlight. “The flash that comes after stillness is seen; a constant light is not.” Let your speech have one summit the listener carries away: one lit word, quietly raised, does what a hundred shouted words cannot.

❦  Behavioural translation (not a slogan only). Teach the child: “Before you speak a sentence, choose the one word that matters most, and lift only that word a little. Do not blaze every word, or nothing will be seen. The place of your emphasis changes your whole meaning.” By the one lit word, Noor's meaning stayed in the hearts.

Craft & Media

The drawing. The learner writes a sentence and colours the word he will emphasize with a single bright colour, so he sees his emphasis with his own eye.

The play. “The unbroken Lightning”: one child flashes without cease (every word lit), and another flashes one flash after a stillness. Whose flash did we see?
