Before You Begin · for Teacher & Parent

❧  Proactive caution. This book grows the sequencing of speech. Sequencing is an ordering that serves clarity and persuasion by the truth, not an art of manipulation or of dressing up a falsehood. Teach the learner to order his speech so his honest idea arrives clearly, never to deceive his listener with a cunning arrangement.
❦  How to use this book. Two tellings · brief (3 minutes) and full (7 minutes). The tale closes the Fruit: Noor has mastered lighting the important word, and now learns to order her whole speech so that it leads and persuades.

The Heart of the Book

Noor has learned emphasis, the lighting of the important word. Now, at the close of the Fruit, she learns to order her whole speech: sequencing. She drives her ideas in a persuasive order (an opening that prepares, an argument that proves, a close that gathers all), so that she leads the listener step by step to her aim, as a guide drives his caravan by a sure road. Scattered speech drifts and is lost; ordered speech arrives and persuades.

❦  Historical footnote. ʿAbd al-Qāhir al-Jurjānī (d. 471 AH), the imam of rhetoric and author of the “Theory of Naẓm” (word-order), taught that meaning does not lie in words taken singly, but in their arrangement and the fineness of their sequence. From him we draw that speech has an order that leads the meaning: an opening, then an argument, then a close. (In this fruit-world he is honoured through Grandmother Ramla's telling, not present in person.)

The Root & Its Kin · a Skill and a Language

❧  Body before symbol (the first CRT principle). The child meets this root through the body of the caravan: three cards laid in a row, three fingers counted off, a path walked station by station. He does not parse letters; he feels his ideas fall into a line that carries the listener forward. The morphology below is for the teacher alone.
Root (the skill)Root family (words of the same lineage)
s-w-qsawq · siyāq · masāq · sāqa · sāʾiq
❦  How to explain it to the child. “The word sawq (driving) has siblings! One who drives a car or a caravan is a sāʾiq, and the ordering and flow of speech is a siyāq. And when you led your speech in order, you suqta it. All one family from one father: s-w-q.”
❦  Morphological note for the teacher. The root (s-w-q) is hollow (its middle is a wāw). From it come sawq (leading toward a destination) and siyāq (an ordered sequence of speech). Tell the teacher that siyāq in language is the ordering and arrangement of speech that leads the meaning toward its goal.
✦  Seed of Debate (the thread grows). In debate, the highest dialogue, sequencing becomes the very mechanism of disputation: each side drives its argument in order (a claim, then a proof), then listens and replies in turn, fairly and seeking evidence, with no chaos and no interruption. So the thread grows: from contentment with the truth (Book 16), to the lit word of the argument (Book 17), to the order of the disputation (this book), toward its crown in the Harvest.

The Full Telling: “Noor's Sequencing”

For reading aloud with the learner · seven minutes · with five original plates in the fruit palette

I. Noor speaks scattered ideas with no order, and persuades no one

Noor now spoke with a lovely melody and emphasis. But when she wished to persuade her friends of an idea, her speech came out scattered: she started from the middle, jumped to the end, went back to the beginning, then gave a reason and forgot her original idea!

The listeners were lost, and did not grasp what she wanted, and she persuaded no one, though her idea was good. Noor did not know that a good idea without order is lost. Scattered speech, however lovely its tune, wanders and never arrives.

II. The Scattering Dust flies in every direction with no destination

And in the desert there was the Scattering Dust. It blew in every direction at once, with no order and no destination, so it reached no place and built nothing, but only blinded the eyes and dispersed in the air. It said to Noor as it flew:

“Say all your ideas at once, Noor! Order nothing! Begin from anywhere, and jump as you please! Order is a chain, and chaos is freedom!”

Noor tried the Dust's way: she said her ideas jumbled, all at once. So her words flew off in every direction, and the listeners were struck with bewilderment, and her aim never arrived. She discovered that scattered speech is like dust: it fills the air, but reaches nothing, builds nothing, and persuades no one.

III. Grandmother Ramla brings Noor al-Jurjānī's wisdom of sequencing

And Noor remembered what is told of the imam of the language, ʿAbd al-Qāhir al-Jurjānī, who taught that the eloquence of speech lies in the fineness of its arrangement and the order of its sequence. And Grandmother Ramla was beside her, reminding her of his wisdom, and she said as if carrying his meaning:

“Noor, speech is like a caravan: it has a beginning, a road, and a destination. Order your speech in three: begin with your idea in one clear sentence (the opening), then give its reason and proof (the argument), then close with a summary that gathers it all (the close). So the listener follows you step by step until he is convinced.”

And Grandmother Ramla added: “You are a complete fruit now, Noor. And every fruit ripened in order: a blossom, then a setting, then a fruit. Drive your speech in that same order, step after step, and your aim arrives ripe and persuasive.”

IV. Noor drives her speech in order: an opening, an argument, a close

Noor ordered her speech. She wished to persuade her friends to plant a garden, and she said:

Opening “I propose that we plant a garden.”
Argument “Because it will give us fruit and shade, and gather us together.”
Close “So let us begin tomorrow, together.”

And a lovely thing happened: the listeners followed her step by step, they grasped the idea, then were convinced by the reason, then grew eager for the close. They all agreed! Noor learned that ordered speech leads the listener calmly from the idea to conviction, like a caravan travelling safely toward its destination.

V. Noor drives her speech in order; the Dust learns and becomes an ordered caravan

Noor came to drive her speech in order in every situation: she begins, then proves, then closes. Her speech came to lead, to persuade, and to arrive. She said with confidence: “I drive my speech in order.”, and people followed her speech and were convinced by it, for it took them by the hand step by step.

And the wonder happened: the Scattering Dust saw how Noor's speech arrived by order. And it tried to arrange itself… and for the first time its motes gathered into a single line, and turned toward one destination, until it became an ordered caravan travelling by a clear road toward its goal! And it whispered: “I used to fly with no destination, so I never arrived and never built; now I have ordered myself and moved in sequence… and I have become a caravan that reaches its goal!”

And Noor knew that if she ever debated, she would drive her argument in order, then listen to her opponent and reply in turn, by proof, for a debate is a shared sequencing in fairness, not a victory by loudness. And so Noor completed the whole stage of the Fruit (contentment, emphasis and sequencing), and her fruit ripened complete, and she made ready to gather the Harvest: wisdom and legacy.

❦  The Silence of the Bodily Question (4–5 seconds). “Think of something you want to persuade someone of. Order it in three: What is your idea? What is its proof? What is your close?”

The Brief Telling (for the youngest · 3 minutes)

The same tale, in shorter sentences.

Noor wished to persuade her friends of an idea, but her speech came out scattered, so the listeners were lost.

The Scattering Dust flew with no destination; Noor copied it, and her speech flew off and never arrived.

Grandmother Ramla reminded Noor of al-Jurjānī's wisdom: “Order your speech: an opening, an argument, a close, so that the listener follows and is convinced.”

Noor drove her speech in order and persuaded everyone, and said: “I drive my speech in order.” So the Dust became an ordered caravan.

Sub-Degree & Mastery Criterion (graduated)

A two-stage criterion. The fruit made complete (beginning): orders his speech in steps when reminded. The fruit made complete (mastery): drives his speech, unprompted, in a persuasive order (opening, argument, close), leading the listener to his aim. With this the Fruit is complete.

❧  CRT Can-Do (a communicative criterion; CEFR B2 / ACTFL Intermediate High). The child can organize an argument into an opening, a supporting proof, and a summarising close, drive the sequence to lead a listener to a conclusion, and tell an ordered speech from a scattered one.

CASEL Behavioural Criteria (Relationship Skills & Self-Management)

  • Orders his ideas before he speaks.
  • Begins with an opening, proves with an argument, and closes with a summary.
  • Leads the listener to his aim clearly.
  • Distinguishes ordered speech from scattered speech.

The Three-Station Caravan

The same three stations, as a frame to carry to any idea, one after another, so that it arrives:

1 · The Opening Your idea, in one clear sentence; the first finger is held. Noor: “I propose that we plant a garden.”
2 · The Argument Its reason and its proof; the second finger is held. Noor: “Because it gives us fruit and shade, and gathers us together.”
3 · The Close A summary that gathers it; the third finger is held. Noor: “So let us begin tomorrow, together.”

For a younger learner, three short sentences suffice, one for each station. If he is lost on the way, he returns to his fingers and begins again at the first station, and is not blamed.

Try the frame on a new idea before you use it on your own: “I would like us to read together for ten minutes each evening, because stories rest the mind and give us something to share, so may we begin tonight?”

❦  The lesson of the caravan. Scattered words drift like dust; ordered words lead like a caravan to its goal. In the Arabic master: «أَبْدَأُ، فَأُبَرْهِنُ، فَأَخْتِمُ · فَأَصِلُ بِكَ إِلَى مَقْصُودِي» · I begin, then prove, then close · so I bring you to my aim.

The Four Inner Senses of Sequencing

Sequencing, too, is felt in the body before it is a skill. CRT awakens the child's interoception, the inner sense of the path that carries a listener forward. Guide the child to notice, without naming the terms:

Inner senseWhat the child feels in sequencingA guiding whisper
The chosen startThe clear first step from which the road begins“Feel where your road begins · one clear opening.”
The forward pullThe way each part draws the listener on to the next“Feel each part pull to the next, like a caravan on its road.”
The proving weightThe steadiness a reason gives beneath the idea“Feel the ground your proof lays under your idea.”
The gathered closeThe rest of arriving, all the parts gathered at the end“Feel the close gather the whole road into one arrival.”
❦  How to use these whispers. Lay three cards on the table and let the child arrange them before he speaks. Ask which station he is at, and whether the listener has been led there or only told.

Safe-Stop Protocol & Inclusion

❧  Safe-stop protocol. Sequencing is an ordering that serves clarity and persuasion by the truth, not an art of trickery or deceit. We teach the learner to order his speech so his honest idea arrives clearly, never to dress up a falsehood or mislead his listener with a cunning arrangement.
ConditionSuggested alternative
Autism spectrum (ASD)A clear, logical order often helps; give a fixed visual structure (1 opening, 2 argument, 3 close) as cards he arranges.
Executive-function difficultyUse cards or visual symbols to order the ideas step by step before speaking.
Language difficultyAccept a simple order of two sentences (idea + reason) before moving to three; what matters is the sequence.
ImpulsivityTrain him to pause and order the ideas in his mind before rushing into scattered speech.
Low confidenceEncourage him that order makes his speech stronger and clearer, so his confidence grows as he speaks.
Cultural differenceRespect the differing conventions of discourse-ordering across cultures and languages.
Persuasion and pressingThe child learns to present his case and then accept another’s “no”, and that he too has the right to say “no” when someone presses him. A child is never persuaded into anything that harms him or into keeping a frightening secret, however well ordered the speaker’s argument; he tells a trusted adult at once.

Guide for Parent & Teacher

i. Before the session · the screen-box. Put devices away. Much of what screens offer is short, scattered clips; here we teach the learner to build ordered, persuasive speech.

ii. During the session · the three-station caravan game. Choose a topic, and train the learner to say it in three: my idea, my proof, my close, as if driving a caravan through three stations.

iii. After the session · “persuade me in order.” When the learner asks for something, ask him to persuade you in order: What do you want? Why? What is your close? Reward the ordering, not just the request.

❦  Make it measurable. Record on the fridge ledger: “How many times did our child order his speech (idea, proof, close) and persuade clearly?” A star for each. Count the stars at week's end.

The Juthur Scale (JPS) · Book Eighteen Unit

❧  Important. The Juthur Scale is a pedagogical observation tool under development · not a psychological or medical diagnosis. Its readings are advisory and pass no verdict upon the child.
❦  How the measure grows. The Juthur Scale (JPS) has five cumulative dimensions. With this book the Fruit stage is complete (contentment, emphasis, sequencing). The new dimension: the sequencing of speech and its persuasive order, a threshold to the Harvest.

Filled twice, by two independent raters where possible. The marks are behaviourally anchored:

MarkBehavioural anchor (what you actually observe)

◐  sometimesOrders his speech only when reminded; does not yet drive the sequence unprompted.
●  oftenUnprompted, drives his speech in a persuasive order (opening, argument, close) and leads the listener to his aim.

The rating procedure, the rule for resolving differences between raters, and the research status of the scale (exploratory, testable, never diagnostic) are set out once, in Book I.

Questions of Understanding

  • Why did Noor persuade no one, though her idea was good? recall
  • Why did the Scattering Dust reach no place and build nothing? inference
  • Order an idea you want to share in three parts now: idea, proof, close. How did it become clearer? sensory
  • When did someone persuade you with ordered speech that took you step by step? personal link
  • The Dust flew everywhere because order felt like a chain that trapped it. Have you ever poured out everything at once, afraid order would slow you? What helped the Dust find its road? empathy for the other
  • Remember a time someone persuaded you with ordered speech. Was what they persuaded you of worth it? And how do you know when to say “no”? second link

Craft & Media

The drawing. Ask the child to draw a caravan of three stations along a road: the first station (my idea), the second (my proof), the third (my close), and write an idea on each station.

The play. Children play the “persuading caravan”: each chooses a topic and drives his speech through three ordered stations to persuade the rest. Whoever orders his speech and persuades wins.

Listening. There is no recording of this book yet. Read the tale aloud, slowly, and keep the pauses. The Listening room gathers the recordings that exist.

The Fruit's Sequencing

To give children a calm place beside the screens, and grow ordered speech that leads and persuades.

Noor has learned to light the key word, but when she tries to persuade her friends, her speech comes out scattered, and no one can follow her good idea. When the Scattering Dust tells her to fling every thought at once, and her words blow off and reach nowhere, the wisdom of the imam of rhetoric, recalled by a grandmother, teaches her that speech is a caravan with a beginning, a road, and a destination. So Noor learns to drive her speech in three parts (an opening, an argument, a close) and lead her listeners step by step to “yes,” until even the scattering dust gathers into an ordered caravan that reaches its goal. A tangible, sensory tale in the tradition of Concrete Reading Theory: no screen of scattered clips, only a voice learning that ordered words lead like a caravan to their goal.
