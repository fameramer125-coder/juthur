Before You Begin · for Teacher & Parent

❧  Proactive caution. This book grows the manners of disagreement. Do not force the child to give up his opinion; teach him instead to voice it calmly. If the child is quick to anger, help him withdraw and breathe before he argues, and never meet his anger with a matching anger of your own.
❦  How to use this book. Two tellings · brief (3 minutes) and full (7 minutes). The tale continues: Noor's heart has learned to feel with another; now she learns how to disagree with him without wounding him.

The Heart of the Book

Noor has learned empathy. Now she faces the hardest test of a great heart: what if her opinion differs from that of someone she loves? Disagreement is natural and lovely, as the leaves differ in shape, but some people turn it into war. The skill is to differ in opinion while respect and affection remain: neither to erupt like a volcano nor to cut off a friendship.

❦  Historical footnote. Imam Malik ibn Anas (d. 179 AH), a great scholar of Madinah, is remembered for honouring those who differed from him in opinion, holding that a scholar may be right or wrong. From him we draw the manners of disagreement: that a difference of opinion need not spoil affection. (In this leaves-world he is honoured through Grandmother Ramla's telling, not present in person.)

The Root & Its Kin · a Skill and a Language

❧  Body before symbol (the first CRT principle). The child meets this root through the body of calm difference: a steadying breath, a lowered voice, an open face turned to the one he differs from. He does not parse letters; he feels that two opinions can stand side by side like two leaves on one branch. The morphology below is for the teacher alone.
Root (the skill)Root family (words of the same lineage)
kh-l-fkhilāf · mukhālif · ikhtilāf · ikhtalafa · mukhtalif
❦  How to explain it to the child. “The word khilāf (disagreement) has siblings! One whose opinion differs from yours is a mukhālif; opinions being unalike is ikhtilāf; and when your view differed from his, the two of you ikhtalaftumā. All one family from one father: kh-l-f.”
❦  Morphological note for the teacher. The root (kh-l-f) is sound. From it come ikhtilāf (a difference of views) and khilāfa (that one succeeds another). Remind the teacher that a difference of variety is a mercy, unlike the blameworthy quarrel that spoils hearts.

The Full Telling: “Noor's Disagreement”

For reading and reflection with the learner · seven minutes · with five original plates in the leaves palette

I. Noor differs with her friend, and the voices rise

One day Noor and her friend wished to play. Noor said, “Let us play hide-and-seek!” and her friend said, “No, let us play ball!” They differed. And instead of differing quietly, each raised a voice: “My opinion is better!” “No, I am right!”

Their faces darkened, and they drew apart, angry. The disagreement was small, but the way they disagreed made it large. Noor felt a tightness: why does a difference of opinion turn into enmity? Must one win and the other lose?

II. The Angry Volcano erupts at every disagreement

And in the garden there was the Angry Volcano. Whenever anyone differed with it in the smallest thing, it flared and erupted and hurled its lava and terrified everyone. It said to Noor, smoking with anger:

“Whoever differs with you in opinion is an enemy, Noor! Erupt in his face like me! Whoever does not agree with me, I burn! There is no room for two opinions, only my opinion is right!”

Noor tried the Volcano's way: she flared and raged at everyone who differed with her. But they all feared her and fled, and she was left alone atop a volcano of anger. She discovered that whoever erupts at every disagreement burns up his friendships and stays alone in his ash.

III. Grandmother Ramla brings Noor Imam Malik's wisdom of the manners of disagreement

And Noor remembered what is told of Imam Malik ibn Anas, who honoured those who differed from him, holding that every person may be right or may err. And Grandmother Ramla was beside her, reminding her of his wisdom, and she said as if carrying his meaning:

“Noor, a difference of opinion is a lovely thing! If we all thought the very same way, we would never learn anything new. What matters is how you differ: speak your opinion quietly, hear his opinion with respect, and stay friends. Two differing opinions are like two flowers of two colours in one garden.”

And Grandmother Ramla added: “Look at your leaves, Noor, each leaf is a different shape, yet they all live in peace on one tree. Be so with your friends: differ in opinion, and stay on the branch of friendship.”

IV. Noor differs quietly and with respect: she speaks her view and honours her friend's

Noor went back to her friend, and drew a calm breath, as she had learned to do earlier, looked at him with affection, and said in a quiet, respectful voice:

“I respect your opinion, my friend; ball is a truly lovely game. But I think hide-and-seek is more fun today, because there are many of us. What if we play hide-and-seek now, and ball after it?”

Her friend calmed, and smiled, because Noor had respected his opinion and not attacked him. They agreed on a solution that pleased them both. Noor learned that a quiet disagreement opens solutions, while an angry one closes hearts.

V. Noor and her friend differ and stay friends; the Volcano calms into a firm green mountain

Noor came to differ with her friends politely: she would speak her opinion, hear theirs, and keep loving them despite the difference. She said to her friend with love: “I disagree with you · and I love you.”, and their disagreements became lovely conversations that drew them closer, not wars that pulled them apart.

And the wonder happened: the Angry Volcano saw how friends differ in peace and stay dear to one another. And it tried to be calm… and for the first time, when someone differed with it, it did not erupt! It breathed, and its lava cooled, and it grew firm, until it became a firm green mountain with trees springing up on it! And it whispered: “I used to erupt, so I burned and stayed alone. Now I have calmed… and I have become a mountain that shelters people!”

And the Volcano learned that calm in disagreement turns fire into firmness, and loneliness into fellowship. And so Noor learned to differ with a great heart, and her leaves grew all the more lush, and she drew nearer to the fruits of wisdom.

❦  The Silence of the Bodily Question (4–5 seconds). “Remember a time you disagreed with a friend. How would you have said your opinion quietly, with respect for his, without getting angry?”

The Brief Telling (for the youngest · 3 minutes)

The same tale, in shorter sentences.

Noor and her friend differed over a game; the voices rose, their faces darkened, and they drew apart.

The Angry Volcano erupted at every disagreement, so Noor copied it, and everyone fled from her.

Grandmother Ramla reminded Noor of Imam Malik's wisdom: “A difference of opinion is lovely! Speak your view quietly, and hear his with respect.”

Noor said calmly: “I respect your opinion, but I see…”, so they agreed in peace, and the Volcano cooled into a green mountain.

Sub-Degree & Mastery Criterion (graduated)

A two-stage criterion. Unfolding leaves (beginning): restrains his anger in a disagreement with a reminder. ← Unfolding leaves (mastery): differs in opinion quietly and with respect, states his view with a reason, hears the one who differs, and keeps the affection.

❧  CRT Can-Do (a communicative criterion; CEFR B1 / ACTFL Intermediate Mid). The child can express a differing opinion politely and with a reason, hear the other's view in full, seek a shared solution, and keep the friendship, differing in mind while the hearts stay near.

CASEL Behavioural Criteria (Relationship Skills)

  • Voices his differing opinion quietly and without raising his voice.
  • Respects the opinion of the one who differs and hears it in full.
  • Seeks a middle solution that pleases both sides.
  • Keeps the affection with the one he disagrees with.

The Four Inner Senses of Disagreement

Calm disagreement, too, is felt in the body before it is a skill. CRT awakens the child's interoception, the inner sense of the steadying that keeps a difference from becoming a war. Guide the child to notice, without naming the terms:

Inner senseWhat the child feels in a disagreementA guiding whisper
The rising heat (the signal to pause)The warmth in the chest that warns before an outburst“Feel the heat rise? That is the moment to breathe, not to erupt.”
The cooling breathThe calm that returns when he breathes before he speaks“Draw one slow breath, and feel the fire become firmness.”
The room for twoThe ease of letting another's opinion sit beside his own“Feel that both views can rest here, like two leaves on one branch.”
The kept affectionThe warmth that stays for the friend even as the minds differ“Feel your heart still near him, even while your opinions part.”
❦  How to use these whispers. Use them at a real, small difference of opinion, before the voices rise. Let the child feel the heat in the chest first, then the breath, then make room for the other view.

Safe-Stop Protocol & Inclusion

❧  Safe-stop protocol. If the learner's anger swells within a disagreement, teach him to withdraw for a moment and breathe (as in Book One) before he resumes the discussion. We do not force him to abandon his opinion, but to voice it with manners.
ConditionSuggested alternative
Emotional-regulation difficultyLink the book to the breath (Book One): when angry, we stop and breathe before speaking; a brief withdrawal to calm is allowed.
Autism spectrum (ASD)A view that departs from a fixed rule may be hard to accept; explain with concrete examples that people hold different opinions, and honour his need for clarity.
Quickness to angerTeach a self-stop signal (I raise my hand, I count to five) before replying in a disagreement.
Shyness or avoidance of confrontationEncourage him to voice his differing opinion with confidence and gentleness, and reassure him that disagreement is a legitimate right.
Language difficultyAccept the expression of difference in a short sentence or on a card; what matters is the calm and the respect.
Conflictual environmentOffer a model of calm disagreement to the one accustomed to shouting in disputes, without blame.
Harm, not disagreementThe etiquette of disagreement does not mean silence about harm. If what is aimed at the child is a threat, violence or repeated insult, this is not a difference of opinion but a matter of protection, and a trusted adult is told at once.

Guide for Parent & Teacher

i. Before the session · the screen-box. Put devices away. Online arguments are often shouting and name-calling; here we teach the manners of disagreement they so often lack.

ii. During the session · the game of two opinions. Pose a question with no single answer (which fruit is best?), and practise saying the differing opinion quietly and with respect, without calling the other wrong.

iii. After the session · the grown-ups' model. When you disagree in front of the child, disagree quietly and with respect, so he sees how a fine disagreement looks. The example is more eloquent than the counsel.

❦  Make it measurable. Record on the fridge ledger: “How many times did our child differ in opinion quietly and with respect, without anger?” A star for each. Count the stars at week's end.

“Screens often model shouting in disagreement. We teach its manners.”

The Juthur Scale (JPS) · Book Thirteen Unit

❧  Important. The Juthur Scale is a pedagogical observation tool under development · not a psychological or medical diagnosis. Its readings are advisory and pass no verdict upon the child.
❦  How the measure grows. The Juthur Scale (JPS) has five cumulative dimensions. The Leaves added empathy. This book deepens the relationship skills with a new dimension: the manners of disagreement.

Filled twice, by two independent raters where possible. The marks are behaviourally anchored:

MarkBehavioural anchor (what you actually observe)
○  rarelyErupts or ruptures at a disagreement; cannot bear a differing opinion.
◐  sometimesDiffers calmly only when reminded; the affection is not yet kept unprompted.
●  oftenUnprompted, differs quietly and with a reason, hears the other, and keeps the affection.

The rating procedure, the rule for resolving differences between raters, and the research status of the scale (exploratory, testable, never diagnostic) are set out once, in Book I.

Questions of Understanding

  • Why did Noor's small disagreement with her friend grow large? recall
  • Why did the Angry Volcano stay alone in its ash? inference
  • Disagree with the one beside you over some small thing, quietly, right now. How did you both feel? sensory
  • When did you disagree with a friend and stay friends? How did you do it? personal link
  • The Volcano erupted because a differing opinion felt like a threat to it. Have you ever flared to defend your view? What could help the Volcano feel safe enough to calm? empathy for the other
  • Remember a time your voice rose in a disagreement. What did you want the other to hear? And how could you have said it calmly? second link
  • When did hearing an opinion unlike yours teach you something new? reflection
A difference of opinion never spoils a matter of affection; I differ with you, and my heart stays with you.
◆  A closing wisdom · the manners of disagreement. “My opinion is right, and may be wrong; another's opinion is wrong, and may be right.” So we agree on what we agree upon, and each excuses the other in what we differ upon, and the affection remains, though the opinions part.

❦  Behavioural translation (not a slogan only). Teach the child: “When your opinion differs from another's, do not raise your voice and do not get angry. Say quietly: I respect your opinion, but I see otherwise, because… A difference of opinion does not mean enmity.” By the manners of disagreement, Noor and her friend stayed friends.

Craft & Media

The drawing. Ask the child to draw a great tree whose leaves are all different shapes on one branch, like different opinions living in peace within one friendship.

The play. Children play the “two opinions” game: one voices a view, another differs quietly and with respect, and together they seek a solution that pleases them both.

Listening. There is no recording of this book yet. Read the tale aloud, slowly, and keep the pauses. The Listening room gathers the recordings that exist.

To give children a calm place beside the screens, and grow hearts that can differ without wounding.

Noor's heart has learned to feel with others, and now meets the hardest test of a great heart: what if she differs from the one she loves? When the Angry Volcano tells her that whoever differs is an enemy, and she erupts and is left alone in her ash, the wisdom of a great scholar, recalled by her grandmother, teaches her that leaves differ in shape yet share one branch. A tangible, sensory tale in the tradition of Concrete Reading Theory: no screen that shouts · only a heart learning to differ with grace.
