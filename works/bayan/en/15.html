The Single Thread · presence, turned toward every heart in the circle. All the Leaves' skills meet here: counsel gathers empathy, honest disagreement, and wise silence into one shared decision: you speak to give your view and are silent to hear another's. It reaches back through the whole of the Leaves and forward to the ripening Fruit, where the counselled word becomes a sweet, mature harvest.

Before You Begin · for Teacher & Parent

❧  Proactive caution. This book grows counsel. Counsel does not mean that the learner abandons his opinion out of fear or dissolves into the group. Teach him to voice his view with confidence and to listen to others, and teach that the group's decision is respected while his right to a polite disagreement remains. Counsel is for matters that belong to the group; a question of safety is decided by the adults and is never put to a vote.
❦  How to use this book. Two tellings · brief (3 minutes) and full (7 minutes). The tale closes the Leaves: Noor now speaks, listens, feels and differs with grace, and she learns to decide with the group, not alone.

The Heart of the Book

Noor has learned speech and silence, empathy and disagreement. Now, at the close of the Leaves, she learns to gather all of it into the highest shared skill: counsel. Not to decide alone what concerns the group, but to gather the views, listen to all, and decide together. The lone decision may err; the counselled one holds a blessing and a wisdom.

❦  Historical footnote. Khadija bint Khuwaylid (d. three years before the Hijra), a great lady of the Arabs, was famed for her mind and sound judgement; she was consulted, and her wise view was taken. From her we draw that counsel and the shared view are a blessing, and that a woman, too, is a person of judgement and counsel. (In this leaves-world she is honoured through Grandmother Ramla's telling, not present in person.)

The Root & Its Kin · a Skill and a Language

❧  Body before symbol (the first CRT principle). The child meets this root through the body of the circle: sitting with the group and not above it, turning his eyes to each face, asking and then waiting to hear. He does not parse letters; he feels that many voices, gathered, become one wiser choice. The morphology below is for the teacher alone.
Root (the skill)Root family (words of the same lineage)
sh-w-rshūrā · mustashīr · mushāwara · tashāwara · mashūra
❦  How to explain it to the child. “The word shūrā (counsel) has siblings! One who asks another's view is a mustashīr; the view you give one who asks you is a mashūra; and when you gathered the views together, you tashāwartum. All one family from one father: sh-w-r.”
❦  Morphological note for the teacher. The root (sh-w-r) is hollow (its middle is a wāw). From it come shūrā, mashūra, and istishāra. Tell the teacher that the origin of shawr is the drawing of honey from the hive; so counsel is the drawing of the best view out of the minds, as one draws honey.

The Full Telling: “Noor's Counsel”

For reading and reflection with the learner · seven minutes · with five original plates in the leaves palette

I. Noor decides alone a matter that concerns everyone, without asking her friends

Noor had become eloquent and wise, so her friends were glad to have her lead them. One day the group wished to choose a place to play. And Noor said, alone: “I have decided! We will play by the river, and that is the end of it!”

But her friends stood hesitant. Some of them feared the river, and some preferred the hill, but Noor did not ask them. They felt their opinion had no worth. The lone decision, even from a wise one, wounds the one who was not consulted, for the matter belongs to them all.

II. The Lone Whirlwind coils around itself and decides alone

And in the garden there was the Lone Whirlwind. It coiled around itself alone, deciding everything by itself, asking no one and hearing no view. It said to Noor as it spun:

“Decide alone, Noor! You are the cleverest! Ask no one, for your view is enough. Whoever consults others is weak! I coil around myself and decide, and I need no one!”

Noor tried the Whirlwind's way: she decided everything alone. But her friends drew away, one by one, for no one loves to be a follower without a voice. And Noor came to spin alone in an emptiness, like the Whirlwind. She discovered that whoever always decides alone, stays always alone.

III. Grandmother Ramla brings Noor Khadija bint Khuwaylid's wisdom of counsel

And Noor remembered what is told of the lady Khadija bint Khuwaylid, the woman of mind and judgement, who was consulted and whose view was taken. And Grandmother Ramla was beside her, reminding her of her wisdom; she said as if carrying her meaning:

“Noor, you are wise, but one view, however wise, stays a single view. When you gather everyone's views, each sees what you do not see, and the decision is completed. Ask, “What do you think?” and listen; then decide together. Counsel draws the best that is in the minds.”

And Grandmother Ramla added: “Look at your leaves, Noor. No leaf sways alone; they all sway together and make one lovely rustle. Be so with your friends: one decision you make together, that all are pleased with and all carry together.”

IV. Noor gathers her friends in a circle and asks them their views

Noor gathered her friends in a circle, and sat with them, not above them, and said: “We want to choose a place to play, and the matter concerns us all. What do you all think? Let us hear each one.”

And one said: “I fear the river.” And another said: “I love the hill.” And Noor said: “Thank you all. What do you think, shall we play at the safe hill, which is near, and which most of you love?”

And all rejoiced, and agreed happily, for the decision had become their decision, not Noor's alone. Noor learned that when she listens to all, she reaches a wiser decision, and all become happy with it and responsible for it.

V. A shared decision that gladdens all; the Whirlwind learns and becomes a breeze that gathers seeds

Noor came to take counsel with her friends in every matter that concerned them: she gathered the views, and listened, and they decided together. She said to them with love: “We decide together.”, and they loved her as a leader, for she did not lead them from above, but walked with them and heard them.

And the wonder happened: the Lone Whirlwind saw how the friends gathered around Noor in love. And it tried to stop coiling around itself… and for the first time, it opened, and its spinning calmed, and it became a gentle breeze that moved among the flowers, gathering their seeds and carrying them together to sow in every place! And it whispered: “I used to coil around myself, so I stayed alone and hollow; now I have opened and gathered… and I have become a breeze that sows life!”

And the Whirlwind learned that whoever opens and gathers and takes counsel makes life and fellowship. And so Noor completed the whole stage of the Leaves (empathy, fine disagreement, wise silence and counsel), and her lush leaves grew full, and she ripened and made ready to bear the fruits of wisdom.

❦  The Silence of the Bodily Question (4–5 seconds). “Think of a decision that concerns your group (your family or friends). How would you gather their views and ask each one, before you decide together?”

The Brief Telling (for the youngest · 3 minutes)

The same tale, in shorter sentences.

Noor decided the place to play alone, without asking her friends, so they stood hesitant.

The Lone Whirlwind decided alone; Noor copied it, and her friends drew away, one by one.

Noor remembered Khadija bint Khuwaylid's wisdom: “Gather the views, and ask “What do you think?” and listen; then decide together.”

Noor gathered her friends and asked: “What do you all think?” They rejoiced in the shared decision, and the Whirlwind became a breeze that gathers seeds.

Sub-Degree & Mastery Criterion (graduated)

A two-stage criterion. Leaves in counsel (beginning): asks another's view before deciding, when reminded. Leaves in counsel (mastery): gathers the group's views unprompted, listens to all, and shares in a group decision that pleases everyone. With this the Leaves are complete.

❧  CRT Can-Do (a communicative criterion; CEFR B1 / ACTFL Intermediate Mid). The child can open a shared matter to the group, ask each member for a view, listen to all, weigh his own view against the group's, and reach a decision that all are pleased with and responsible for.

CASEL Behavioural Criteria (Responsible Decision-Making & Relationship Skills)

  • Gathers the group's views before a shared decision.
  • Listens to every view and values it.
  • Shares in reaching a decision that pleases everyone.
  • Does not impose his view in what concerns the group.

The Four Inner Senses of Counsel

Counsel, too, is felt in the body before it is a skill. CRT awakens the child's interoception, the inner sense of the circle in which many voices become one wiser choice. Guide the child to notice, without naming the terms:

Inner senseWhat the child feels in counselA guiding whisper
The seated equalityThe ease of sitting with the group, not above it“Feel yourself in the circle, one among friends, not on a throne.”
The turning gazeThe attention that moves from face to face, asking“Let your gaze go round the circle · each face has a view to give.”
The gathered voicesThe fullness of many views coming together“Feel the views gather, like leaves making one rustle.”
The shared carryingThe warmth of a decision that all hold together“Feel the decision rest on all of you, lighter, because it is shared.”
❦  How to use these whispers. Use them when a real choice belongs to the whole group, such as a game or an outing. Let the child feel the circle first, then ask each person in turn.

Safe-Stop Protocol & Inclusion

❧  Safe-stop protocol. Counsel does not mean the learner's view dissolves into the group out of fear, nor that he is forced to agree with the majority against his conscience. We teach him to voice his view with confidence, and to respect the group's decision, while his right to a polite disagreement remains.
ConditionSuggested alternative
Shyness or withdrawalLet him give his view in a way that is comfortable (in writing, on a card, whispered to a facilitator), so he shares in counsel without the dread of the group.
Autism spectrum (ASD)Make the steps of counsel clear with a fixed visual structure (we ask, we listen, we vote, we decide), and honour his manner of taking part.
Low confidenceEncourage him that his view matters and is heard, and celebrate his taking part however simple.
A leaning to dominateTeach the natural leader that the true leader listens and gathers, and does not impose; train him to give others their turn.
Language difficultyAccept a view expressed by a gesture or by choosing from pictures within the process of counsel.
Cultural differenceRespect the differing forms of consultation across cultures and families.
A decision that shuts someone outNothing that harms or excludes someone is decided by majority, such as agreeing never to play with one particular child. Shura seeks what is best for all and never justifies wronging the minority; adults step in if this appears.

Guide for Parent & Teacher

i. Before the session · the screen-box. Put devices away. Family decisions are made by dialogue, not by a screen; we grow a learner who is an owner of opinion and a listener to others.

ii. During the session · the family council of counsel. Hold a small council for a simple decision (the holiday meal, the weekend activity), and ask each member for a view, then decide together.

iii. After the session · “your view matters to us.” Let the learner feel that his view is heard in family decisions suited to his age. The learner who is consulted learns to consult.

❦  Make it measurable. Record on the fridge ledger: “How many times did our child gather others' views or share in a group decision by listening?” A star for each. Count the stars at week's end.

“The screen decides for the child, alone. We teach him to consult and decide with others.”

The Juthur Scale (JPS) · Book Fifteen Unit

❧  Important. The Juthur Scale is a pedagogical observation tool under development · not a psychological or medical diagnosis. Its readings are advisory and pass no verdict upon the child.
❦  How the measure grows. The Juthur Scale (JPS) has five cumulative dimensions. With this book the Leaves stage is complete (empathy, disagreement, silence, counsel). The new dimension: counsel and the shared decision, a threshold to the Fruit stage.

Filled twice, by two independent raters where possible. The marks are behaviourally anchored:

MarkBehavioural anchor (what you actually observe)
○  rarelyDecides alone what concerns the group; asks no one and hears no view.
◐  sometimesGathers views and shares a decision only when reminded; does not yet consult unprompted.
●  oftenUnprompted, gathers the views, listens to all, and shares in a decision that pleases everyone.

The rating procedure, the rule for resolving differences between raters, and the research status of the scale (exploratory, testable, never diagnostic) are set out once, in Book I.

Qarār (from q-r-r) and ijmāʿ (from j-m-ʿ) come from other roots. They sit here because counsel ends in a decision, and the best decision is one that all can accept.

Output (Arabic): «نَتَشَاوَرُ، وَنُصْغِي لِكُلِّ رَأْيٍ، فَنُقَرِّرُ مَعًا قَرَارًا أَحْكَمَ.»
English Bridge: “We consult, so we decide together.” Many leaves make one rustle; many minds make one wise choice.

Questions of Understanding

  • Why did Noor's friends stand hesitant when she decided alone? recall
  • Why did the Lone Whirlwind keep spinning alone in an emptiness? inference
  • Gather the views of two people beside you on some small matter, then decide together. How did they feel? sensory
  • When did you share in a group decision and feel your view was heard? personal link
  • The Whirlwind decided alone because sharing felt like weakness or loss of control. Have you ever wanted to decide everything yourself? What could help it feel safe enough to open and gather? empathy for the other
  • Have you ever felt your opinion was not asked for in a decision that concerned you? How do you spare others that feeling? second link
  • When did gathering others' views lead you to a choice you would not have reached alone? reflection
The view of the group is wiser than the view of one; we take counsel, and reach what no one reaches alone.
◆  A closing wisdom · the crown of the Leaves. “The lone decision may err; the counselled one holds a blessing.” When you gather the views, each sees what you cannot see, and the decision is completed, and all who share in it are pleased with it and carry it together.

❦  Behavioural translation (not a slogan only). Teach the child: “Before you decide a matter that concerns you all, gather the views. Ask each one, “What do you think?” and listen; then decide together. A decision everyone shares in is a decision everyone is pleased with.” By counsel, Noor's decision became everyone's decision.

Craft & Media

The drawing. Ask the child to draw a circle of counsel: friends seated in a ring, a thought-bubble above each, and at the centre the shared decision they agreed upon.

The play. Children play “the counsel circle”: they pose a decision (which game shall we play?), each gives his view in turn, then they vote and decide together. The whole group wins.

Listening. There is no recording of this book yet. Read the tale aloud, slowly, and keep the pauses. The Listening room gathers the recordings that exist.

The Leaves' Counsel

To give children a calm place beside the screens, and grow hearts that gather and decide together.

At the close of the lush Leaves, Noor, who can now feel, differ with grace, and keep a wise silence, faces the crown of the shared skills: should she decide alone what concerns the group, or decide it together? When the Lone Whirlwind tells her to decide by herself and ask no one, and she spins alone in an empty hollow, the wisdom of a great woman of counsel, recalled by her grandmother, teaches her that no leaf sways alone. So Noor gathers her friends in a circle, sits with them not above them, asks “What do you all think?” and reaches a decision they all share and carry together, until even the whirlwind opens into a breeze that gathers seeds and sows life. A tangible, sensory tale in the tradition of Concrete Reading Theory: no screen that decides for the child, only a heart learning that many minds make one wise choice.
