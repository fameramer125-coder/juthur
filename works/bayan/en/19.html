Before You Begin · for Teacher & Parent

❧  Proactive caution. This book grows wisdom and deliberation. Deliberation does not mean staying silent about the truth, or shirking a hard moment. Teach the learner that some words (a call for help, or the report of a harm done to him or to another) do not wait for the right time. They are said at once, to someone he trusts.
❦  How to use this book. Two tellings · brief (3 minutes) and full (7 minutes). The tale opens the Harvest: Noor has completed her ripening, and now gathers the first fruit of a lifetime, wisdom in speech.

The Heart of the Book

Noor has reached the Harvest, where the fruits of a whole life's journey are gathered. The first of the harvest is wisdom. Having learned every art of speech and of silence, she now learns their essence: to say the right word, at the right time, to the right person. For wisdom is not the knowing of all things, but the good choosing of the word and its season.

❦  Historical footnote. al-Aḥnaf ibn Qays (d. c. 67 AH), a chief of the Arabs, proverbial for forbearance, wisdom and deliberation, took his time and weighed his words, and so hit the mark. From him we draw that wisdom is the fruit of deliberation and of the good choosing of the word in its time. (In this harvest-world he is honoured through Grandmother Ramla's telling, not present in person.)

The Root & Its Kin · a Skill and a Language

❧  Body before symbol (the first CRT principle). The child meets this root through the body of the pause: a hand laid on the chest, a breath, a silent count of three, weighing before the word leaves the lips. He does not parse letters; he feels the deliberation settle him, and the word ripen before it is spoken. The morphology below is for the teacher alone.
Root (the skill)Root family (words of the same lineage)
ḥ-k-mḥikma · ḥakīm · muḥkam · aḥkama · ḥukm
❦  How to explain it to the child. “The word ḥikma (wisdom) has siblings! The owner of wisdom is ḥakīm; firm, well-made speech is muḥkam; and when you perfected a thing and completed it, you aḥkamta it. All one family from one father: ḥ-k-m.”
❦  Morphological note for the teacher. The root (ḥ-k-m) is sound. Its origin is restraint (“he reined the beast”: he held it by its bridle); hence wisdom, which restrains its owner from a slip. Tell the teacher that wisdom is the placing of a thing in its place, and of the word in its time.
✦  The Debate bears fruit (the crown of the thread). Here the seed of debate we planted comes to its fullness, from contentment with the truth (Book 16), to the lit word of the argument (17), to the mechanism of disputation (18), crowned now by wisdom. A praiseworthy debate (munāẓara) aims at the showing of the truth, not at victory; a blameworthy wrangling (mirāʾ) aims only at a triumph for the self. It is told that the grammarians Sībawayh and al-Kisāʾī disputed the “Wasp Question” (المسألة الزنبورية): the grammarians judged Sībawayh's view the sounder, yet the “win” in the gathering fell to the other. The wise lesson: the victory is not always the truth, so seek the truth, not the applause, and submit to it when it appears, even on your opponent's tongue. That is the perfection of wisdom.

The Full Telling: “Noor's Wisdom”

For reading aloud with the learner · seven minutes · with five original plates in the harvest palette

I. Noor rushes to speak in every situation, with no deliberation and no choosing of the time

Noor had become a full, ripe tree, bearing golden fruit and mastering every art of speech. But she was hasty: she said whatever word came to her at once, at any time, to anyone, without pausing to weigh whether it was the right word, or whether this was its time.

So at times she counselled a grieving friend when it was no time for counsel, or spoke a truth at a wrong moment so that it wounded rather than helped. Noor did not know that a right word in the wrong time can harm. Wisdom is not only in what you say, but in when, and how, and to whom.

II. The Reckless Storm rushes without patience, and shatters

And on the horizon the Reckless Storm blew up. It rushed with all its force in every direction without patience, uprooting the useful crop with the harmful, and shattering out of season. It said to Noor as it raged:

“Rush without thinking, Noor! Say everything at once, at full force, at any time! Patience is weakness, and rushing is strength! Do not wait for the fit time, all times are the same!”

Noor tried the Storm's way: she rushed out her speech without patience. So she shattered feelings, and spoiled situations, and spoke a right thing at a wrong time so that it became a wrong. She discovered that rushing without wisdom is like the storm: a force that shatters and does not build, and tears up the useful with the harmful.

III. Grandmother Ramla brings Noor al-Aḥnaf ibn Qays's wisdom of speech

And Noor remembered what is told of the deliberate sage al-Aḥnaf ibn Qays, proverbial for forbearance, patience, and fine speech. And Grandmother Ramla was beside her, reminding her of his wisdom, and she said as if carrying his meaning:

“Noor, wisdom is three: the right word, at the right time, to the right person. Before you speak, deliberate a little, and weigh: will my speech help now? Many a right word has harmed because it was said out of its time. Deliberation is not weakness, it is the bridle of wisdom.”

And Grandmother Ramla added: “Look at your golden fruits, Noor, they were gathered only in their season. Had they been plucked before their time, they would have been bitter. So it is with the word: it has a season. Say it in its time, and it will be sweet and useful, like a ripe fruit.”

IV. Noor deliberates and thinks before she speaks, and chooses the right word

Noor began to deliberate. Before she spoke, she was silent a moment, and thought: What is the right word? Is this its time? To whom do I say it? When she saw a grieving friend, she did not rush with counsel, but waited until he was calm. And before she spoke, she laid her hand on her chest and counted within: one… two… three, and felt the weight of the pause settle her, then chose a gentle word in its fit time.

And a lovely thing happened: her words came to hit the mark and to bear fruit! The counsel in its time helped, and the fit word healed, and the truth in its season was accepted. Noor learned that the moment of deliberation before speaking is what turns a word from a storm into a fruit. Wisdom is a pause, and then a striking of the mark.

V. Noor says the right word in its time, and debates for the truth; the Storm becomes a wise wind

Noor grew wise: she deliberated, and weighed, and chose the right word in its right time. People came to her to hear her word, for it was little, precious, and true to the mark. She said with the calm of the wise: “I make my word firm, and choose its time.”, and her word came to bear fruit in the hearts as her tree bore fruit. And one day Noor debated a friend on a matter: she drove her argument in order and calm, then listened to his in turn. And when the truth appeared on his tongue, she submitted to it gladly, without stubbornness, and said: “Submitting to the truth is the true victory.” So all who were near her knew that a wise debate shows the truth, and does not seek the triumph.

And the wonder happened: the Reckless Storm saw how Noor's deliberate word bore fruit. And it tried to grow calm and patient… and for the first time its rushing eased, and it came to choose when to blow and where, until it became a wise wind that scatters the seeds in their season only, and makes life spring up wherever it passes! And it whispered: “I used to rush out of season and shatter; now I have taken my time and chosen my moment… and I have become a wind that sows life in its season!”

And the Storm learned that patience turns shattering into sowing, and recklessness into wisdom. And so Noor gathered the first fruit of the Harvest: wisdom. Her word became right and useful, and one last fruit remained for her to gather: to pass on what she had learned to those who come after.

❦  The Silence of the Bodily Question (4–5 seconds). “Think of an important word you want to say to someone. Ask yourself: Is it the right word? Is this its fit time?”

The Brief Telling (for the youngest · 3 minutes)

The same tale, in shorter sentences.

Noor became ripe and eloquent, but she rushed out her speech without deliberation, so she sometimes wounded without meaning to.

The Reckless Storm blew up, rushing without patience and shattering; Noor copied it, and spoiled situations.

Grandmother Ramla reminded Noor of al-Aḥnaf ibn Qays's wisdom: “Wisdom is a right word, at a right time, to a right person. Deliberate and weigh.”

Noor deliberated and chose the word in its time, and it bore fruit, and she said: “I make my word firm, and choose its time.” So the Storm became a wise wind.

Sub-Degree & Mastery Criterion (graduated)

A two-stage criterion. A harvest gathered (beginning): deliberates before speaking, when reminded. A harvest gathered (mastery): weighs his speech unprompted, so he chooses the right word in its fit time for the fit person, and it hits the mark and bears fruit.

❧  CRT Can-Do (a communicative criterion; CEFR C1 / ACTFL Advanced Low). The child can pause and weigh before speaking, choose apt words for the situation, the time, and the person, manage a dialogue or debate to show the truth rather than win, and submit to the truth when it appears, even on another's tongue.

CASEL Behavioural Criteria (Responsible Decision-Making & Self-Awareness)

  • Deliberates and thinks before speaking on important matters.
  • Chooses the word fit for the situation.
  • Heeds the fit time and the state of the listener.
  • Discerns that a right word out of its time may harm.

The Three Rights of Wisdom

al-Aḥnaf's wisdom is a threefold measure. Before an important word, weigh it against all three:

The Right Word Is this the fit word · true, kind, and useful? “You made a mistake in the sum, and I will help you correct it.”
The Right Time Is this its season · is the listener ready to receive it? She waits for a sad friend to grow calm, then speaks to him.
The Right Person Is this the one it should be said to? She tells a trusted adult what is beyond her strength, and does not lay it on a friend her own age.
❦  The pause that weighs. Lay a hand on the chest, breathe, and count within: one, two, three. Then ask the Three Rights. In the Arabic master: «الْكَلِمَةُ الصَّائِبَةُ، فِي الْوَقْتِ الصَّائِبِ، لِلشَّخْصِ الصَّائِبِ», for a right word out of its time may harm, and in its time bears fruit.

One exception is never put off: a cry for help, or a report of harm, is said at once to a trusted adult. Here wisdom lies in speed.

The Four Inner Senses of Wisdom

Wisdom, too, is felt in the body before it is a skill. CRT awakens the child's interoception, the inner sense of the pause that ripens a word. Guide the child to notice, without naming the terms:

Inner senseWhat the child feels in wisdomA guiding whisper
The reining pauseThe steadying halt before the word leaves the lips“Feel the pause rein you in, like a hand on a bridle.”
The weighing heartThe inner weighing of the word against its time“Feel the word settle on the scale · is it right, is this its season?”
The reading of the momentThe sensing of whether the listener is ready“Feel the moment · is he ready to receive your word now?”
The ripe releaseThe ease of a word that falls in its right time“Feel the word fall ripe, in its season · sweet, not bitter.”
❦  How to use these whispers. Use them before a real word that matters, with a hand on the chest and a silent count of three. Ask the three questions in order: is it the right word, is it the right time, is it the right person?

Safe-Stop Protocol & Inclusion

❧  Safe-stop protocol. Wisdom and deliberation do not mean staying silent about the truth or shirking hard situations, but the good choosing of the word and its time. We teach the learner that some words (a call for help, or the reporting of a harm) do not wait for a fit time. They are said at once.
ConditionSuggested alternative
Impulsivity / hyperactivityTeach the tangible pause rule (count to three, breathe, then speak) as a practical tool before speaking.
Autism spectrum (ASD)Clarify the situations of fit time with concrete examples and social stories, and honour his directness while teaching consideration.
Anxiety or hesitationDistinguish wise deliberation from a silence of fear; encourage him to say his word with confidence in its time.
Difficulty reading situationsTeach the signs of the fit time (is the listener calm? busy? grieving?) step by step.
An expression-suppressing environmentReassure him that wisdom is not suppression, but the good choosing of the time, with his right to express himself intact.
Cultural differenceRespect the differing conventions of the timing of speech and of courtesy across cultures.
“The right time” as an excuse for delayA child is not taught to wait for “the right time” before telling of harm or fear. He is told plainly: what hurts or frightens you needs no right time; tell a grown-up you trust today, and if he does not listen, go to another.

Guide for Parent & Teacher

i. Before the session · the screen-box. Put devices away. Screens often reward the hasty, instant reply; here we grow deliberation and the wisdom of choosing the word and its time.

ii. During the session · the fit-time game. Present situations and ask: Is this a fit time for this word? Why? Train the learner to weigh the word and its time.

iii. After the session · the moment of deliberation. Encourage the learner in the pause rule before speaking on important matters: I breathe, I think, then I speak. Your own deliberation is the most eloquent example.

❦  Make it measurable. Record on the fridge ledger: “How many times did our child deliberate and choose his word and its time wisely, instead of rushing?” A star for each. Count the stars at week's end.

“Screens reward the hasty reply. We teach the wisdom of the word in its time.”

Craft & Media

The drawing. Ask the child to draw a balance-scale: a word in one pan and a clock (the time) in the other, and write beneath it: “The right word in its time bears fruit.”

The play. Children play the “sage of the moment”: a situation is presented (an angry friend, a piece of sad news), and each proposes what the right word is and when to say it. Whoever chooses the word and its time most wisely wins.

Listening. There is no recording of this book yet. Read the tale aloud, slowly, and keep the pauses. The Listening room gathers the recordings that exist.

The Harvest's Wisdom

To give children a calm place beside the screens, and grow the wisdom of the right word in its right time.

Noor has reached the Harvest, ripe and eloquent, yet she rushes out her speech without weighing it, so a right word lands at the wrong moment and wounds. When the Reckless Storm tells her that patience is weakness and tears up the useful with the harmful, the wisdom of a deliberate sage, recalled by a grandmother, teaches her the three rights: the right word, at the right time, to the right person. So Noor learns to pause, to lay her hand on her chest and count to three, to say the fit word in its season, and when she debates, to seek the truth rather than the win, submitting to it gladly even on another's tongue, until even the reckless storm gentles into a wise wind that sows the seed in its season. A tangible, sensory tale in the tradition of Concrete Reading Theory: no screen of hasty replies, only a voice learning that wisdom is a right word said in its time.
