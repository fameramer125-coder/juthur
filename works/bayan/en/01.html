Before You Begin · for Teacher & Parent

❧  Caution for the guide. Before practising deep-breathing exercises, review the children's health conditions. A child with asthma, an anxiety disorder, or a respiratory sensitivity may be affected by prolonged deep breathing. For them, keep the breath natural and short (a single breath), and never ask a child to hold the breath.
❦  How to use this book. It holds two tellings of the tale– a brief telling (3 minutes, for the youngest, age 4) and a full telling (7 minutes, for ages 5–6). Choose what suits your child's attention.

The Heart of the Book

Before a child learns to speak, he learns to breathe with awareness. The breath is the first presence, the first word the body utters before the tongue, and a child's first proof that he is here.

❦  Historical footnote. Jabir ibn Hayyan was a pioneering Arab scientist of the second Hijri century, reckoned among the founders of the experimental method in chemistry, renowned for exact observation and patience in trial, the very virtue we borrow here: a patient listening to silent things.

The Root & Its Kin · a Skill and a Language

❧  Body before symbol (the first CRT principle). The child meets this root only through the body, never through analysis. He does not parse letters; he feels the breath · the phonetic sensation of n-f-s: the hum of n at the nose, the soft push of f as air brushes the lip, the whispered s like a slow leak of air. The morphology below is for the teacher alone, that the guide may see the household from which the word is born.

Each book in “The Art of Expression” rests upon one root that teaches the skill; then, for the guide, we open a window onto that root's family– the words born of it, to show that language is one interwoven household.

The Full Telling: “Noor Breathes”

For reading aloud with the child (ages 5–6) · seven minutes · with five original plates in the seed palette

I. Noor the seed sleeps in the dark of the soil, breathing with the rain

In a far-off land there was a sleeping seed named Noor. She did not speak, for how should a seed speak while it lies in the darkness of the soil?

Yet she breathed. Whenever morning woke, she drew a deep breath with the rain; and whenever evening fell, she loosed a warm sigh into the earth.

II. The Hasty Wind rushes by shouting, and Noor is flustered

One day a Hasty Wind passed. It ran without pause and cried in every quarter:

“Speak, Noor! Quickly! Why are you silent? Whoever does not speak fast is not heard!”

Noor was flustered. She tried to speak swiftly, like the Wind, and found no word; and the more she hurried, the more her chest drew tight.

III. The wise Jabir and Grandmother Ramla sit in stillness, and breathe together

In that moment, beside the soil, sat the wise Jabir ibn Hayyan and, with him, Grandmother Ramla– for the two of them knew the speech of silent things.

Each laid a palm upon the belly and drew a deep, slow breath, exactly as the seed. Then Grandmother Ramla said, in a voice like a breeze:

“Noor, heed not the Hasty Wind. You are speaking now. Every breath is a word. The rain hears you, and the soil answers.”

Said Noor, still flustered: “But I do not know the words.”

Jabir smiled and said:

“Words are not in the mouth. They are in the belly. Feel your roots. Do you feel the rain entering? That is the first letter. Do you feel the soil growing warm? That is the last. And you, between them, a whole word.”

IV. Noor draws a deep breath without haste, and her chest widens

Noor drew a deep breath, unhurried. A calm inhaling… then a calmer letting-go. And her chest widened. And for the first time she felt that she was, here.

V. Noor opens into a flower and says “I am here,” and the Wind grows still

And she said · not with a tongue, but with a pulse: “I am here.”

This was the first speech the seed learned, before she opened and before she grew. She said it with her body, not her mouth.

As for the Hasty Wind, it paused a moment, and breathed itself… and grew a little still. For it had learned that some speech asks for an unhurried while.

❦  The Silence of the Bodily Question (4–5 seconds). “Lay your hand upon your belly. Draw a deep breath. Did you feel that you are here, that you exist before you say a thing?”

The Brief Telling (for the youngest · 3 minutes)

For a child of four · short sentences, the selfsame meaning.

There was a tiny seed named Noor. She did not speak, but she breathed.

A Hasty Wind came and said, “Quickly! Speak!” So Noor was afraid, and her chest drew tight.

Grandmother Ramla sat beside her and said gently, “Take a breath. Every breath is a word.”

Noor breathed slowly… and her chest widened. And she said, “I am here!” And the Wind grew still.

Sub-Degree & Mastery Criterion (graduated)

A two-stage criterion. A sleeping seed– the child takes a conscious breath with a reminder from the companion. ← An awakening seed– he lays his palm upon his belly and breathes consciously unprompted. Thus the degree names a journey, not a fixed point.

❧  CRT Can-Do (a communicative criterion; below CEFR pre-A1, at ACTFL Novice Low). The child can use a conscious deep breath, hand on the belly, to communicate to another the message “I am present and ready,” in response to a partner's smile, even before uttering the words “I am here.” Thus body-language is placed inside the communicative frame, the theory's fullest proof.

CASEL Behavioral Criteria (Self-Awareness)

  • Names his feeling in a single word after the activity (e.g., calm, at ease).
  • Pauses two seconds before speaking · unprompted.
  • Tells the inhaling from the letting-go bodily (points to his chest rising and falling).
  • Shows a readiness to repeat the activity and asks for it himself.

The Four Inner Senses of the Breath

Beyond the five outward senses, CRT awakens the child's interoception– the quiet inner senses by which the body knows itself. The breath is their first doorway. Guide the child to notice, without naming them as terms:

Inner senseWhat the child feels in the breathA guiding whisper
Weight (heaviness)The body settling, growing heavy and rooted as the breath slows“Feel yourself growing heavy, like a seed sinking into warm soil.”
The in-between (interoception)The soft stir between chest and belly where the breath turns“Where does the breath stop and turn around inside you?”
BalanceThe gentle sway steadying as the breath evens out“Sit like a still tree; let the breath make you steady.”
WarmthThe warm air leaving, the cool air entering“Is the breath you let out warm? Is the breath you take in cool?”
❦  Why this matters. A child who can feel weight, warmth, balance, and the inner turn of the breath owns an inner compass for calm, the bodily ground on which every later skill (voice, gaze, listening) will stand.

Safe-Stop Protocol & Inclusion

❧  Safe-stop protocol. If the child feels dizzy, short of breath, or uneasy, halt the activity at once, return to natural breathing, and reassure with a touch or a calm word. Conscious breathing is an invitation, never a compulsion.
ConditionSuggested alternative
Motor difficultyIt suffices to watch his belly rise and fall, or to use a companion's hand.
Visual impairmentReplace “looking into the eyes” with hearing the companion's breath or a gentle touch of the hand.
Hyperactivity / attentionShorten to a single breath, tied to a game: “blow a feather, slowly.”
Sensory sensitivity (dislikes touch)No touch at all; it is enough to watch a doll's breathing.
Autism spectrum (ASD)Use a Visual Timer showing the breath's span, reducing auditory stimuli and harsh light.
Social anxietyBegin breathing alone in a quiet corner, then move gradually to a single companion, no group at first.

Guide for Parent & Teacher

i. Before the sitting · the screen-box. Lay all devices in a closed box. Do not war upon the screen; offer instead a fairer thing: the presence of a living person.

ii. During the sitting · the shared breath. Do not instruct your child, breathe with him. A child learns not by command but by imitation. Seeing you lay a palm upon your belly and close your eyes, he will follow, unbidden.

iii. After the sitting · the daily breath-moments. Make “the breath-moment” a custom: on waking, before eating, before sleep, on returning from school. One minute is enough.

❦  Make it measurable. Keep, upon the fridge, a small ledger: “How many breath-moments did we take today?”, a little star for each. At week's end, count the stars together. So the habit turns from a vague feeling into a number the child sees, and grows.

“The screen teaches the child to be there. We teach him to be here.”

The Juthur Scale (JPS) · Book One Unit

❧  Important. The Juthur Scale is a pedagogical observation tool under development for tracking a skill's growth · not a psychological or medical diagnosis. Its readings are advisory, aiding the teacher, and pass no verdict upon the child.
❦  How the measure works. The full Scale (JPS) holds five dimensions raised cumulatively across the books: body awareness · emotional regulation · social presence · active listening · bodily expression. Book One teaches but two, so it would be developmentally false to measure dimensions not yet taught. This unit therefore measures only what this book taught (8 items), joining the full scale by degrees.

Filled twice, before beginning, and after three weeks. To lessen bias it is filled by two independent raters (teacher and parent), neither seeing the other's answer. The three marks are behaviorally anchored– choose by what you see, not by impression:

MarkBehavioral anchor (what you actually observe)
○  rarelyAnswers or acts at once, body restless; no self-initiated breath or pause.
◐  sometimesPauses or breathes only when the guide raises the “calm-hand” cue; not yet on his own.
●  oftenUnprompted, lays a hand on his belly and takes a clear breath before he speaks or acts.
❦  Discrepancy-resolution protocol (inter-rater reliability). Compare the two raters item by item. Where they agree, record the mark. Where they differ by one step (e.g., ○ vs ◐), record the lower mark and note it for the next observation. Where they differ by two steps (○ vs ●), do not average: the two raters observe one shared session together, then reconcile against the anchors above and record the agreed mark. Note the count of two-step disagreements, a high count signals the anchors need re-reading, not the child re-testing.

Questions of Understanding

  • What was Noor doing while she lay in the dark of the soil? recall
  • Why did Noor's chest draw tight when she tried to hurry like the Wind? inference
  • Show me with your hand how the seed breathes, and where did you feel the breath in your body? sensory-motor
  • When the Hasty Wind came, did you feel confusion and curiosity at once? simultaneous emotions
  • The Hasty Wind was afraid that no one would hear it. Have you ever felt you must be loud or fast to be seen? What could we say to the Wind to help it feel heard without hurrying? empathy for the other
  • When do you feel that you are truly “here”? personal link
Before I speak, I breathe; for the calm breath says: I am here.

❦  Behavioral translation (not a slogan only). Teach the child the rule in three steps: (1) A stop-signal, say within: “Stop; I am a seed.” (2) Take three calm breaths. (3) Say to yourself: “I can grow calm,” then speak. Remind him: so did Noor when the Hasty Wind flustered her, she stopped, breathed, grew calm, then spoke.

The Twelve Zero-Standards

The covenant of “nothing missing, nothing forced”, twelve zeroes the book must hold.

  • Zero logical error– the tale runs seed → strain → calm → growth, unbroken.
  • Zero moral coercion– conscious breathing is an invitation, never a command.
  • Zero structural contradiction– the two sub-degrees are consistent with the arc.
  • Zero psychological gap– Noor is “flustered,” not “terrified”; the emotion fits the age.
  • Zero disconnected episode– a bidirectional memory bridge binds this book to the whole.
  • Zero passive activity– every skill becomes a sensory anchor of the body.
  • Zero stock icon– the seal and all five plates are original vector art.
  • Zero symbolic error– seed = breath = awakening; the metaphor holds throughout.
  • Zero psychological miscue– the Wind learns; it is understood, not defeated.
  • Zero architectural break– identity → root → tale → application → measure → review is whole.
  • Zero cognitive coercion– “You are speaking now” is discovery, not dictation.
  • Zero cultural imbalance– a male sage and a female elder share the wisdom.
