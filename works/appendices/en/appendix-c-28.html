Stories 1–28, complete

JUTHOOR Roots APPENDIX C Letter Circles — The Complete 28 Stories حَلَقَاتُ الْحُرُوفِ Five Volumes · 28 Characters · 56 Activities · Complete Illustration Brief Dr. Adel F. Amer · CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 · 2026

How to Use This Appendix Each of the 28 story cards in this appendix is a complete teaching instrument. It is not a full narrative text — it is the pedagogical scaffold for a story that the teacher animates in the classroom. Every card contains four elements: CHARACTER & TAGLINE — the story's protagonist and central tension, designed to prime the child's emotional engagement before the phoneme is introduced. ILLUSTRATION BRIEF — precise visual instructions for the illustrator, specifying the pedagogically critical detail on each page. Page 1 establishes context; Page 3 always shows the articulatory moment. MAGIC WORDS — four key vocabulary items to review after the story. These words all contain the target phoneme; at least one is tactilely or kinesthetically evocative. POST-STORY ACTIVITIES — two embodied activities (labelled ① and ②) aligned to specific Naraf stations. Both activities can be completed in 5–8 minutes and require no specialist materials.

🖨️ Tactile Printing Specification Per the author's original specification: all story text is to be printed using a raised-letter technique (embossed or textured ink) so that children can read with their fingertips as well as their eyes. Recommended font size gradient: 40pt on early pages, reducing as the child progresses through the volume. This specification applies to the illustrated publication; the current document is the pedagogical brief.

📋 Translation Committee Note The Arabic source contained complete entries for all 28 letters. This English edition is a full, faithful translation of all 28 story entries. The character names have been adapted to be pronounceable in English while retaining their Arabic phonological anchoring (e.g., 'Buboo' for bā', 'Mumoosh' for mīm). Articulatory descriptions have been expanded to professional speech-language pathology standards for international use.

Volume I — Labial & Early Alveolar Sounds Stories 1–6. These are the sounds children produce earliest — they require the most visible, most touchable articulatory movements. Start here. The mouth is already familiar with these sounds from babbling; the stories give them names.

1 ب Story 1 of 28 — Letter "ب" (bā') Character: Buboo the Bubble "The bubble that almost didn't burst" Phoneme: /b/ · Articulation: Bilabial plosive — both lips press tightly, then release with a burst 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: Buboo: a sad blue bubble with tiny lips inside it, floating above an open soap bottle. Page 3 (close-up): Close-up of Buboo's lips pressed tight, then bursting wide open — sound lines: 'bā!' flying outward. • Bubble • Boom • Two lips • Burst ① 'Buboo Moment': children stand, press lips tight (count to 3), then at 'BURST!' open mouths and jump — shouting 'bā!' ② 'Bubble Hunt': teacher blows real soap bubbles; children shout 'bā!' and pop one bubble each — binding phoneme to real tactile experience.

2 م Story 2 of 28 — Letter "م" (mīm) Character: Mumoosh the Bee "The shy bee who hums with her nose" Phoneme: /m/ · Articulation: Bilabial nasal — lips sealed, sound flows through the nose with vibration 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: A small bee hiding behind a flower, mouth firmly closed, antenna drooping shyly. Page 3 (close-up): The bee with eyes closed, hands on her nose — hum-lines radiating from her nostrils. • Bee • Hum • Nose • Honey ① Children sit, pinch noses gently, hum 'mmmmm' and feel the vibration. Then say 'mīm' and open eyes. ② 'Nose Hum Race': who can hold 'mmm' the longest without opening lips?

3 ت Story 3 of 28 — Letter "ت" (tā') Character: Tamra the Silent Drum "The drum that only beat when the tongue tapped" Phoneme: /t/ · Articulation: Alveolar plosive (voiceless) — tongue tip taps behind upper teeth, air pops out 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: A small drum sitting alone in a barn corner — silent, dusty, waiting. Page 3 (close-up): A crocodile's tongue behind upper teeth, with a quick downward arrow and tiny air burst. • Drum • Tongue • Teeth • Tap ① Children tap index fingers on the table saying 'ta ta ta' with each tap, then say 'tā' in unison. ② 'Tongue Drum': use tongue as drumstick — tap behind top teeth rapidly, then stop on 'tā'.

4 د Story 4 of 28 — Letter "د" (dāl) Character: Dukduk the Rooster "The rooster whose crow woke the sleeping hen" Phoneme: /d/ · Articulation: Alveolar plosive (voiced) — same position as tā' but throat vibrates 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: A sad rooster who cannot crow — the hens sleep on undisturbed. Page 3 (close-up): The rooster's tongue behind upper teeth, throat vibrating — zigzag vibration lines along neck. • Rooster • Hen • Tongue • Vibration ① Children place hands on throats and say 'da da' — feel the vibration. Compare with 'ta ta' which has none. ② 'Voice On / Voice Off': alternate 't' (off) and 'd' (on) — feel throat switch between silent and buzzing.

5 ك Story 5 of 28 — Letter "ك" (kāf) Character: Kaku and the Broken Cake "The cake that only cracked when the throat spoke" Phoneme: /k/ · Articulation: Velar plosive (voiceless) — back of tongue meets soft palate, air released 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: A boy standing before a beautiful intact cake — unable to make a sound from the back of his throat. Page 3 (close-up): Arrow from deepest throat toward the open mouth, labelled 'k' — a tiny burst of air at the exit. • Cake • Throat • Cough • Crack ① Children give a gentle 'kh-kh' cough, then extend to 'kā kā' — tapping feet with each syllable. ② 'Throat Map': children touch back of tongue to roof of mouth (velum) and feel the 'click' of /k/.

6 ط Story 6 of 28 — Letter "ط" (ṭā') Character: Tata and the Flying Leg "The frog who learned to leap when he puffed his cheeks" Phoneme: /tˤ/ · Articulation: Pharyngealised (emphatic) alveolar — tongue tip behind upper teeth, cheeks puffed, velarised 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: A small frog on a lily pad, gazing enviously at a bird soaring overhead. Page 3 (close-up): The frog's tongue behind upper teeth, cheeks puffed wide — a heavy sound cloud labelled 'ṭā' exits. • Bird • Heavy • Puffed • Leap ① Children puff cheeks and press on the backs of their hands to feel the pressure — say 'ṭā ṭā'. ② 'Frog Jump': say 'ṭā' with full cheek puff, then jump forward — land in silence.

Volume II — Dental & Fricative Sounds Stories 7–12. Friction sounds that require precise tongue placement and air-stream control. The voiced/voiceless pairs (s/z, th/dh, f/ẓ) are introduced together so children discover the concept of voicing through their own bodies.

7 س Story 7 of 28 — Letter "س" (sīn) Character: Sasun the Fast Clock "The clock that ticked with a cold breeze" Phoneme: /s/ · Articulation: Alveolar fricative (voiceless) — upper teeth on lower, thin cold air stream 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: A tiny clock ticking at high speed — face flushed, hands blurring. Page 3 (close-up): Upper teeth resting lightly on lower teeth — thin cold air lines streaming between them. • Clock • Air • Teeth • Speed ① Children hold palms before mouths and whistle cold air 'ssss' — feel the coolness on the hand. ② 'Fast-Slow Sīn': say 'ssss' fast (ticking), then slow into 'sā' — mapping tempo to articulatory control.

8 ز Story 8 of 28 — Letter "ز" (zāy) Character: Zazon the Buzzing Wasp "The wasp who taught the children to listen with their necks" Phoneme: /z/ · Articulation: Alveolar fricative (voiced) — same as sīn but throat buzzes 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: A wasp flying in tight circles around a child's ear — the child frozen in surprise. Page 3 (close-up): A finger pressed to the wasp's neck — vibration zigzag lines radiate outward. • Wasp • Buzz • Flower • Hum ① Children press index fingers to throats and buzz 'zzz' — feel the strong vibration. Compare to 'sss' (no buzz). ② 'Voiced or Voiceless': teacher alternates 's' and 'z' — children raise hand when throat buzzes.

9 ث Story 9 of 28 — Letter "ث" (thā') Character: Thamra the Chatty Snake "The snake whose tongue spoke between her teeth" Phoneme: /θ/ · Articulation: Dental fricative (voiceless) — tongue tip between upper and lower teeth, gentle air 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: A small snake sticking its tongue out experimentally — uncertain, curious. Page 3 (close-up): The snake's tongue placed between the teeth — soft air lines drifting past it. • Snake • Tongue • Teeth • Blow ① Children extend tongue tip between teeth and blow gently — 'th th' — raise hands to feel cool air. ② 'Snake Walk': crawl on floor whispering 'thththth' continuously — stop only when reaching a letter card.

10 ذ Story 10 of 28 — Letter "ذ" (dhāl) Character: Dhahaba the Clever Wolf "The wolf cub whose tongue buzzed between her teeth" Phoneme: /ð/ · Articulation: Dental fricative (voiced) — same as thā' but with vocal cord vibration 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: A little wolf cub with a bee tangled in her fur — she does not growl, she hums. Page 3 (close-up): The wolf's tongue between teeth, hand on throat — vibration lines along the neck. • Wolf • Clever • Vibration • Tongue ① Children touch throat, place tongue between teeth, say 'ðhð' — feel the buzz versus 'th' silence. ② 'Pair Up': children in pairs alternately say 'th' and 'ðh' and hold hand to partner's throat.

11 ف Story 11 of 28 — Letter "ف" (fā') Character: Finfin the Gentle Wind "The breeze who lived in a bottle and only left softly" Phoneme: /f/ · Articulation: Labiodental fricative (voiceless) — upper teeth rest on lower lip, gentle airflow 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: A tiny breeze-sprite curled inside a glass bottle, pressing gently against the cork. Page 3 (close-up): Upper teeth resting on lower lip — a delicate air stream flowing over the lip edge. • Breeze • Lip • Teeth • Gentle ① Children rest upper teeth on lower lip and blow gently — 'fi fi fi' — watch a paper strip flutter. ② 'Wind Gauge': hold a paper strip at arm's length — /f/ should flutter it; /b/ should not. Experiment.

12 ظ Story 12 of 28 — Letter "ظ" (ẓā') Character: Ẓilāl and the Clever Shadow "The gazelle who found her shadow by puffing her cheeks" Phoneme: /ðˤ/ · Articulation: Pharyngealised dental fricative — tongue between teeth, cheeks puffed, emphatic voicing 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: A little gazelle standing under a tree with no shade — puzzled and hot. Page 3 (close-up): The gazelle's tongue between puffed-cheek teeth — a heavy voiced sound cloud: 'ẓā'. • Shadow • Elegant • Emphasis • Tongue ① Children puff cheeks, place tongue between teeth, say 'ẓā ẓā' — compare the heaviness to 'ðhā'. ② 'Heavy-Light Pair': alternate 'ðhā' (light) and 'ẓā' (heavy) — children mime carrying a light vs heavy object.

Volume III — Guttural & Pharyngeal Sounds Stories 13–18. The six sounds most distinctive to Arabic — the ones that mark a speaker as a native user of the language. These are also the sounds that heritage speakers and second-language learners find most challenging. The stories use deep-body sensation (pressure, warmth, vibration) rather than visual analogy.

13 أ Story 13 of 28 — Letter "أ" (alif / hamza) Character: Unfūsh the Silent Rose "The rose whose breath paused at the top of the throat" Phoneme: /ʔ/ · Articulation: Glottal stop — vocal cords briefly close and release; the 'catch' in the throat 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: A gentle breeze inside a rose — transparent, silent, held. Page 3 (close-up): A wide-open mouth — tiny closed vocal cord symbols at the throat with a soft release arrow. • Rose • Whisper • Open • Echo ① Children open mouths wide and whisper 'āh' — place hands on throats to feel the micro-stop. ② 'Echo Circle': one child whispers 'ā'; next child echoes louder; continues around the circle.

14 ه Story 14 of 28 — Letter "ه" (hā') Character: The Hoopoe and the Tired Flower "The hoopoe who revived a flower with a warm breath" Phoneme: /h/ · Articulation: Glottal fricative — air passes through open vocal cords; warm, breathed sound 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: A hoopoe bird beside a wilting flower — pausing, head tilted with care. Page 3 (close-up): The hoopoe exhaling warm breath — a gentle wave moving toward the flower, lifting its petals. • Hoopoe • Breath • Warm • Flower ① Children place palms before mouths and say 'hā' — feel the warm exhaled air on their hands. ② 'Warm the Flower': each child holds a drawn flower; say 'hā' and watch if the paper petal moves.

15 ح Story 15 of 28 — Letter "ح" (ḥā') Character: Hamid and the Fire That Wouldn't Light "The boy whose deep breath finally made the fire glow" Phoneme: /ħ/ · Articulation: Pharyngeal fricative (voiceless) — constriction deep in the pharynx; warm, pressured air 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: A boy crouching over kindling — trying again and again to blow a spark to life. Page 3 (close-up): Sound emerging from the deep throat — slight constriction visible, a warm dense air-stream. • Kindling • Throat • Warmth • Pressure ① Children place hands on throats and say 'ḥā' with depth — feel the deep pressure. Compare to 'hā' (lighter). ② 'Deep-Shallow Pair': alternate 'hā' (shallow/glottal) and 'ḥā' (deep/pharyngeal) — children mime depth by moving hand further from mouth.

16 ع Story 16 of 28 — Letter "ع" (ʿayn) Character: ʿUnʿun and the Dry Well "The boy who found water deep in his own throat" Phoneme: /ʕ/ · Articulation: Pharyngeal fricative (voiced) — strong pharyngeal constriction with vibration; the deepest Arabic sound 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: A boy peering into a dry stone well — his reflection far below, distorted. Page 3 (close-up): A deep-throat diagram with tight constriction — strong vibration zigzag lines rising up the neck. • Well • Deep • Throat • Vibration ① Children squeeze throat muscles gently and say 'ʿa ʿa' — feel strong vibration. This is the hardest Arabic sound. ② 'Well Echo': say 'ʿā' softly into cupped hands — the resonance feels 'deep' compared to any other vowel.

17 خ Story 17 of 28 — Letter "خ" (khā') Character: Khaṭṭāf and the Annoying Fly "The boy whose rough throat sound chased the fly away" Phoneme: /x/ · Articulation: Velar fricative (voiceless) — back of tongue near soft palate, rough airflow; like clearing the throat 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: A room with a buzzing fly — the boy frustrated, unable to speak or shoo it. Page 3 (close-up): A rough sound cloud rising from the back of the throat — friction lines at the velum. • Fly • Rough • Throat • Chase ① Children clear their throats gently and say 'khā khā' — feel the friction at the back. ② 'Rough vs Smooth': contrast 'kā' (smooth /k/) with 'khā' (rough /x/) — mime sandpaper vs glass.

18 غ Story 18 of 28 — Letter "غ" (ghayn) Character: Ghālib and the Echoing Cave "The boy whose voice rumbled through the cave walls" Phoneme: /ɣ/ · Articulation: Velar fricative (voiced) — same position as khā' but vocal cords buzz; like a gargled 'g' 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: A boy standing at the entrance of a deep cave — his voice bouncing back strangely. Page 3 (close-up): A deep vibrating sound cloud from the throat — strong zigzag lines at the velum with a voiced label. • Cave • Echo • Vibration • Deep ① Children place hands on throats and say 'ghā ghā' — feel the buzzing. Compare with 'khā' (no buzz). ② 'Cave Resonance': hum 'ghā' toward cupped hands — the rich voiced resonance is distinct from 'khā'.

Volume IV — Tongue-Tip & Blade Sounds Stories 19–24. Six sounds that require precise tongue placement — from the alveolar nasal (n) through the trill (r) to the post-alveolar afficate (j). The rā' story acknowledges that this sound takes the longest to acquire and builds a culture of patient celebration around every attempt.

19 ن Story 19 of 28 — Letter "ن" (nūn) Character: Nunu the Lineage Ant "The ant whose sound escaped through her nose, not her mouth" Phoneme: /n/ · Articulation: Alveolar nasal — tongue behind upper teeth, mouth closed, sound exits through nose 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: A tiny ant deep in a burrow — mouth sealed, antenna vibrating with a hum. Page 3 (close-up): The ant's mouth closed, tongue pressed behind upper teeth — sound waves exiting through the nose. • Ant • Nose • Tongue • Channel ① Children close mouths, press tongues behind upper teeth, and say 'nnn' — feel sound exit through nose. ② 'Pinch Test': pinch nose while saying 'nnn' — sound stops. Open nose — it returns. Nasal resonance explained.

20 ر Story 20 of 28 — Letter "ر" (rā') Character: Raroun and the Trembling Tongue "The patient boy who waited for his tongue to learn to flutter" Phoneme: /r/ · Articulation: Alveolar trill — tongue tip taps rapidly behind upper teeth with multiple vibrations 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: A boy with a sad face — letters in books blur because his tongue will not flutter. Page 3 (close-up): The tongue tip fluttering rapidly behind upper teeth — multiple quick-tap arrows. • Drawing • Flutter • Tongue • Patience ① Children attempt 'rrr' — any flutter counts. Applaud every attempt, even one tap. ② 'One Tap at a Time': start with a single tongue-tap (like 'd') and increase speed gradually — the trill emerges naturally.

21 ل Story 21 of 28 — Letter "ل" (lām) Character: Lu'lu'a and the Lost Pearl "The shell who found her pearl when her tongue touched the roof" Phoneme: /l/ · Articulation: Alveolar lateral — tongue tip touches roof of mouth then releases air around the sides 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: A small shell weeping a pearl — the pearl resting on the ocean floor, unreachable. Page 3 (close-up): The shell's tongue touching the roof of the mouth — side-arrows showing air flowing around the tongue. • Pearl • Touch • Roof • Descent ① Children touch the roof of their mouths with fingertip — then mimic with tongue tip saying 'lā lā lā'. ② 'Roof-Touch Song': tap tongue on palate in rhythm while chanting the letter's chant (Appendix G).

22 ش Story 22 of 28 — Letter "ش" (shīn) Character: Shamshoum and the Shy Sun "The cloud that spread its rough breath across the sky" Phoneme: /ʃ/ · Articulation: Post-alveolar fricative — tongue blade near hard palate, broad airstream, hushing sound 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: A small cloud hiding a sun — golden rays peeking from the edges, trying to break through. Page 3 (close-up): Upper teeth slightly apart, tongue retracted — a wide spread of rough air exiting forward. • Sun • Air • Rough • Spread ① Children hold palms forward and release 'shh' — feel the wide spread of warm air (vs the narrow 'ss'). ② 'Librarian Game': one child is the librarian who shushes — 'shhhh!' — others freeze.

23 ج Story 23 of 28 — Letter "ج" (jīm) Character: Juljulān and the Silent Bell "The bell that rang when the tongue struck the roof" Phoneme: /dʒ/ · Articulation: Post-alveolar affricate — tongue blade strikes hard palate then releases with friction 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: A bell hanging on a school door — beautiful but completely silent, students confused. Page 3 (close-up): The bell's tongue striking the roof of the mouth — impact point marked, then a release arrow. • Bell • Middle • Tongue • Roof ① Children press tongue blade to roof and say 'ja ja ja' — feel the strike-and-release sensation. ② 'Bell Ring': say 'jā' once cleanly — pause — 'jā' again. Each clear strike 'rings' the bell.

24 ي Story 24 of 28 — Letter "ي" (yā') Character: Yannu'u and the Right Hand "The child whose raised hand called out across the field" Phoneme: /j/ · Articulation: Palatal approximant — tongue middle rises toward hard palate without touching it 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: A child raising their right hand high, calling out joyfully across an open field. Page 3 (close-up): The tongue middle rising near the hard palate — a curved arrow showing the glide, no contact. • Hand • Call • Middle • Tongue ① Children raise right hands and extend 'yā yā' — feel the tongue glide without touching the palate. ② 'Glide vs Strike': alternate 'jā' (contact) and 'yā' (no contact) — children mime tapping vs floating.

Volume V — Long Vowels & Orthographic Complexities Stories 25–28. These final four entries address letters that are as much about orthography as phonology: the wāw glide, the dotless yā' (alif maqṣūra), the multi-form hamza, and the mandatory lām-alif ligature. The stories use visual and tactile metaphors to make spelling logic embodied and memorable.

25 و Story 25 of 28 — Letter "و" (wāw) Character: Wawwāf and the Long River "The river who only flowed when the lips made a circle" Phoneme: /w/ · Articulation: Bilabial-velar approximant — rounded lips, back tongue raised; a long glide like /u/ in motion 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: A small river drying up in summer heat — a fish flopping in the last shallow puddle. Page 3 (close-up): Rounded fish-lips with a long sound wave streaming forward — 'wūūūū' flowing like water. • River • Lips • Round • Long ① Children form fish-lips and extend 'wūūū' — imagine swimming slowly through the sound. ② 'River Race': who can hold 'wū' the longest without breaking the lip circle?

26 ى Story 26 of 28 — Letter "ى" (alif maqṣūra) Character: Maqṣūr and the Curved Fish "The fish who swam in a curve and wrote a dotless yā'" Phoneme: /aː/ (word-final) · Articulation: Long vowel /aː/ written as a dotless yā' at the end of certain words; not a consonant 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: A small fish swimming in a graceful curve — its tail scribing the shape of the letter on a rock. Page 3 (close-up): The fish writing 'عَصَا' on the rock — the final letter looking like yā' without dots. • Staff • Guidance • Yā' • Alif ① Children draw a yā' in the air — then erase the dots with a finger — 'that's alif maqṣūra!' ② 'Spot the Curved Alif': children search a simple text for words ending in ى — circle them in a special colour.

27 ء Story 27 of 28 — Letter "ء" (hamza) Character: Hamza and the Many-Seated Chair "The cat who sat on four different chairs — all of them hers" Phoneme: /ʔ/ (on multiple seats) · Articulation: Glottal stop as a phoneme — can sit on alif, wāw, yā' (dotless), or alone; spelling is contextual 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: A clever cat with no chair of her own — eyeing four different chairs around the room. Page 3 (close-up): Four chairs labelled: alif-seat (أ), wāw-seat (ؤ), yā'-seat (ئ), alone (ء) — cat on each. • Chair • Alif • Wāw • Yā' ① Children build a row of four chairs and seat a toy animal on each — naming the hamza form as they go. ② 'Which Chair?': teacher says a word with hamza; children point to the correct chair (form).

28 لا Story 28 of 28 — Letter "لا" (lām-alif) Character: Lālif and the Two Bonded Friends "The two letters who could only be written together" Phoneme: /laː/ · Articulation: Mandatory ligature — lām + alif always joins into لا; also the word 'lā' meaning 'no' 🎨 Illustration Brief ✨ Magic Words 🏃 Post-Story Activities Page 1: Two separate letter-characters (lām and alif) sitting apart on opposite ends of a bench — lonely. Page 3 (close-up): The two characters clasping hands — merging into the single لا form, smiling. • Lām • Alif • Bond • No ① Children hold two wooden pieces — lām block and alif block — then press them together saying 'lā'. ② 'No Game': teacher describes something impossible ('Can fish fly?') — children snap pieces together and say 'lā!' clearly.
