Stories 1 to 28, with three spelling companions

Juthur Roots · Appendix C · Letter Circles

Dr. Adel F. Amer · 2026

How to Use This Appendix

Each card in this appendix is a teaching instrument, not a finished narrative. It is the scaffold for a story that the teacher tells aloud in the classroom. Every card has four parts.

Character and tagline
The protagonist and the central tension, meant to engage the child's feelings before the sound is introduced.
Illustration brief
Precise instructions for the illustrator. Page 1 sets the scene and Page 3 always shows the articulatory moment. Pages 2 and 4 follow the story as the teacher tells it, and the illustrator may shape them.
Magic words
Four key words to review after the story. In the Arabic edition each carries the target sound, and at least one is tactile or physical. They are given here in English so that teachers who do not read Arabic can follow; the three cards added in this revision also give the Arabic word.
Post-story activities
Two embodied activities, marked ① and ②. Each takes five to eight minutes and needs no special materials.

Three Notes for the Teacher

Safety and comfort. Hands rest lightly on the throat; nothing is pressed, squeezed or held until it strains. Puffed cheeks are a playful cue for heaviness, and the real movement is the back of the tongue pulling toward the throat. Stop any activity that causes coughing, dizziness or distress, and let any child watch instead of join.

Varieties of Arabic. Children bring many home dialects into a Gulf classroom: jīm may sound like g or y, qāf like g or a glottal stop, thā' and dhāl like s, z, t or d. Model the standard sound clearly, welcome the child's own, and never mark a home pronunciation wrong.

The two jobs of alif. As the seat of hamza it is taught in Story 16, where the glottal stop is the lesson. As the long vowel /aː/ it sounds in nearly every 'ā' of this appendix, and children meet it as the stretched sound that follows each letter.

Tactile Printing Specification

As the author originally specified, all story text is to be printed with a raised-letter technique (embossed or textured ink) so that children can read with their fingertips as well as their eyes. The recommended type size starts at 40 pt on the early pages and reduces as the child moves through the volume. This applies to the illustrated publication; the present document is the pedagogical brief.

Translation Committee Note

The Arabic cards were rendered into English in full. Character names were adapted to be pronounceable in English while keeping their Arabic phonological anchor (for example 'Buboo' for bā' and 'Mumoosh' for mīm), and the articulatory descriptions were expanded to the standard of speech-language pathology for international use.

Revision of October 2026. The earlier English set held 25 of the 28 letters of the alphabet and filled the last three places with spelling cards. This edition adds the cards for qāf, ḍād and ṣād, so that the numbered stories are the 28 letters, and it moves the three spelling cards (alif maqṣūra, hamza, lām-alif) into a separate group of companions. The place-of-articulation labels now follow Arabic, not English: tā' and dāl, for example, are dental, not alveolar.

Volume I, Stops and the Bilabial Nasal

Stories 1 to 8. These are the sounds children make earliest and see most easily: the lips (bā', mīm), the tongue tip against the teeth (tā', dāl), and then the back of the mouth, first at the soft palate (kāf) and then farther back (qāf). The volume ends with the first heavy pair, ṭā' and ḍād, which add one new idea: the back of the tongue pulls toward the throat. The mouth already knows the first sounds from babbling; the stories give them names.

Story 1 of 28 · ب (bā')

Character: Buboo the Bubble. "The bubble that almost didn't burst"

Sound: /b/. Articulation: Bilabial plosive, both lips press tightly, then release with a burst.

Illustration brief

Page 1: Buboo: a sad blue bubble with tiny lips inside it, floating above an open soap bottle.

Page 3 (close-up): Close-up of Buboo's lips pressed tight, then bursting wide open, sound lines: 'bā!' flying outward.

Magic words
  • Bubble
  • Boom
  • Two lips
  • Burst
Post-story activities

① 'Buboo Moment': children stand, press lips tight (count to 3), then at 'BURST!' open mouths and jump, shouting 'bā!'

② 'Bubble Hunt': teacher blows real soap bubbles; children shout 'bā!' and pop one bubble each, binding phoneme to real tactile experience.

Story 2 of 28 · م (mīm)

Character: Mumoosh the Bee. "The shy bee who hums with her nose"

Sound: /m/. Articulation: Bilabial nasal, lips sealed, sound flows through the nose with vibration.

Illustration brief

Page 1: A small bee hiding behind a flower, mouth firmly closed, antenna drooping shyly.

Page 3 (close-up): The bee with eyes closed, hands on her nose, hum-lines radiating from her nostrils.

Magic words

Post-story activities

② 'Nose Hum Race': who can hold 'mmm' the longest without opening lips?

Story 3 of 28 · ت (tā')

Character: Tamra the Silent Drum. "The drum that only beat when the tongue tapped"

Sound: /t/. Articulation: Dental plosive (voiceless): the tongue tip touches the back of the upper front teeth, then lets a small puff of air pop out.

Illustration brief

Page 1: A small drum sitting alone in a barn corner, silent, dusty, waiting.

Page 3 (close-up): Close-up of a drum skin drawn as the ridge behind the upper teeth, a tongue tip tapping it with a quick downward arrow and a tiny puff of air.

Magic words

  • Tongue
  • Teeth
  • Tap
Post-story activities

① Children tap index fingers on the table saying 'ta ta ta' with each tap, then say 'tā' in unison.

② 'Tongue Drum': use tongue as drumstick, tap behind top teeth rapidly, then stop on 'tā'.

Story 4 of 28 · د (dāl)

Character: Dukduk the Rooster. "The rooster whose crow woke the sleeping hen"

Sound: /d/. Articulation: Dental plosive (voiced): same place as tā', but the throat hums.

Illustration brief

Page 1: A sad rooster who cannot crow; the hens sleep on undisturbed.

Page 3 (close-up): The rooster's tongue behind upper teeth, throat vibrating, zigzag vibration lines along neck.

Magic words
  • Rooster
  • Hen
  • Tongue
  • Vibration
Post-story activities

① Children place hands on throats and say 'da da', feel the vibration. Compare with 'ta ta' which has none.

② 'Voice On / Voice Off': alternate 't' (off) and 'd' (on), feel throat switch between silent and buzzing.

Story 5 of 28 · ك (kāf)

Character: Kaku and the Broken Cake. "The cake that only cracked when the throat spoke"

Sound: /k/. Articulation: Velar plosive (voiceless), back of tongue meets soft palate, air released.

Illustration brief

Page 1: A boy standing before a beautiful intact cake, unable to make a sound from the back of his throat.

Page 3 (close-up): Arrow from deepest throat toward the open mouth, labelled 'k', a tiny burst of air at the exit.

Magic words
  • Cake
  • Throat
  • Cough
  • Crack
Post-story activities

① Children give a gentle 'k-k' cough, then stretch it into 'kā kā', tapping a foot on each syllable.

② 'Back-of-the-Mouth Map': children press the back of the tongue against the soft part of the roof of the mouth, then let go with 'kā' and notice the little pop.

Story 6 of 28 · ق (qāf)

Character: Qunfudh the Hedgehog. "The hedgehog who rolled his tongue up to the very back of his mouth"

Sound: /q/. Articulation: Uvular plosive (voiceless): the back of the tongue rises to touch the uvula, farther back than for kāf, and releases with a deep, dry pop.

Illustration brief

Page 1: A small hedgehog curled into a tight ball beside a lantern, nose tucked in, too shy to uncurl.

Page 3 (close-up): Cross-section of the hedgehog's mouth: the back of the tongue lifted to touch the soft back of the roof, much farther back than for 'k', with a small deep burst labelled 'qā'.

Magic words
  • Hedgehog (قُنْفُذ)
  • Moon (قَمَر)
  • Heart (قَلْب)
  • Pen (قَلَم)
Post-story activities

① Children cup their hands to make a well and say 'qā' as if dropping a pebble to the very bottom, listening for it to land deep. Compare with 'kā', which lands near the top.

② 'Hedgehog Curl': children curl up small on the floor and uncurl on 'qā' with a little pop. The teacher alternates 'kā' (near) and 'qā' (far), and the children say which one they heard.

Story 7 of 28 · ط (ṭā')

Character: Tata and the Great Leap. "The frog who learned to leap when he puffed his cheeks"

Sound: /tˤ/. Articulation: Emphatic (pharyngealised) dental plosive: the tongue tip meets the upper front teeth while the back of the tongue pulls toward the throat, so the sound comes out heavy and dark.

Illustration brief

Page 1: A small frog on a lily pad, gazing enviously at a bird soaring overhead.

Page 3 (close-up): The frog's tongue tip at the upper teeth, the back of the tongue pulled low, and a heavy sound cloud labelled 'ṭā' leaving the mouth. The puffed cheeks are a playful cue for heaviness.

Magic words
  • Bird
  • Heavy
  • Puffed
  • Leap
Post-story activities

① Children puff their cheeks, hold the back of one hand in front of the mouth, and feel the heavy burst on 'ṭā ṭā'.

② 'Frog Jump': say 'ṭā' with full cheek puff, then jump forward, land in silence.

Story 8 of 28 · ض (ḍād)

Character: Ḍayf the Heavy-Footed Guest. "The guest whose heavy steps taught the whole house how to say ḍ"

Sound: /dˤ/. Articulation: Emphatic (pharyngealised) voiced dental plosive: same place as dāl, but the tongue spreads wide against the upper side teeth and its back pulls toward the throat, which darkens the vowel beside it.

Illustration brief

Page 1: A little tent with its door open and a tray of dates and water set out. A big, gentle ox stands at the entrance, lifting one hoof with care.

Page 3 (close-up): Cross-section of the mouth: the tongue spread wide and flat, its sides pressing the upper side teeth, its back pulled low, and a heavy sound cloud labelled 'ḍā'.

Magic words
  • Guest (ضَيْف)
  • Light (ضَوْء)
  • Laugh (ضَحِكَ)
  • Fog (ضَبَاب)
Post-story activities

① 'Heavy Steps': children walk slowly with big, soft stamps and say 'ḍā' on each step, spreading the whole tongue wide and flat. Then they tiptoe lightly with 'dā' and feel the difference.

② 'Light or Heavy?': the teacher alternates 'dā' and 'ḍā'. Children tiptoe for light and stamp for heavy, and listen to how the vowel after the heavy sound grows darker.

Volume II, Fricatives

Stories 9 to 15. Friction sounds that need precise tongue placement and a steady stream of air. Voiced and voiceless pairs are introduced together (s with z, th with dh), so children discover voicing through their own bodies. Ṣād follows its light partner sīn, and ẓā' closes the volume as the heavy partner of dhāl. Fā' stands alone, because Arabic has no voiced partner for it.

Story 9 of 28 · س (sīn)

Character: Sasun the Fast Clock. "The clock that ticked with a cold breeze"

Sound: /s/. Articulation: Alveolar fricative (voiceless), upper teeth on lower, thin cold air stream.

Illustration brief

Page 1: A tiny clock ticking at high speed, face flushed, hands blurring.

Page 3 (close-up): Upper teeth resting lightly on lower teeth, thin cold air lines streaming between them.

Magic words
  • Clock
  • Air
  • Teeth
  • Speed
Post-story activities

① Children hold a palm before the mouth and hiss a cold 'ssss', feeling the coolness on the hand.

② 'Fast-Slow Sīn': say 'ssss' fast (ticking), then slow into 'sā', mapping tempo to articulatory control.

Story 10 of 28 · ز (zāy)

Character: Zazon the Buzzing Wasp. "The wasp who taught the children to listen with their necks"

Sound: /z/. Articulation: Alveolar fricative (voiced), same as sīn but throat buzzes.

Illustration brief

Page 1: A wasp flying in tight circles around a child's ear, the child frozen in surprise.

Page 3 (close-up): A finger pressed to the wasp's neck, vibration zigzag lines radiate outward.

Magic words
  • Wasp
  • Buzz
  • Flower
  • Hum
Post-story activities

① Children press index fingers to throats and buzz 'zzz', feel the strong vibration. Compare to 'sss' (no buzz).

② 'Voiced or Voiceless': teacher alternates 's' and 'z', children raise hand when throat buzzes.

Story 11 of 28 · ص (ṣād)

Character: Ṣaqr the Falcon. "The falcon whose thin whistle grew wide and heavy enough to carry across the desert"

Sound: /sˤ/. Articulation: Emphatic (pharyngealised) voiceless alveolar fricative: same place as sīn, but the back of the tongue pulls toward the throat, making the hiss wide, low and heavy.

Illustration brief

Page 1: A young falcon on a perch, beak open in a thin, high hiss that goes nowhere, while a sparrow chirps in circles around him.

Page 3 (close-up): Cross-section of the mouth: the tongue tip near the upper front teeth as for sīn, the back of the tongue pulled low toward the throat, the lips slightly rounded, and a wide, heavy air stream labelled 'ṣā'.

Magic words
  • Falcon (صَقْر)
  • Morning (صَبَاح)
  • Voice (صَوْت)
  • Rock (صَخْرَة)
Post-story activities

① 'Falcon Glide': children spread their arms and glide slowly around the room, hissing a low, heavy 'ṣṣṣ', then flutter like sparrows with a light, thin 'sss'.

② 'Sparrow or Falcon?': the teacher says 'sā' or 'ṣā'. Children flutter for the light sound and glide for the heavy one, and notice that the mouth feels rounder and larger for the falcon.

Story 12 of 28 · ث (thā')

Character: Thamra the Chatty Snake. "The snake whose tongue spoke between her teeth"

Sound: /θ/. Articulation: Dental fricative (voiceless), tongue tip between upper and lower teeth, gentle air.

Illustration brief

Page 1: A small snake sticking its tongue out experimentally, uncertain, curious.

Page 3 (close-up): The snake's tongue placed between the teeth, soft air lines drifting past it.

Story 13 of 28 · ذ (dhāl)

Character: Dhahaba the Clever Wolf. "The wolf cub whose tongue buzzed between her teeth"

Sound: /ð/. Articulation: Dental fricative (voiced), same as thā' but with vocal cord vibration.

Illustration brief

Page 1: A little wolf cub with a bee tangled in her fur; she does not growl, she hums.

Page 3 (close-up): The wolf's tongue between teeth, hand on throat, vibration lines along the neck.

  • Clever
  • Vibration
  • Tongue

② 'Pair Up': children in pairs take turns saying 'th' and 'dh' while the listener watches the speaker's tongue tip and the speaker feels their own throat.

② 'Heavy-Light Pair': alternate 'dhā' (light) and 'ẓā' (heavy); children mime carrying a light and then a heavy object.

Volume III, Glottal, Pharyngeal and Velar-Uvular Sounds

Stories 16 to 21. Six sounds that English-speaking learners and many heritage speakers find hardest. The stories lean on deep-body sensation (pressure, warmth, vibration) rather than visual analogy. Hands rest lightly on the throat; nothing is ever pressed or squeezed.

Story 16 of 28 · أ (alif / hamza)

Character: Unfūsh the Silent Rose. "The rose whose breath paused at the top of the throat"

Sound: /ʔ/. Articulation: Glottal stop, vocal cords briefly close and release; the 'catch' in the throat.

Illustration brief

Page 1: A gentle breeze inside a rose, transparent, silent, held.

Page 3 (close-up): A wide-open mouth, a tiny pair of closed vocal-cord symbols at the throat, and a soft release arrow.

Magic words
  • Rose
  • Whisper
  • Open
  • Echo
Post-story activities

① Children open mouths wide and whisper 'āh', place hands on throats to feel the micro-stop.

② 'Echo Circle': one child whispers 'ā'; next child echoes louder; continues around the circle.

Story 17 of 28 · ه (hā')

Character: The Hoopoe and the Tired Flower. "The hoopoe who revived a flower with a warm breath"

Sound: /h/. Articulation: Glottal fricative, air passes through open vocal cords; warm, breathed sound.

Illustration brief

Page 1: A hoopoe bird beside a wilting flower, pausing, head tilted with care.

Page 3 (close-up): The hoopoe exhaling warm breath, a gentle wave moving toward the flower, lifting its petals.

Magic words
  • Hoopoe
  • Breath
  • Warm
  • Flower
Post-story activities

① Children place palms before mouths and say 'hā', feel the warm exhaled air on their hands.

② 'Warm the Flower': each child holds a drawn flower; say 'hā' and watch if the paper petal moves.

Story 18 of 28 · ح (ḥā')

Character: Hamid and the Fire That Wouldn't Light. "The boy whose deep breath finally made the fire glow"

Sound: /ħ/. Articulation: Pharyngeal fricative (voiceless), constriction deep in the pharynx; warm, pressured air.

Illustration brief

Page 1: A boy crouching over kindling, trying again and again to blow a spark to life, while an adult's steady hands hold the tinder safely in place.

Page 3 (close-up): Sound emerging from the deep throat, slight constriction visible, a warm dense air-stream.

Magic words
  • Kindling
  • Throat
  • Warmth
  • Pressure
Post-story activities

① Children rest a hand lightly on the throat and say 'ḥā' from deep down, feeling the pressure low in the throat. Compare with the lighter 'hā'.

② 'Deep-Shallow Pair': alternate 'hā' (shallow/glottal) and 'ḥā' (deep/pharyngeal), children mime depth by moving hand further from mouth.

Story 19 of 28 · ع (ʿayn)

Character: ʿUnʿun and the Dry Well. "The boy who found water deep in his own throat"

Sound: /ʕ/. Articulation: Pharyngeal fricative (voiced), strong pharyngeal constriction with vibration; the deepest Arabic sound.

Illustration brief

Page 1: A boy peering into a dry stone well, his reflection far below, distorted.

Page 3 (close-up): A deep-throat diagram with tight constriction, strong vibration zigzag lines rising up the neck.

Magic words
  • Well
  • Deep
  • Throat
  • Vibration
Post-story activities

① Children rest a hand lightly on the throat (never pressing) and say 'ʿa ʿa', as if turning back a small yawn at the last moment, feeling the vibration under the fingertips.

② 'Well Echo': say 'ʿā' softly into cupped hands; the resonance feels deeper than any other vowel. Praise every attempt; this sound is learned slowly, and that is normal.

Illustration brief

Page 1: A room with a buzzing fly, the boy frustrated, unable to speak or shoo it.

  • Throat
  • Chase
Post-story activities

① Children clear their throats gently and say 'khā khā', feel the friction at the back.

② 'Rough vs Smooth': contrast 'kā' (smooth /k/) with 'khā' (rough /x/), mime sandpaper vs glass.

Page 3 (close-up): A deep vibrating sound cloud from the throat, strong zigzag lines at the velum with a voiced label.

Volume IV, Tongue-Tip and Blade Sounds

Stories 22 to 27. Six sounds that need precise tongue placement, from the alveolar nasal (n), through the trill (r) and the lateral (l), to the post-alveolar fricative (sh) and affricate (j), and last the palatal glide (y). The rā' story accepts that this sound takes the longest to acquire, and builds a culture of patient celebration around every attempt.

Story 22 of 28 · ن (nūn)

Character: Nunu the Humming Ant. "The ant whose sound escaped through her nose, not her mouth"

Sound: /n/. Articulation: Alveolar nasal, tongue behind upper teeth, mouth closed, sound exits through nose.

Illustration brief

Page 1: A tiny ant deep in a burrow, mouth sealed, antenna vibrating with a hum.

Page 3 (close-up): The ant's mouth closed, tongue pressed behind upper teeth, sound waves exiting through the nose.

Magic words
  • Ant
  • Nose
  • Tongue
  • Channel
Post-story activities

① Children close mouths, press tongues behind upper teeth, and say 'nnn', feel sound exit through nose.

② 'Pinch Test': pinch nose while saying 'nnn', sound stops. Open nose, it returns. Nasal resonance explained.

Story 23 of 28 · ر (rā')

Character: Raroun and the Trembling Tongue. "The patient boy who waited for his tongue to learn to flutter"

Sound: /r/. Articulation: Alveolar trill, tongue tip taps rapidly behind upper teeth with multiple vibrations.

Illustration brief

Page 1: A boy with a sad face, letters in books blur because his tongue will not flutter.

Page 3 (close-up): The tongue tip fluttering rapidly behind upper teeth, multiple quick-tap arrows.

Magic words
  • Drawing
  • Flutter
  • Tongue
  • Patience
Post-story activities

① Children attempt 'rrr', any flutter counts. Applaud every attempt, even one tap.

② 'One Tap at a Time': start with a single quick tongue tap, like the 'dd' in American 'ladder', and speed it up gradually; the trill often appears by itself. Some children trill late and some never do; one clean tap is also correct Arabic.

Story 24 of 28 · ل (lām)

Character: Lu'lu'a and the Lost Pearl. "The shell who found her pearl when her tongue touched the roof"

Sound: /l/. Articulation: Alveolar lateral, tongue tip touches roof of mouth then releases air around the sides.

Illustration brief

Page 1: A small shell weeping a pearl, the pearl resting on the ocean floor, unreachable.

Page 3 (close-up): The shell's tongue touching the roof of the mouth, side-arrows showing air flowing around the tongue.

Magic words
  • Pearl
  • Touch
  • Roof
  • Descent
Post-story activities

① Children touch the roof of their mouths with fingertip, then mimic with tongue tip saying 'lā lā lā'.

② 'Roof-Touch Song': tap the tongue on the roof of the mouth in rhythm while chanting the letter's chant from the Foundational Texts.

Story 25 of 28 · ش (shīn)

Character: Shamshoum and the Shy Sun. "The shy sun whose hush spread wide enough to chase the cloud away"

Sound: /ʃ/. Articulation: Post-alveolar fricative, tongue blade near hard palate, broad airstream, hushing sound.

Illustration brief

Page 1: A small cloud hiding a sun, golden rays peeking from the edges, trying to break through.

Page 3 (close-up): Teeth close but not touching, the blade of the tongue raised toward the hard palate, and a wide, soft stream of air spreading forward.

  • Air
  • Rough
  • Spread
Post-story activities

① Children hold their palms forward and release a long 'shh', feeling a wide spread of warm air, then compare it with the narrow, cool 'ss'.

② 'Librarian Game': one child is the librarian who shushes, 'shhhh!', others freeze.

Story 26 of 28 · ج (jīm)

Illustration brief

Page 1: A bell hanging on a school door, beautiful but completely silent, students confused.

Page 3 (close-up): The bell's tongue striking the roof of the mouth, impact point marked, then a release arrow.

  • Bell
  • Middle
  • Tongue
  • Roof
Post-story activities

① Children press tongue blade to roof and say 'ja ja ja', feel the strike-and-release sensation.

② 'Bell Ring': say 'jā' once cleanly, pause, 'jā' again. Each clear strike 'rings' the bell.

Story 27 of 28 · ي (yā')

Character: Yannu'u and the Right Hand. "The child whose raised hand called out across the field"

Sound: /j/. Articulation: Palatal approximant, tongue middle rises toward hard palate without touching it.

Illustration brief

Page 1: A child raising their right hand high, calling out joyfully across an open field.

Page 3 (close-up): The tongue middle rising near the hard palate, a curved arrow showing the glide, no contact.

Magic words
  • Hand
  • Call
  • Middle
  • Tongue
Post-story activities

① Children raise right hands and extend 'yā yā', feel the tongue glide without touching the palate.

② 'Glide vs Strike': alternate 'jā' (contact) and 'yā' (no contact), children mime tapping vs floating.

Volume V, The Glide and the Spelling Companions

Story 28 and Companions 1 to 3. Wāw closes the alphabet as the lip-rounded glide. The three companion cards that follow are not letters of the alphabet but spelling puzzles that children meet as soon as they read: the dotless yā' that sounds like alif (alif maqṣūra), the hamza that sits on different seats, and the lām-alif ligature that is always written as one. They use visual and tactile metaphors to make spelling logic physical and memorable.

Story 28 of 28 · و (wāw)

Character: Wawwāf and the Long River. "The river who only flowed when the lips made a circle"

Sound: /w/. Articulation: Bilabial-velar approximant, rounded lips, back tongue raised; a long glide like /u/ in motion.

Illustration brief

Page 1: A small river drying up in summer heat, a fish flopping in the last shallow puddle.

Page 3 (close-up): Rounded fish-lips with a long sound wave streaming forward, 'wūūūū' flowing like water.

Magic words
  • River
  • Lips
  • Round
  • Long
Post-story activities

① Children form fish-lips and extend 'wūūū', imagine swimming slowly through the sound.

② 'River Race': who can hold 'wū' the longest without breaking the lip circle?

Companion 1 of 3 · ى (alif maqṣūra)

Character: Maqṣūr and the Curved Fish. "The fish who swam in a curve and wrote a dotless yā'"

Sound: /aː/ (word-final). Articulation: Long vowel /aː/ written as a dotless yā' at the end of certain words; not a consonant.

Illustration brief

Page 1: A small fish swimming in a graceful curve, its tail scribing the shape of the letter on a rock.

Page 3 (close-up): The fish writing 'فَتَى' on the rock, the final letter looking like a yā' without dots.

Magic words
  • Youth
  • Guidance
  • Came
  • Upon
Post-story activities

① Children draw a yā' in the air, then erase the dots with a finger, 'that's alif maqṣūra!'

② 'Spot the Curved Alif': children search a simple text for words ending in ى and circle them in a special colour.

Companion 2 of 3 · ء (hamza)

Character: Hamza and the Many-Seated Chair. "The cat who sat on four different chairs, all of them hers"

Sound: /ʔ/ (on multiple seats). Articulation: Glottal stop as a phoneme, can sit on alif, wāw, yā' (dotless), or alone; spelling is contextual.

Illustration brief

Page 1: A clever cat with no chair of her own, eyeing four different chairs around the room.

Page 3 (close-up): Four chairs labelled: alif-seat (أ), wāw-seat (ؤ), yā'-seat (ئ), alone (ء), cat on each.

Magic words
  • Chair
  • Alif
  • Wāw
  • Yā'
Post-story activities

① Children build a row of four chairs and seat a toy animal on each, naming the hamza form as they go.

② 'Which Chair?': teacher says a word with hamza; children point to the correct chair (form).

Companion 3 of 3 · لا (lām-alif)

Character: Lālif and the Two Bonded Friends. "The two letters who could only be written together"

Sound: /laː/. Articulation: Mandatory ligature, lām + alif always joins into لا; also the word 'lā' meaning 'no'.

Illustration brief

Page 1: Two separate letter-characters (lām and alif) sitting apart on opposite ends of a bench, lonely.

Page 3 (close-up): The two characters clasping hands, merging into the single لا form, smiling.

Magic words
  • Lām
  • Alif
  • Bond
  • No
Post-story activities

① Children hold two wooden pieces, lām block and alif block, then press them together saying 'lā'.

② 'No Game': teacher describes something impossible ('Can fish fly?'), children snap pieces together and say 'lā!' clearly.
